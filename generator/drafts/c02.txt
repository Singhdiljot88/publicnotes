## west-bengal-2026-bjp-first-government | West Bengal 2026: the BJP forms its first government

The results of the West Bengal assembly election were counted on 4 May 2026. The Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP) won 206 seats and the All India Trinamool Congress (TMC) won 80, out of 293 seats counted; polling in one seat took place later. The result ended 15 years of TMC rule under Mamata Banerjee. Before that, the Left Front had governed the state for 34 years, from 1977 to 2011.

The most symbolic contest was Bhabanipur in Kolkata, the chief minister's own seat. Banerjee lost it to the BJP's Suvendu Adhikari by about 15,000 votes, according to reported counting figures. Adhikari had also defeated her at Nandigram in 2021. He was once a senior TMC leader and a minister in her government from 2016 to 2020, before joining the BJP in 2020.

Adhikari was sworn in on 9 May 2026 as the first BJP chief minister of West Bengal. Commentators, including the Institute of South Asian Studies in Singapore, described the result as a turning point: the BJP had never governed the state, and its rise from a marginal force to a two-thirds majority took about a decade.

The result also shows how quickly a dominant regional party can be displaced once a major leader defects and the main opposition vote consolidates behind one party.

- The BJP won 206 seats to the TMC's 80 in May 2026, ending 15 years of TMC rule.
- Mamata Banerjee lost Bhabanipur to Suvendu Adhikari.
- Adhikari became West Bengal's first BJP chief minister on 9 May 2026.

## tamil-nadu-2026-tvk-vijay | Tamil Nadu 2026: Vijay's TVK breaks the Dravidian duopoly

In the 2026 Tamil Nadu assembly election, actor Vijay's Tamilaga Vettri Kazhagam (TVK), contesting its first election, emerged as the largest party with 108 of 234 seats. The ruling Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam (DMK) won 59, the AIADMK 47 and the Congress 5. The majority mark is 118, so the TVK was ten short. Chief Minister M. K. Stalin lost his own seat, Kolathur, to the TVK's V. S. Babu by 8,795 votes.

Because Vijay won from two constituencies and must give up one, the party's effective strength was 107. He staked a claim with letters of support from 121 MLAs. These came from the Congress (5), the CPI (2), the CPI(M) (2), the Viduthalai Chiruthaigal Katchi (VCK) and the Indian Union Muslim League (IUML), all parties that had contested the election as part of the DMK-led alliance. Vijay was asked to prove his majority on the floor of the House by 13 May.

He was sworn in on 10 May 2026 at the Jawaharlal Nehru Stadium in Chennai, along with nine ministers. Reports described it as the first government in nearly six decades that is not led by either the DMK or the AIADMK, which have alternated in power since 1967.

The episode also illustrates the governor's discretion in a hung assembly: the largest party was invited and given a deadline for a floor test, in line with the principle the Supreme Court set out in the S. R. Bommai case (1994) that majority must be tested on the floor of the House.

- The TVK won 108 of 234 seats in its first election; the DMK fell to 59.
- Vijay formed the government with outside support from former DMK allies and took oath on 10 May 2026.
- It is the first non-DMK, non-AIADMK government in Tamil Nadu since 1967.

## kerala-2026-udf-returns | Kerala 2026: the UDF returns after a decade

Kerala kept its habit of voting out the incumbent in 2026, after breaking it in 2021. The Congress-led United Democratic Front (UDF) defeated the Left Democratic Front (LDF), which had governed for ten years under Pinarayi Vijayan. The LDF was reduced to 35 of the 140 seats. Within the UDF, the Congress won 63 seats and the IUML 22. On the Left, the CPI(M) won 26 and the CPI 8. Reports differ slightly on the UDF's exact total, but all put it at about a hundred seats, a large majority.

The BJP won three seats: Rajeev Chandrasekhar in Nemom, V. Muraleedharan in Kazhakoottam and B. B. Gopakumar in Chathamangalam, with about 11.4% of the vote. This is its best result in the state; its only previous assembly win was Nemom in 2016.

The Congress legislature party chose V. D. Satheesan, the Leader of the Opposition in the outgoing assembly, as its leader on 14 May. He was sworn in as chief minister on 18 May 2026, with 20 ministers taking oath at the same time, so the full cabinet was in place from day one.

The loss also left the Left without a government in any Indian state for the first time in decades, since Kerala was the last state it ruled. This makes the result significant beyond Kerala, for the future of the Left parties in national politics.

- The UDF won about a hundred of 140 seats; the LDF fell to 35.
- V. D. Satheesan was sworn in as chief minister on 18 May 2026 with a full cabinet of 20.
- The BJP won three seats, its best ever in Kerala.
W: Kerala's result ends the Left's last state government and tests the anti-incumbency pattern in Indian state politics.

## assam-2026-bjp-majority-alone | Assam 2026: the BJP wins a majority on its own

In Assam, the National Democratic Alliance (NDA) won a third straight term, taking 102 of the 126 seats. The BJP alone won 82, its first majority on its own in the state, with a vote share of 38.59%. Its allies, the Bodoland People's Front (BPF) and the Asom Gana Parishad (AGP), won 10 seats each. The Congress won 21.

Himanta Biswa Sarma, who became chief minister in 2021, was sworn in for a second consecutive term on 12 May 2026. Prime Minister Narendra Modi and Union Home Minister Amit Shah attended the ceremony, along with other NDA leaders, according to reports.

The return of the BPF as an NDA partner is notable. The BPF dominates the Bodoland Territorial Region, a Sixth Schedule area governed partly by its own territorial council under the 2020 Bodo peace accord. Its alliance with the BJP brought the Bodo vote back into the ruling coalition.

The election was also the first in Assam after the 2023 delimitation of assembly and Lok Sabha seats in the state. That exercise, carried out by the Election Commission on the basis of the 2001 Census, redrew constituency boundaries while keeping the total at 126; it was criticised by opposition parties at the time.

- The NDA won 102 of 126 seats; the BJP alone won 82, its first solo majority in Assam.
- Himanta Biswa Sarma was sworn in for a second term on 12 May 2026.
- Allies BPF and AGP won 10 seats each; the Congress won 21.

## puducherry-2026-rangasamy-fifth-term | Puducherry 2026: Rangasamy's fifth term and how a UT chief minister is appointed

The NDA retained power in the Union Territory of Puducherry in 2026, winning 18 of the 30 elected seats. N. Rangasamy's All India N.R. Congress (AINRC) won 12 and the BJP 4. On the opposition side, the DMK won 5 and the Congress just 1. Rangasamy won both seats he contested.

He was then sworn in for a record fifth term as chief minister. The appointment process differs from a state's, which is why it is worth knowing. In a state, the governor appoints the chief minister under Article 164. In Puducherry, the Government of Union Territories Act, 1963, provides that the chief minister is appointed by the President, and other ministers are appointed by the President on the chief minister's advice. Accordingly, reports said President Droupadi Murmu appointed Rangasamy before the Lieutenant Governor administered the oath.

Puducherry is one of three Union Territories with a legislature, along with Delhi and Jammu and Kashmir. Article 239A allows Parliament to create a legislature and council of ministers for Puducherry. Its assembly can also include up to three members nominated by the Union government, a provision upheld by the Supreme Court in 2018 and often contested in close houses.

- The NDA won 18 of 30 seats; the AINRC won 12 and the BJP 4.
- N. Rangasamy began a record fifth term as chief minister in May 2026.
- A Puducherry chief minister is appointed by the President under the 1963 Act, not by the Lieutenant Governor.

## bihar-samrat-choudhary-first-bjp-cm | Bihar: Nitish Kumar steps down and Samrat Choudhary becomes chief minister

On 5 March 2026, Nitish Kumar, Bihar's longest-serving chief minister, announced that he would move to the Rajya Sabha. He was elected to the Upper House in the March biennial polls and resigned as chief minister on 14 April 2026. The NDA had won the Bihar assembly election in November 2025 and he had been sworn in again after it.

On 15 April 2026, Samrat Choudhary of the BJP was sworn in as chief minister, making him the first BJP chief minister of Bihar. Reports named Brijendra Prasad Yadav and Vijay Kumar Chaudhary, both of the Janata Dal (United), as deputy chief ministers. The arrangement reverses the long-standing pattern in which the BJP was the junior partner in the Bihar NDA and the JD(U) held the top post.

The change happened without an election. Under Article 164, the chief minister is appointed by the governor and holds office during the governor's pleasure, which in practice means as long as he or she has the confidence of the assembly. When a chief minister resigns, the whole council of ministers goes with him or her, and the governor invites the person elected leader by the majority to form a new government.

Taken with West Bengal, it means the BJP had chief ministers in two big eastern states for the first time in 2026.

- Nitish Kumar resigned as Bihar chief minister on 14 April 2026 after his election to the Rajya Sabha.
- Samrat Choudhary became Bihar's first BJP chief minister on 15 April 2026.
- The JD(U) took the deputy chief minister posts in the new arrangement.

## karnataka-shivakumar-becomes-cm | Karnataka: D. K. Shivakumar replaces Siddaramaiah

Karnataka saw a change of chief minister within the ruling Congress in 2026. Siddaramaiah, who had led the government since the Congress won the 2023 assembly election, resigned. On 30 May 2026, the Congress legislature party elected D. K. Shivakumar, the state Congress president and deputy chief minister, as its leader.

Shivakumar was sworn in as chief minister on 3 June 2026. G. Parameshwara, a senior Dalit leader and former home minister, became deputy chief minister. Reports said 13 ministers also took oath at the ceremony.

The change brought to an end a long-running contest between the two leaders. In 2023, after the Congress won, both were contenders for the top post; Siddaramaiah became chief minister and Shivakumar his deputy, and there was frequent speculation about a power-sharing understanding, which party leaders never formally confirmed.

Constitutionally, the process followed the usual pattern. The legislature party chooses its leader, often with the national leadership's say, and the governor appoints that leader as chief minister under Article 164. The episode is a reminder that in India's parliamentary system the people elect legislators, not a chief minister directly, so a ruling party can change its leader between elections without a fresh vote.

- Siddaramaiah resigned and D. K. Shivakumar was elected Congress legislature party leader on 30 May 2026.
- Shivakumar was sworn in on 3 June 2026, with G. Parameshwara as deputy chief minister.
- The legislature party, not the voters directly, chooses the chief minister.

## manipur-presidents-rule-ends | Manipur: President's rule ends after a year

President's rule was imposed in Manipur on 13 February 2025, after the resignation of Chief Minister N. Biren Singh following months of ethnic violence between the Meitei and Kuki-Zo communities that began in May 2023. The state assembly was kept in suspended animation rather than dissolved, which left open the option of a new government being formed without an election.

That happened on 4 February 2026. President Droupadi Murmu signed the order revoking President's rule, and hours later Governor Ajay Bhalla administered the oath to Yumnam Khemchand Singh of the BJP as chief minister at Lok Bhavan in Imphal. Two deputy chief ministers were sworn in with him: Nemcha Kipgen and Losii Dikho. The assembly met for its session the next day.

The constitutional mechanism matters. Under Article 356, the President can take over a state's government if satisfied that it cannot be carried on in accordance with the Constitution. A proclamation must be approved by both Houses of Parliament within two months and lasts six months at a time, up to a maximum of three years. Extension beyond one year normally requires a national emergency or an Election Commission certificate that elections cannot be held, under Article 356(5). Revoking it before the one-year mark avoided that test.

The appointment of deputy chief ministers from different communities was seen as an effort at reconciliation, although the new government still faces the task of restoring trust and resettling displaced people.

- President's rule in Manipur, imposed on 13 February 2025, was revoked on 4 February 2026.
- Yumnam Khemchand Singh was sworn in as chief minister the same day, with two deputy chief ministers.
- Article 356(5) restricts extending President's rule beyond one year.

## nitin-nabin-bjp-president | Nitin Nabin becomes the BJP's youngest national president

On 20 January 2026, Nitin Nabin was declared elected as the national president of the Bharatiya Janata Party, succeeding J. P. Nadda. Born on 23 May 1980, he became the youngest person to lead the party. Reports said he was the only candidate and was elected unopposed.

Nabin is a five-time member of the Bihar assembly. He first won Patna West in a 2006 by-election, and then the Bankipur seat in 2010, 2015, 2020 and 2025. He served as a minister in the Bihar government, holding road construction and urban development, and resigned after being named the party's national working president in December 2025. Nadda had led the party since January 2020; his term was extended beyond its scheduled end because of the 2024 Lok Sabha election.

Prime Minister Narendra Modi described the organisational election as fully democratic. In March 2026, Nabin was also elected to the Rajya Sabha from Bihar.

Party organisation matters for UPSC because the Constitution says little about political parties. They are registered with the Election Commission under Section 29A of the Representation of the People Act, 1951, and the Tenth Schedule (anti-defection law) is one of the few places they are named. Internal party democracy is not legally enforced; the Law Commission's 170th report (1999) and others have recommended regulating it.

- He is the youngest person to hold the post and a five-time MLA from Bihar.
- Parties are registered under Section 29A of the RP Act, 1951; internal democracy is not legally regulated.

## rajya-sabha-polls-march-2026 | The March 2026 Rajya Sabha elections

Elections to 37 Rajya Sabha seats in ten states were scheduled for 16 March 2026, as members' six-year terms ended. Rajya Sabha members are elected indirectly by the elected members of state assemblies, using proportional representation by means of the single transferable vote, under Article 80. About one-third of members retire every two years, which is why these are called biennial elections.

Of the 37 seats, 26 candidates were elected unopposed because the number of candidates equalled the number of seats. Voting took place for the remaining 11 seats in Bihar (5), Odisha (4) and Haryana (2). Reports by 17 March said the NDA had won 21 seats, the INDIA bloc 13 and the Biju Janata Dal 1, with counting in Haryana held up by a complaint.

The Bihar contest drew attention. The NDA was expected to win four of five seats but won all five after three Congress MLAs and one RJD MLA did not vote with their alliance. Those elected included former chief minister Nitish Kumar and BJP president Nitin Nabin. In Odisha, there were allegations of horse-trading around the fourth seat.

Rajya Sabha voting is by open ballot: since a 2003 amendment, party MLAs must show their marked ballot to their party's authorised agent. The Supreme Court upheld this in Kuldip Nayar v. Union of India (2006). Cross-voting does not attract disqualification under the Tenth Schedule, because a Rajya Sabha election is not a vote in the House, though parties may take disciplinary action.

- 37 seats were up in March 2026; 26 were filled unopposed and 11 went to a vote.
- The NDA won all five seats in Bihar after cross-voting by opposition MLAs.
- Rajya Sabha polls use an open ballot for party MLAs, upheld in Kuldip Nayar (2006).
