How the Protests Started After Lahore — and Who Led Them

A note in my own words, started 1 November 2026

After the Lahore resolution and the 26 January 1930 pledge, the question was: what does a pledge do? Gandhi's answer was to pick a law everyone felt, and break it peacefully, in public.

How it started — salt, not a slogan

Gandhi chose the salt tax because salt was used by rich and poor alike, and Britain held a monopoly on making it. He wrote to Viceroy Irwin in advance that he would break the salt law, then:

  • 12 March 1930: Gandhi left Sabarmati Ashram, Ahmedabad, with 78 selected volunteers, on the Dandi March
  • 5 April 1930: after walking about 390 km, they reached Dandi
  • 6 April 1930: Gandhi broke the salt law by making salt from seawater — that act formally launched the Civil Disobedience Movement

From there it spread wherever the sea was within reach, and then beyond salt: no-tax campaigns, boycotts of foreign cloth, and picketing of liquor shops.

Who led it — not one man, but a chain of leaders

  • Mahatma Gandhi — the starter: led the Dandi March, broke the salt law at Dandi, was arrested on 5 May 1930 at Karadi, near Dandi
  • Jawaharlal Nehru — United Provinces: arrested early, in April 1930 in Allahabad, and led the no-revenue / no-rent campaign there
  • Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel — Gujarat: organised ground support and logistics for the march, and led the no-tax / land-revenue campaigns in Bardoli and Kheda
  • C. Rajagopalachari — Tamil Nadu: led the salt satyagraha march to Vedaranyam
  • Khan Abdul Ghaffar Khan — North-West Frontier: mobilised the Khudai Khidmatgars, the “Frontier Gandhi” movement, in non-violent protest at Peshawar
  • Sarojini Naidu, Kasturba Gandhi, and thousands of women — Dharasana and beyond: after Gandhi's arrest, the Dharasana Salt Works raid fell to Sarojini Naidu, Imam Sahib, Manilal and others. On 21 May 1930, volunteers walked forward unarmed, were beaten with steel-tipped lathis, and did not raise a hand to resist — an event reported worldwide by American journalist Webb Miller

That chain is the point: when one leader was arrested, the next took the same walk. The protest did not stop at Dandi — it started there.

Why salt worked as a protest

It was small enough to do by hand, clear enough to understand, and impossible to dismiss as abstract. A handful of salt, made in public, said: we will not obey an unjust law, and we will not use violence to disobey it.
