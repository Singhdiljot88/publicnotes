The 31 October 2026 symbolic public impeachment: what happened, and what it was not

Note 017 · Theme 2: Election Commission & voter rolls · Fact-checked 1 November 2026 · Not published as legal advice

On 31 October 2026, the opposition INDIA bloc held a symbolic "public impeachment" — also called Jan Mahabhiyog — of Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar in New Delhi. The event had been planned for 30 October and was postponed by a day because of rain. It took place at the official residence of Congress president Mallikarjun Kharge, at 10 Rajaji Marg, and was presided over by former Lok Sabha Speaker Meira Kumar, with Saugata Roy and Awadhesh Prasad reported as deputy chairs. Reporting put attendance at more than 250 opposition MPs, along with leaders, activists, students and civil society representatives.

At the gathering, voters spoke about their experiences during SIR. According to reporting of the event, speakers described names deleted from rolls, notices over mapping discrepancies, and cases where a voter was wrongly marked dead, including an account introduced by Mazdoor Kisan Shakti Sangathan founder Nikhil Dey. Kharge moved a resolution, and Rahul Gandhi, Leader of the Opposition in the Lok Sabha, gave the concluding address. The resolution, passed unanimously at the event according to reporting, demanded the CEC's removal and criminal proceedings against him over allegations of electoral manipulation and voter exclusion. Those are political demands and allegations, not findings of a court or of Parliament.

What the event was not matters as much as what it was. It was not a constitutional impeachment, it had no power to remove the CEC, and it did not decide any legal question. The actual removal process is parliamentary and is explained in Note 8. A symbolic hearing can publicise grievances, put testimony on the public record, and increase political pressure, but testimony at such an event does not by itself prove the constitutional grounds of proved misbehaviour or incapacity. No response from the Election Commission specifically to the 31 October event itself was found in the coverage checked for this note; the Commission's general position on SIR is set out in Note 4.

Key takeaways

  • On 31 October 2026, the INDIA bloc held a symbolic public impeachment of CEC Gyanesh Kumar at Kharge's New Delhi residence.
  • Voters gave accounts of deletions, and a resolution demanded removal and criminal proceedings — as political demands, not legal findings.
  • The event had no constitutional power to remove the CEC; only the parliamentary process in Note 8 can do that.

Why it matters for civil studies: Symbolic politics and constitutional procedure can look similar in a headline, but only one changes who holds office — confusing them weakens public understanding of both.

Discussion question: When, if ever, is a symbolic public hearing a useful democratic tool, and when does it risk misleading citizens about what has actually been decided?
