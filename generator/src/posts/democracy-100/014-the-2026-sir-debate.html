The 2026 SIR debate: what the opposition alleged, and what the EC said

Note 014 · Theme 2: Election Commission & voter rolls · Fact-checked 1 November 2026 · Not published as legal advice

By late 2026, SIR had become one of India's most contested electoral issues. The debate is best understood as two different claims about the same exercise, not as a settled finding by either side. Readers should treat disputed numbers and motives as allegations or responses unless a court or an independently verified record has decided them.

Opposition parties, led in this campaign by the INDIA bloc, alleged large-scale wrongful deletion of eligible voters during SIR, manipulation of rolls, and changes to registration procedures without adequate parliamentary oversight. At the symbolic public impeachment event on 31 October 2026, Congress president Mallikarjun Kharge alleged, according to reporting of the event, that SIR had led to the deletion of 13.2 crore voters' names. (Some PTI-derived reports render the figure he cited as 13.3 crore; either way it is an attributed claim, not a verified total.) Other opposition-linked critiques have cited figures around 13–14 crore across phases. These figures are attributed claims, not verified totals of eligible voters wrongly removed, and different reports count draft-stage exclusions, final deletions, and different phases differently. Opposition speakers also alleged that voters were asked to prove citizenship, received notices over mapping discrepancies, or were wrongly marked dead. Those individual accounts were presented at political events; they are allegations and personal testimonies, not court findings.

The Election Commission has rejected the charge that SIR was designed to disenfranchise eligible voters. It has said the exercise aims to identify ineligible and duplicate entries while providing mechanisms for eligible voters to seek inclusion, and that SIR decisions were taken unanimously by the Commission. In its Press Note No. 120 of 26 September 2026, reiterated in an October 2026 post reported in the press, it said the Bihar SIR order of 24 June 2025 had unanimous approval and that the SIR declaration attached to Form 6 had been upheld by the Supreme Court. The Commission also pointed to the Supreme Court judgment of 27 May 2026, reported as upholding the Bihar SIR framework, while later hearings continued on how specific procedures, such as Form 6, were applied.

The honest summary for a student is therefore threefold: the legal authority for SIR was upheld in the Bihar case as reported; the correctness of individual inclusions and exclusions remains disputed; and no single deletion figure in public debate should be quoted without naming who claimed it and what the Commission said in reply.

Key takeaways

  • The opposition alleged wrongful large-scale deletions and procedural changes; the ECI denied a design to disenfranchise voters.
  • A figure of 13.2 crore was alleged at the 31 October 2026 event; it is not presented here as a proven fact.
  • The Supreme Court reportedly upheld the Bihar SIR framework in May 2026, while disputes over application continued.

Why it matters for civil studies: This debate teaches source discipline — distinguishing a legal ruling, an official claim, a political allegation, and a verified individual case.

Discussion question: What evidence, beyond a total deletion figure, would you need before concluding that a roll revision was fair or unfair?
