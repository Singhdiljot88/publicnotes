Governors and the Centre-State relationship in practice

Note 006 · Theme 1: Constitution & institutions · Fact-checked 1 November 2026 · Not published as legal advice

The Governor is the constitutional head of a state, mirroring the President at the Centre, but the office sits at the fault line of Indian federalism. Under Articles 153 to 167, each state has a Governor, appointed by the President — in practice, on the advice of the Union government — and holding office during the President's pleasure. Article 154 vests the state's executive power in the Governor, while Article 163 provides for a Council of Ministers headed by the Chief Minister to aid and advise the Governor, except where the Constitution requires the Governor to act in discretion.

Normally, the Governor acts on ministerial advice. Discretion matters in specific situations: inviting a leader to form a government when no party has a clear majority, testing whether a government retains confidence, and reporting on a breakdown of constitutional machinery under Article 356. Under Article 200, a Governor may assent to a bill, withhold assent, return it for reconsideration (except a money bill), or reserve it for the President's consideration.

State governments and opposition parties have at times alleged that Governors delay assent to bills, reserve bills unnecessarily, or act in ways that favour the Union government. Courts have repeatedly set boundaries: in S.R. Bommai (1994), the Supreme Court held that a government's majority must be tested on the floor of the House, not on the Governor's subjective satisfaction, and that President's Rule is subject to judicial review.

Key takeaways

  • Governors are constitutional heads, normally acting on the advice of the state Council of Ministers.
  • Discretion is real but limited, mainly in government formation, confidence, and Article 356 reports.
  • Centre-State tension often centres on assent to bills, timing, and the use of discretion.

Why it matters for civil studies: The Governor's office shows how federalism works not only in constitutional text, but in conventions, timing, and judicial review.

Discussion question: How can the Governor's role as a link between Centre and state be preserved without turning the office into a partisan one?
