How to become a civil servant: UPSC and public service as a democratic career

Note 098 · Theme 10: Youth & future · Fact-checked 1 November 2026 · Not published as legal advice

For many young Indians, the civil services are a way to work inside the state that makes and implements policy, from a district office to a central ministry. The main route is the Civil Services Examination conducted by the Union Public Service Commission, a constitutionally established body under Articles 315 to 323. It recruits for the Indian Administrative Service, the Indian Police Service, the Indian Foreign Service, and other central services. State public service commissions run separate examinations for state services, and there are specialist routes in engineering, medicine, teaching, banking, and the armed forces, but UPSC remains the best-known national gateway.

The examination has three stages, each to be cleared in order. The Preliminary examination is an objective screening test with two papers: General Studies and the Civil Services Aptitude Test, which is qualifying. The Main examination is descriptive, with nine papers in total, of which seven count towards merit, plus qualifying language papers. The final stage is the Personality Test, or interview. Final merit is based on the Main examination, worth 1,750 marks, plus the interview, worth 275 marks, a total of 2,025. Preliminary marks do not count towards final ranking. After selection, IAS officers train at the Lal Bahadur Shastri National Academy of Administration in Mussoorie.

Eligibility is broad in one sense and narrow in another. A candidate generally needs a bachelor's degree from a recognised university, and final-year students may apply for the Preliminary examination subject to producing proof of passing before the Main examination. For the general category, the age range is commonly stated as 21 to 32 years as of 1 August in the examination year, with relaxations and attempt limits that vary by category. Young readers should always check the current year's official notification, because age limits, attempt counts, and syllabus details can change, and coaching summaries can go out of date.

Public service also raises democratic questions. Civil servants are expected to serve the government of the day while upholding the Constitution, the law, and the public interest, even under political pressure. Supporters of a permanent civil service argue it provides continuity, expertise, and impartial implementation. Critics argue it can become distant or rule-bound. Both views point to the same skills for aspirants: knowledge of history, polity, economy, and current affairs, plus judgement and integrity.

Key takeaways

  • UPSC's examination has three stages: Prelims, Mains, and Personality Test, with final merit from Mains plus interview.
  • General eligibility is a bachelor's degree and age 21 to 32, with category relaxations and attempt limits; check the current notification.
  • Civil servants must balance loyalty to the elected government with loyalty to the Constitution and the public interest.

Why it matters for civil studies: The civil service is where democratic decisions become services, so its recruitment, training, and ethics shape everyday democracy.

Discussion question: Should a civil servant follow a minister's instruction they believe is unwise but lawful, and where should they draw the line?
