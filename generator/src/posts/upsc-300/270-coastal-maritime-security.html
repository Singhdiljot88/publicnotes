Coastal and maritime security

Note 270 · Theme 27: Internal security · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

India's coastline of about 7,500 km, with nine coastal states, four coastal union territories and over 1,300 islands, is both an economic asset and a security challenge. The 1993 Mumbai blasts used explosives landed on the Raigad coast, and in November 2008, ten Lashkar-e-Taiba terrorists travelled from Karachi by sea, hijacked an Indian fishing boat and landed in Mumbai, where their attacks killed 166 people. The attacks exposed serious gaps in coastal surveillance and coordination.

India then overhauled coastal security. It adopted a three-tier structure: the Indian Navy is responsible for overall maritime security, including coastal and offshore security; the Indian Coast Guard, set up in 1978, guards the territorial waters and the exclusive economic zone; and state marine police patrol shallow waters close to the coast, supported by coastal police stations built under the Coastal Security Scheme. The navy's Sagar Prahari Bal provides fast patrol boats. A Coastal Surveillance Network of radars along the coast and on islands, the National Command, Control, Communication and Intelligence Network, and the Information Management and Analysis Centre in Gurugram link agencies. Fishing boats now need registration and transponders, and fishermen carry biometric identity cards so that boats can be tracked and identified.

Other maritime threats include piracy, as off Somalia, where the Indian Navy has deployed ships in the Gulf of Aden since 2008 and rescued ships and crews; attacks on shipping in the Red Sea since 2023; smuggling of drugs, gold and weapons by sea, with large drug seizures off Gujarat; illegal fishing; and the presence of foreign research and naval vessels in the Indian Ocean.

Gaps remain in manpower and training for state marine police, maintenance of boats, coordination between agencies, and monitoring of small vessels. Experts have long recommended a central maritime security coordinator, and the government appointed India's first National Maritime Security Coordinator in 2022.

Key takeaways

  • The 2008 Mumbai attacks, launched by sea, led to an overhaul of coastal security.
  • The navy, Coast Guard and state marine police form a three-tier system linked by a coastal radar network.
  • Piracy, Red Sea attacks, drug smuggling and illegal fishing are continuing maritime threats.

Why it matters for UPSC: Coastal security is a frequent GS III question, especially in the context of 26/11 and the Indian Ocean.

Practice question: How has India's coastal security improved since 2008? What gaps still need attention?
