Cyber security and cyber crime

Note 268 · Theme 27: Internal security · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

As India digitises government services, payments and infrastructure, it faces growing cyber threats. These include attacks on critical infrastructure such as power grids, ports and hospitals; data breaches; ransomware, which locks data until a ransom is paid; espionage by state-backed groups; and a rapid rise in financial fraud. The ransomware attack on AIIMS New Delhi in November 2022, which disrupted services for days, and the suspected cyber intrusion linked to the Mumbai power outage of October 2020 showed the risks.

Cyber crime against individuals has exploded. Common forms include phishing, UPI and card fraud, fake investment and job schemes, sextortion, and "digital arrest" scams, in which fraudsters pose as police or investigators on video calls and extort money. Many scams are run from compounds in South-East Asia, sometimes staffed by trafficked workers, including Indians. The government has blocked lakhs of SIM cards and devices linked to fraud.

India's institutional framework includes the Indian Computer Emergency Response Team (CERT-In), set up in 2004 as the national agency for responding to cyber incidents; since 2022, organisations must report incidents to it within six hours. The National Critical Information Infrastructure Protection Centre (NCIIPC) protects critical sectors. The Indian Cyber Crime Coordination Centre (I4C), launched in 2020 under the Home Ministry, runs the National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal and the 1930 helpline. The Defence Cyber Agency handles military cyber operations. The legal base is the Information Technology Act, 2000, along with the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023, and the Telecommunications Act, 2023.

Gaps include the absence of an updated national cyber security strategy since the 2013 policy, a shortage of skilled professionals, dependence on imported hardware, weak cyber hygiene in government offices and small businesses, and difficulties in cross-border investigation. India has not joined the Budapest Convention on cybercrime, but participated in negotiating the UN Convention against Cybercrime adopted in 2024.

Key takeaways

  • Threats range from attacks on critical infrastructure to mass financial fraud and "digital arrest" scams.
  • CERT-In, NCIIPC and I4C (with the 1930 helpline) are key institutions.
  • India lacks an updated cyber security strategy since 2013 and faces skills and hardware gaps.

Why it matters for UPSC: Cyber security is a named GS III topic and increasingly important as digital public infrastructure grows.

Practice question: What steps should India take to protect its critical information infrastructure from cyber attacks?
