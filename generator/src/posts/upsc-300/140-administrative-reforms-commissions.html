Administrative reforms commissions and good governance

Note 140 · Theme 14: Governance & public policy · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

Good governance is often defined through features identified by the United Nations: participation, rule of law, transparency, responsiveness, consensus orientation, equity and inclusiveness, effectiveness and efficiency, and accountability. India has set up two major commissions to reform its administration in pursuit of these ideals.

The First Administrative Reforms Commission (1966–70), chaired first by Morarji Desai and later by K. Hanumanthaiah, made around 20 reports. Its best-known recommendation was the creation of the Lokpal and Lokayuktas to deal with complaints against ministers and officials.

The Second Administrative Reforms Commission (2005–09), chaired by Veerappa Moily, submitted fifteen reports covering a wide range of topics. These included the right to information, unlocking human capital, crisis management, ethics in governance, public order, local governance, conflict resolution, e-governance, social capital, refurbishing personnel administration, citizen-centric administration, and the organisational structure of the government. Among its recommendations were a code of ethics for ministers and civil servants, a Lokpal with constitutional status, state funding of elections, fixed tenures for senior officials, performance-based appraisal, a new law to replace the Police Act of 1861, empowering local governments with real functions and funds, and making citizens' charters effective.

Some recommendations have been implemented, partly or fully, such as the Lokpal law, e-governance initiatives and changes in performance appraisal. Many others remain on paper. The reports remain a rich source of ideas for UPSC answers, especially in GS II governance and GS IV ethics.

The concept of "minimum government, maximum governance" has been used by the present government to describe its approach, emphasising simplification of procedures, digitisation and the repeal of obsolete laws; more than 1,500 old laws have been repealed since 2014.

Key takeaways

  • Good governance includes participation, rule of law, transparency, accountability and equity.
  • The First ARC (1966–70) recommended the Lokpal; the Second ARC (2005–09) produced fifteen reports.
  • Many Second ARC recommendations, such as fixed tenures and police law reform, remain unimplemented.

Why it matters for UPSC: Second ARC reports are among the most quoted sources in GS II and GS IV answers.
