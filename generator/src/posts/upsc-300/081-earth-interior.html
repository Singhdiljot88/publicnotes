The Earth's interior

Note 081 · Theme 9: Physical geography · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

No one has drilled through the Earth's crust; the deepest borehole, the Kola Superdeep Borehole in Russia, reached about 12 km, a tiny fraction of the Earth's radius of about 6,371 km. Our knowledge of the interior comes mainly from indirect evidence: seismic waves from earthquakes, volcanic material from depth, meteorites, gravity and magnetic measurements.

Seismic waves are the key tool. Primary (P) waves are compressional and travel through solids, liquids and gases. Secondary (S) waves are shear waves that cannot pass through liquids. When an earthquake happens, S waves are not detected in a large "shadow zone" on the opposite side of the Earth, which tells scientists that the outer core is liquid.

The Earth has three main layers. The crust is thin: about 5 km under the oceans (oceanic crust, mostly basalt) and 30–70 km under continents (continental crust, mostly granite-like rocks). Its boundary with the mantle is the Mohorovičić discontinuity, or Moho. The mantle extends to a depth of about 2,900 km and makes up most of the Earth's volume. Its upper part includes the asthenosphere, a weak, partly molten zone on which the rigid lithosphere (crust plus uppermost mantle) moves; it is the main source of magma. The boundary between mantle and core is the Gutenberg discontinuity. The core is made largely of iron and nickel (sometimes called "nife"). The outer core is liquid, and its movement generates the Earth's magnetic field; the inner core is solid because of immense pressure, despite temperatures comparable to the Sun's surface.

Understanding the interior helps explain plate movements, earthquakes, volcanoes and the magnetic field that protects life from solar radiation.

Key takeaways

  • Seismic waves are the main evidence for the Earth's interior; S waves cannot cross the liquid outer core.
  • The Moho separates crust and mantle; the Gutenberg discontinuity separates mantle and core.
  • The liquid outer core generates the magnetic field; the inner core is solid.
