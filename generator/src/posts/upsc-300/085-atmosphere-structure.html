The atmosphere: composition and layers

Note 085 · Theme 9: Physical geography · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

The atmosphere is a layer of gases held by gravity. Dry air is made up of about 78% nitrogen, 21% oxygen and 0.93% argon, with carbon dioxide at about 0.04% and traces of other gases. Water vapour varies from almost nothing to about 4% and is crucial for weather. Carbon dioxide and water vapour absorb heat radiated by the Earth, keeping the planet warm; this is the natural greenhouse effect. Ozone, concentrated in the stratosphere, absorbs harmful ultraviolet radiation.

The atmosphere is divided into layers by how temperature changes with height. The troposphere is the lowest layer, extending to about 8 km at the poles and about 18 km at the equator. Almost all weather happens here, and temperature falls with height at an average rate of about 6.5°C per kilometre. Its upper boundary is the tropopause.

The stratosphere extends to about 50 km. Temperature rises with height here because ozone absorbs ultraviolet radiation. The air is calm and dry, which is why long-distance jet aircraft often fly in its lower part. The ozone layer lies mainly between about 15 and 35 km. The Montreal Protocol of 1987, which phased out ozone-depleting substances such as chlorofluorocarbons, is considered the most successful environmental treaty, and the ozone layer is recovering.

The mesosphere, up to about 80–85 km, is the coldest layer; meteors burn up here. The thermosphere above it contains the ionosphere, where ionised gases reflect radio waves back to Earth and where auroras occur. The International Space Station orbits in the thermosphere. The exosphere is the outermost layer, gradually merging with space.

Key takeaways

  • Air is about 78% nitrogen and 21% oxygen; CO2 is about 0.04%.
  • The troposphere holds the weather; the stratosphere holds the ozone layer.
  • The ionosphere, in the thermosphere, reflects radio waves.

Why it matters for UPSC: Atmospheric layers and composition are among the most frequently tested Prelims geography topics.

Practice question: Why does temperature decrease with height in the troposphere but increase in the stratosphere?
