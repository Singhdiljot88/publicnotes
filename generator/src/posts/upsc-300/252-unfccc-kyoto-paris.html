The UNFCCC, the Kyoto Protocol and the Paris Agreement

Note 252 · Theme 26: Climate change & disaster management · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

The United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change (UNFCCC) was adopted at the Rio Earth Summit in 1992 and came into force in 1994. Its goal is to stabilise greenhouse gas concentrations at a level that would prevent dangerous human interference with the climate. It established the principle of "common but differentiated responsibilities and respective capabilities" (CBDR-RC): all countries must act, but developed countries, which caused most historical emissions, should take the lead and support developing countries. The parties meet each year at a Conference of the Parties (COP).

The Kyoto Protocol, adopted in 1997 and in force from 2005, set binding emission reduction targets only for developed countries (listed in Annex I), averaging about 5% below 1990 levels in 2008–2012. It created market mechanisms, including the Clean Development Mechanism (CDM), through which developed countries could earn credits by funding emission-cutting projects in developing countries; India hosted many CDM projects. The United States never ratified Kyoto, and Canada withdrew, which weakened it.

The Paris Agreement, adopted at COP21 in December 2015 and in force from November 2016, changed the approach. All countries set their own targets, called Nationally Determined Contributions (NDCs), to be updated every five years with increasing ambition. The agreement aims to keep warming "well below 2°C" and pursue efforts to limit it to 1.5°C. It includes a global stocktake every five years to assess collective progress (the first concluded at COP28 in 2023), an enhanced transparency framework for reporting, Article 6 on carbon markets, and commitments on adaptation and finance. Developed countries had pledged in 2009 to mobilise 100 billion US dollars a year in climate finance for developing countries by 2020, a target reported as met only in 2022.

The United States withdrew from the Paris Agreement under President Trump, rejoined in 2021, and announced a second withdrawal in January 2025.

Key takeaways

  • The UNFCCC (1992) set the principle of common but differentiated responsibilities.
  • The Kyoto Protocol (1997) bound only developed countries; the CDM channelled projects to developing countries.
  • The Paris Agreement (2015) relies on nationally determined contributions and aims for well below 2°C, ideally 1.5°C.

Why it matters for UPSC: The evolution of climate treaties is a high-frequency Prelims and Mains topic.

Practice question: How did the Paris Agreement change the approach of the Kyoto Protocol? Is the change an improvement?
