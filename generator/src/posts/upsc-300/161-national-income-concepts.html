National income: GDP, GNP, GVA and NNP

Note 161 · Theme 17: Growth, development & inflation · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

National income accounting measures the value of goods and services an economy produces. The most widely used measure is Gross Domestic Product (GDP): the market value of all final goods and services produced within a country's borders in a year. "Final" matters. Counting intermediate goods, such as the steel that goes into a car, would count the same output twice. GDP can be measured in three ways that should give the same answer: the production method (adding up the value added at each stage), the income method (adding wages, rent, interest and profits) and the expenditure method (adding consumption, investment, government spending and net exports).

Gross National Product (GNP), now usually called Gross National Income, adds the income residents earn abroad and subtracts the income foreigners earn in the country. For India, GNP is slightly lower than GDP, because foreign investors earn more in India than Indians earn abroad from investment. Net measures subtract depreciation, the wear and tear on capital: Net National Product (NNP) is GNP minus depreciation. NNP at factor cost is what economists traditionally call national income.

India now reports Gross Value Added (GVA) at basic prices by sector. GVA is output minus intermediate consumption. GDP equals GVA plus product taxes minus product subsidies. Basic prices include production taxes, such as land revenue, but exclude product taxes such as GST.

Nominal GDP is measured at current prices. Real GDP adjusts for inflation by valuing output at the prices of a base year, so it shows how much more is actually produced. The ratio of nominal to real GDP is the GDP deflator, a broad measure of inflation. Per capita income divides national income by population and is a rough guide to average living standards, though it says nothing about distribution.

Key takeaways

  • GDP counts final goods and services produced within a country; it can be measured by production, income or expenditure.
  • GNP adds net factor income from abroad; NNP subtracts depreciation.
  • Real GDP removes inflation using base-year prices; the GDP deflator is nominal over real GDP.

Why it matters for UPSC: National income concepts are tested almost every year in Prelims and are the foundation for all economy answers.
