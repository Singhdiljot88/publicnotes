Urbanisation in India

Note 075 · Theme 8: Indian society · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

According to the 2011 Census, 31.1% of Indians lived in urban areas, up from about 17% in 1951. The Census defines urban areas as statutory towns (with a municipality or similar body) and census towns, which meet three criteria: a population of at least 5,000, a density of at least 400 people per square km, and at least 75% of the male working population in non-agricultural work. Many places that function as towns are still governed as villages, so the real level of urbanisation is probably higher. The World Bank and others estimate that India's urban population will reach around 40% by the mid-2030s.

Urban growth has three sources: natural increase in cities, migration from rural areas, and the reclassification of villages as towns. Most growth is concentrated in large cities and their peripheries. India had 53 cities with a population over a million in 2011.

Cities are engines of growth, producing a large share of GDP, but they face severe problems: housing shortages and slums (about 65 million people lived in slums in 2011), inadequate water and sanitation, traffic congestion, air pollution, flooding from encroached drainage, and weak municipal finances. The 74th Amendment (1992) gave constitutional status to urban local bodies, but most remain dependent on state governments for funds and functions.

Government programmes include the Smart Cities Mission and AMRUT (both launched in 2015), the Pradhan Mantri Awas Yojana-Urban for housing, the Swachh Bharat Mission-Urban and urban metro rail projects. Experts argue that empowering city governments, with elected mayors and their own revenue, is as important as investment.

Key takeaways

  • 31.1% of Indians lived in urban areas in 2011; the true figure is likely higher.
  • Census towns need 5,000 people, 400 per square km and 75% male non-farm work.
  • Slums, water, pollution, flooding and weak municipal finances are the main urban challenges.

Why it matters for UPSC: Urbanisation spans GS I society and geography and GS II local governance questions.
