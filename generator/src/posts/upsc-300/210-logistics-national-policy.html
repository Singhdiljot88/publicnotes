Logistics and the National Logistics Policy

Note 210 · Theme 21: Industry, infrastructure & energy · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

Logistics, the movement and storage of goods, determines how competitive a country's industry and agriculture are. High logistics costs make Indian exports more expensive and add to prices at home. For years, India's logistics costs were commonly cited at 13–14% of GDP, though that figure had no firm basis. A study by NCAER for the commerce ministry's Department for Promotion of Industry and Internal Trade, released in September 2025, estimated India's logistics costs at about 7.97% of GDP in 2023-24, much lower than the old estimates but still with room for improvement.

India's freight is heavily skewed towards roads, which are more expensive per tonne-kilometre than rail or water for bulk goods over long distances. Problems include fragmented warehousing, poor last-mile connectivity, delays at ports and borders, multiple documents and checks, and a large unorganised trucking sector.

The National Logistics Policy, launched on 17 September 2022, aims to reduce costs, improve India's ranking in the World Bank's Logistics Performance Index (India ranked 38th in 2023) and create a seamless system. It works alongside PM Gati Shakti. Its tools include the Unified Logistics Interface Platform (ULIP), which integrates data from different ministries so companies can track cargo; the Logistics Ease Across Different States (LEADS) index, which ranks states; standards for warehousing; and the e-logs dashboard for industry to raise problems.

Other reforms have helped. GST removed most state border checkposts from 2017, cutting truck waiting times. E-way bills track goods movement electronically. Dedicated Freight Corridors, multimodal logistics parks, inland waterways under the National Waterways Act, 2016 and the Sagarmala port programme aim to shift cargo to cheaper modes.

Key takeaways

  • An NCAER-DPIIT study put India's logistics costs at about 7.97% of GDP in 2023-24.
  • Freight is skewed towards costlier road transport.
  • The National Logistics Policy (2022), ULIP and LEADS work with Gati Shakti to cut costs.

Why it matters for UPSC: Logistics efficiency links infrastructure, trade and manufacturing, and is a frequent GS III topic.

Practice question: How can India shift more freight from road to rail and waterways? What would be the benefits?
