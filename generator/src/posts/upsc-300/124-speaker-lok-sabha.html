The Speaker of the Lok Sabha

Note 124 · Theme 13: Legislatures & executives in detail · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

The Speaker presides over the Lok Sabha and is its principal spokesperson and guardian of its rights. The Speaker is elected by the members of the house from among themselves, usually at its first sitting, and holds office until just before the first meeting of the next Lok Sabha; the Speaker does not vacate office when the house is dissolved. The Speaker can be removed by a resolution passed by a majority of all the then members of the house, with 14 days' notice. During the removal proceedings, the Speaker cannot preside but can speak and vote, though not in case of a tie.

The Speaker's powers are wide. The Speaker maintains order, interprets the rules of procedure, decides on points of order, and can adjourn or suspend the house. The Speaker certifies money bills, and this decision is final. The Speaker presides over joint sittings of the two houses, decides on the disqualification of members under the anti-defection law (subject to judicial review since the Kihoto Hollohan judgment of 1992), and appoints the chairpersons of committees. The Speaker votes only to break a tie (a casting vote).

The office is meant to be impartial. In Britain, the Speaker leaves their party on election and is traditionally re-elected unopposed; India has not adopted this convention, and the Speaker usually comes from the ruling party. Critics point to delays in deciding defection petitions and the frequent suspension of opposition members as signs of partisanship. The Supreme Court has suggested that Parliament consider giving defection cases to an independent tribunal.

The Deputy Speaker presides when the Speaker is absent. The post remained vacant throughout the 17th Lok Sabha (2019–2024), despite the constitutional requirement in Article 93 that the house "shall" choose a Deputy Speaker.

Key takeaways

  • The Speaker is elected by the Lok Sabha and removed by a majority of all members.
  • The Speaker certifies money bills and decides anti-defection cases, subject to judicial review.
  • The Deputy Speaker's post stayed vacant through the entire 17th Lok Sabha.

Why it matters for UPSC: The Speaker's role and impartiality are frequent Mains questions, and the office's powers appear in Prelims.
