India-United States relations

Note 157 · Theme 16: International relations & organisations · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

India and the United States, the world's largest and oldest democracies, were often described as "estranged democracies" during the Cold War, when India was non-aligned and the US was allied with Pakistan. Relations improved after the Cold War and especially after the India-US Civil Nuclear Agreement of 2008, which ended India's nuclear isolation without requiring it to sign the NPT.

Defence ties have grown steadily. The US designated India a "Major Defence Partner" in 2016, and the two signed four foundational agreements: GSOMIA (2002) on military information security, LEMOA (2016) on logistics, COMCASA (2018) on secure communications, and BECA (2020) on geospatial intelligence. India buys American aircraft such as the C-17, P-8I and Apache, and the two hold the Malabar naval exercise with Japan and Australia. The Initiative on Critical and Emerging Technology (iCET), launched in 2023 and later renamed TRUST, covers semiconductors, AI, space and defence production. In October 2025 the two signed a new ten-year defence framework.

The US is India's largest trading partner for goods, and the Indian diaspora of about five million in the US is among its most successful communities. Both countries are members of the Quad.

The relationship also has friction. Trade disputes are long-standing. In 2025, the US imposed tariffs of up to 50% on many Indian goods, including a penalty linked to India's purchases of Russian oil, before a trade arrangement announced in February 2026 reduced them. Differences persist over India's ties with Russia, immigration and H-1B visas, which most often go to Indian professionals, and the treatment of Indian migrants. India's policy of strategic autonomy means it partners with the US without becoming an ally.

Key takeaways

  • The 2008 civil nuclear deal transformed India-US relations.
  • Four foundational defence agreements (GSOMIA, LEMOA, COMCASA, BECA) underpin military cooperation.
  • Tariffs, Russian oil, visas and migration have caused friction, especially since 2025.

Why it matters for UPSC: India-US relations are among the most important IR topics for both stages of the exam.

Practice question: "India and the United States are partners, not allies." Examine this statement in the light of recent developments.
