Farmer producer organisations and co-operatives

Note 198 · Theme 20: Agriculture & food processing · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

Over 85% of Indian farmers are small and marginal, with less than two hectares. Small farms have little bargaining power when buying inputs or selling produce, and find it hard to reach credit, technology and markets. Collective organisations are one answer. Co-operatives have a long history in India, from the Co-operative Credit Societies Act of 1904 to the dairy success of Amul. The 97th Constitutional Amendment of 2011 made forming co-operatives a fundamental right and added Part IXB, though the Supreme Court in 2021 struck down the parts applying to state co-operatives.

Farmer producer organisations (FPOs) are a newer form, usually registered as producer companies under the Companies Act, combining company-style management with member ownership. They buy inputs in bulk, aggregate and grade produce, run storage and processing, and negotiate with buyers. The Centre launched a scheme in 2020 to form and promote 10,000 new FPOs, with support for management costs, equity grants and credit guarantees, implemented through agencies such as the Small Farmers' Agri-Business Consortium (SFAC) and NABARD.

FPOs face real difficulties. Many are small, short of working capital and professional managers, and depend on promoting agencies. Only a minority become commercially viable. Experts suggest linking FPOs with e-NAM, processors and exporters, and giving them easier access to credit.

The creation of a separate Ministry of Co-operation in 2021 and plans to set up multipurpose primary agricultural credit societies in every panchayat show renewed government interest in the co-operative model.

Key takeaways

  • More than 85% of farmers are small and marginal, which weakens their market power.
  • FPOs, usually producer companies, aggregate inputs and outputs; the Centre promoted 10,000 new FPOs from 2020.
  • The 97th Amendment gave co-operatives constitutional status; a Ministry of Co-operation was created in 2021.

Why it matters for UPSC: FPOs and co-operatives are favoured answers to small-farm problems and appear in both Prelims and Mains.

Practice question: Can farmer producer organisations overcome the disadvantages of small landholdings? Discuss their challenges.
