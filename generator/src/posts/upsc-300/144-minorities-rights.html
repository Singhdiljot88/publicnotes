Minorities: constitutional protection and welfare

Note 144 · Theme 15: Social justice & welfare · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

The Constitution does not define "minority", but protects religious and linguistic minorities. Article 29 protects any section of citizens with a distinct language, script or culture in conserving it. Article 30 gives religious and linguistic minorities the right to establish and administer educational institutions of their choice. Articles 25–28 guarantee religious freedom to all. Article 350A asks states to provide primary education in the mother tongue for linguistic minorities, and Article 350B provides for a Special Officer for Linguistic Minorities.

The National Commission for Minorities Act, 1992 created a statutory commission. Under it, the Union government has notified six religious minorities: Muslims, Christians, Sikhs, Buddhists and Parsis (1993) and Jains (2014). Together they made up about 19% of the population in 2011, with Muslims the largest group at 14.2%. The Supreme Court in T. M. A. Pai Foundation (2002) held that minority status is to be determined state by state, which has led to debates about Hindus being a minority in some states and union territories.

The Sachar Committee (2006) found that Muslims lagged behind on many indicators of education, employment and access to credit, and were under-represented in public services. Its findings led to the Prime Minister's 15-Point Programme for minorities and the Multi-sectoral Development Programme, later restructured as the Pradhan Mantri Jan Vikas Karyakram. Scholarships for minority students have been a major part of welfare, though some schemes have been merged or discontinued in recent years.

Current debates include the Waqf (Amendment) Act, 2025, which changed how waqf properties are managed and included non-Muslims in waqf boards, and which is being challenged in the Supreme Court; state anti-conversion laws; and representation of minorities in legislatures and government.

Key takeaways

  • Articles 29 and 30 protect minority culture and educational institutions.
  • Six religious minorities are notified nationally; minority status is determined state-wise (T. M. A. Pai, 2002).
  • The Sachar Committee (2006) documented Muslim disadvantage; the Waqf Amendment Act (2025) is under challenge.

Why it matters for UPSC: Minority rights and welfare appear regularly in GS II, often linked to secularism and social justice.

Practice question: How do Articles 29 and 30 balance minority rights with the state's power to regulate education?
