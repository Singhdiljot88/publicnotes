India's internal security: threats and institutions

Note 261 · Theme 27: Internal security · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

Internal security means protecting a country from threats within its borders, or from outside actors who work through internal channels. India faces an unusually wide range of such threats: left-wing extremism in central and eastern India, insurgencies in the North-East, terrorism and cross-border infiltration in Jammu and Kashmir, communal and ethnic violence, organised crime, drug trafficking, cyber attacks, and radicalisation through online networks. Many of these are linked to underlying problems such as poor governance, inequality, identity conflicts and hostile neighbours.

Under the Constitution, "public order" and "police" are state subjects, so state police forces have primary responsibility. But Article 355 makes it the Union's duty to protect states against external aggression and internal disturbance, and the Ministry of Home Affairs coordinates internal security nationally.

The Union has several central armed police forces (CAPFs). The Central Reserve Police Force (CRPF) is the largest and leads counter-insurgency against Maoists. The Border Security Force (BSF) guards the borders with Pakistan and Bangladesh, the Indo-Tibetan Border Police (ITBP) the border with China, the Sashastra Seema Bal (SSB) the open borders with Nepal and Bhutan, and the Assam Rifles the Myanmar border and parts of the North-East. The Central Industrial Security Force (CISF) protects airports, ports and key installations, and the National Security Guard (NSG) is the counter-terrorist commando force.

Intelligence comes from the Intelligence Bureau (internal), the Research and Analysis Wing (external) and the National Technical Research Organisation. The National Investigation Agency (NIA) investigates terrorism cases nationwide, and the National Security Council, advised by the National Security Adviser, coordinates strategy.

Experts argue that security forces can contain violence, but lasting peace needs development, good governance, justice and political dialogue.

Key takeaways

  • India faces Maoist, north-eastern, J&K-linked, communal, organised crime and cyber threats.
  • Police and public order are state subjects, but Article 355 obliges the Union to protect states.
  • CAPFs such as the CRPF, BSF, ITBP, SSB and Assam Rifles each have specific roles.

Why it matters for UPSC: The roles of security forces and the federal division of security responsibilities are standard GS III questions.
