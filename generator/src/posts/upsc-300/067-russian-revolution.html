The Russian Revolution

Note 067 · Theme 7: World history · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

In 1917 Russia was an autocracy under Tsar Nicholas II, with a mostly peasant population, a small but growing industrial working class, and a government discredited by defeat in the war with Japan (1904–05) and catastrophic losses in the First World War. Food shortages and inflation added to the anger.

In February 1917 (March by the Western calendar), strikes and mutinies in Petrograd forced the Tsar to abdicate. A Provisional Government took over, but shared power with soviets, councils of workers and soldiers. It kept Russia in the war and delayed land reform, losing support. In October 1917 (November by the Western calendar), the Bolsheviks, led by Vladimir Lenin, seized power in Petrograd. Their slogan was "Peace, land and bread".

The Bolsheviks made peace with Germany in the Treaty of Brest-Litovsk (1918), giving up large territories. They nationalised industry and banks and redistributed land. A civil war (1918–21) between the Red Army, organised by Leon Trotsky, and the anti-Bolshevik Whites, supported by foreign powers, ended in Bolshevik victory. The Union of Soviet Socialist Republics was formed in 1922. Lenin's New Economic Policy (1921) allowed limited private trade to revive the economy.

After Lenin's death in 1924, Joseph Stalin took power. He launched five-year plans for rapid industrialisation from 1928 and forced the collectivisation of agriculture, which caused famine, including in Ukraine. His purges of the 1930s killed or imprisoned millions. The revolution inspired communist and anti-colonial movements worldwide, and India's early planners were influenced by the Soviet model of five-year plans, though India remained a democracy.

Key takeaways

  • The February 1917 revolution ended the Tsar's rule; the Bolsheviks seized power in October 1917.
  • Lenin's government withdrew from the war, nationalised industry and won the civil war; the USSR formed in 1922.
  • Stalin's five-year plans and collectivisation transformed the economy at a huge human cost.

Why it matters for UPSC: The Russian Revolution is a core world history topic and helps explain India's planning model and the Cold War.

Practice question: Why did the Bolsheviks succeed in seizing and holding power in Russia between 1917 and 1922?
