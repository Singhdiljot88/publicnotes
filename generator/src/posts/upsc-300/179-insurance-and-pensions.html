Insurance and pensions

Note 179 · Theme 18: Money, banking & markets · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

Insurance spreads risk: many people pay premiums so that those who suffer losses can be compensated. Life insurance pays on death or maturity; general insurance covers health, property, vehicles, crops and other risks. Insurance penetration, premiums as a share of GDP, remains low in India compared with developed economies, and protection gaps are large, especially for health.

The life insurance industry was nationalised in 1956, creating the Life Insurance Corporation (LIC), and general insurance in 1972. Following the Malhotra Committee's recommendations (1994), the sector was opened to private and foreign companies in 2000, and the Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority (IRDA, now IRDAI) was set up under a 1999 law. The foreign direct investment limit was raised from 26% to 49% in 2015 and to 74% in 2021. In December 2025 Parliament passed the Sabka Bima Sabki Raksha (Amendment of Insurance Laws) Act, allowing 100% foreign direct investment in insurance companies. LIC was listed on the stock exchanges in 2022. IRDAI's goal is "Insurance for All by 2047".

Pensions matter because India's population is ageing and most workers are informal. Government employees who joined before 2004 receive a defined benefit pension under the Old Pension Scheme, paid from current revenues. From 2004, new central government employees (except the armed forces) joined the National Pension System (NPS), a defined contribution scheme regulated by the Pension Fund Regulatory and Development Authority (PFRDA). The NPS was opened to all citizens in 2009. Several states have tried to return to the Old Pension Scheme, which economists warn would burden future budgets. The Union government introduced the Unified Pension Scheme (UPS) from April 2025 as an option within NPS, guaranteeing 50% of average basic pay in the last year for employees with 25 years of service.

For informal workers, the Atal Pension Yojana (2015) and PM Shram Yogi Maan-dhan offer small guaranteed pensions.

Key takeaways

  • India's insurance penetration is low; 100% FDI in insurance was allowed by a law passed in December 2025.
  • IRDAI regulates insurance; PFRDA regulates the NPS.
  • The Unified Pension Scheme (from April 2025) guarantees 50% of pay for central employees with 25 years of service.

Why it matters for UPSC: Insurance and pension reforms are regularly tested in GS III and connect to ageing and fiscal sustainability.

Practice question: Is a return to the Old Pension Scheme fiscally sustainable for states? Discuss.
