Mineral resources of India

Note 101 · Theme 11: Resources & world geography · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

India's minerals are concentrated in the ancient rocks of the Peninsular Plateau, especially the Chotanagpur plateau and the belts running through Jharkhand, Odisha, Chhattisgarh, Karnataka and Goa. Geologists divide minerals into metallic (ferrous, such as iron ore and manganese, and non-ferrous, such as bauxite, copper and zinc) and non-metallic (such as mica, limestone and gypsum), with fuel minerals such as coal treated separately.

Iron ore is found mainly as haematite and magnetite. Odisha is the largest producer, followed by Chhattisgarh, Karnataka and Jharkhand; major mines include Bailadila in Chhattisgarh and Kudremukh in Karnataka (now closed for environmental reasons). India is among the world's largest iron ore producers. Manganese, used in steel making, is found in Odisha, Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra and Karnataka. Bauxite, the ore of aluminium, is concentrated in Odisha's Eastern Ghats, notably Panchpatmali in Koraput, and in Jharkhand, Gujarat and Chhattisgarh. Copper comes mainly from Rajasthan's Khetri belt and Jharkhand's Singhbhum, but India imports most of its needs. Zinc and lead are mined at Zawar and Rampura Agucha in Rajasthan. India was once the world's leading producer of mica, from Jharkhand, Bihar and Andhra Pradesh. Limestone, used for cement, is widely available.

Mining raises difficult questions. Most mineral-rich areas are forested and home to Adivasi communities, so mining often involves displacement and conflicts over consent, as in the Niyamgiri hills of Odisha, where gram sabhas rejected bauxite mining in 2013 after a Supreme Court ruling. District Mineral Foundations, created by the 2015 amendment to the MMDR Act, collect a share of royalties for the welfare of mining-affected areas. The same amendment made auctions the method for allotting mineral concessions.

Key takeaways

  • India's minerals are concentrated in the Peninsular Plateau, especially Odisha, Jharkhand, Chhattisgarh and Karnataka.
  • Odisha leads in iron ore and bauxite; Rajasthan leads in copper, zinc and lead.
  • Mining raises conflicts over forests, displacement and consent, addressed partly through District Mineral Foundations.

Why it matters for UPSC: Mineral distribution is a standard Prelims topic, and mining governance appears in Mains geography and governance questions.

Practice question: How can India balance mineral extraction with the rights of forest-dwelling communities?
