Indian painting: murals, miniatures and folk art

Note 025 · Theme 3: Art & culture · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

Indian painting has three broad traditions. Murals were painted on walls, from the Ajanta caves to the Chola frescoes in the Brihadeeswara temple and the Lepakshi temple paintings of Vijayanagara. Miniatures were small, detailed paintings on paper or cloth, often illustrating manuscripts. Folk and tribal painting has been made by communities for ritual and daily life for centuries.

Miniature painting began with palm-leaf manuscripts in Pala Bengal and Jain western India. The Mughal school, founded by Persian masters at Humayun's and Akbar's courts, combined Persian technique with Indian themes; it illustrated the Hamzanama and Akbarnama and reached a height of naturalism under Jahangir, whose artist Ustad Mansur painted birds and animals. Rajput schools in Mewar, Bundi, Kishangarh and elsewhere painted Krishna legends and courtly life in bold colours; the Bani Thani portrait of Kishangarh is famous. The Pahari schools of the Himalayan hill states, such as Basohli and Kangra, are known for lyrical scenes of Radha and Krishna. The Deccan schools of Bijapur and Golconda blended Persian and local influences.

Folk and tribal traditions are a living heritage. Madhubani (Mithila) painting from Bihar uses bright colours and natural dyes. Warli painting from Maharashtra uses simple white geometric figures. Pattachitra from Odisha and Bengal paints mythological stories on cloth. Gond art from Madhya Pradesh uses dots and lines to depict nature. Kalamkari from Andhra Pradesh uses a pen and natural dyes on fabric. Several of these have Geographical Indication tags.

Key takeaways

  • Indian painting includes murals, miniatures, and folk and tribal art.
  • Mughal, Rajput, Pahari and Deccan schools are the main miniature traditions.
  • Madhubani, Warli, Pattachitra, Gond and Kalamkari are living folk traditions.

Why it matters for UPSC: Matching painting styles to regions and courts is a reliable Prelims question type.

Practice question: Compare the Mughal and Rajput schools of miniature painting in subject matter and style.
