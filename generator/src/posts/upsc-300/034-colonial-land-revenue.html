Colonial land revenue: Permanent Settlement, Ryotwari and Mahalwari

Note 034 · Theme 4: Colonial India & 1857 · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

Land revenue was the largest source of income for the Company, and the way it was collected reshaped rural India. Three main systems were introduced.

The Permanent Settlement was introduced in Bengal, Bihar and Orissa by Lord Cornwallis in 1793. Zamindars were recognised as owners of the land and had to pay a fixed revenue to the Company forever. The Company hoped for stable income and a loyal class of improving landlords. In practice, the revenue was set very high, many old zamindars who failed to pay had their estates auctioned, and the new owners often rented land through layers of intermediaries. Peasants lost their customary rights and faced rising rents.

The Ryotwari system, associated with Thomas Munro and Alexander Read, was introduced in large parts of the Madras and Bombay presidencies. Revenue was settled directly with the cultivator (ryot), who was recognised as the owner. The rate was revised periodically, usually every twenty to thirty years, and was often so high that peasants fell into debt.

The Mahalwari system, introduced in the North-Western Provinces, parts of central India and Punjab from 1822 (Holt Mackenzie's regulation) and revised in 1833, settled revenue with the village community or estate (mahal) as a whole, with headmen responsible for collection.

All three systems converted land into a commodity that could be sold or mortgaged, increased the power of moneylenders, and contributed to rural indebtedness and periodic famines. Abolishing zamindari became a major promise of the national movement and an early task of independent India.

Key takeaways

  • The Permanent Settlement (1793) fixed revenue forever with zamindars in Bengal.
  • The Ryotwari system dealt directly with cultivators in Madras and Bombay.
  • The Mahalwari system settled revenue with village communities in north India.

Why it matters for UPSC: Land revenue systems are among the most frequently tested topics in modern history and agrarian change.

Practice question: Compare the Permanent Settlement and the Ryotwari system. How did each affect the peasantry?
