Earthquakes and volcanoes

Note 083 · Theme 9: Physical geography · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

An earthquake is the shaking of the ground caused by a sudden release of energy, usually when rocks along a fault slip. The point inside the Earth where the rupture begins is the focus or hypocentre, and the point on the surface directly above it is the epicentre. Earthquake size is measured by magnitude, now usually on the moment magnitude scale; each whole number step represents about 32 times more energy. Intensity, measured on scales such as the Modified Mercalli scale, describes the effects at a particular place.

Most earthquakes occur along plate boundaries. About 80% of the world's largest earthquakes happen around the Pacific "Ring of Fire", and many others along the Alpine-Himalayan belt. Undersea earthquakes can trigger tsunamis, as in the Indian Ocean tsunami of 26 December 2004, which killed over 200,000 people across several countries, including more than 10,000 in India. The Bureau of Indian Standards seismic zoning map divides India by expected shaking, with the Himalayan belt, the North-East, Kutch and the Andaman and Nicobar Islands in the highest-risk categories. Major Indian earthquakes include Assam (1950), Latur (1993), Bhuj (2001) and Kashmir (2005).

Volcanoes form where magma reaches the surface. They are found at subduction zones (such as the Andes and Japan), divergent boundaries (such as Iceland) and hotspots (such as Hawaii). Volcanoes are classified as active, dormant or extinct, and by shape: shield volcanoes have gentle slopes from runny basaltic lava, while composite or stratovolcanoes are steep and explosive. India's only active volcano is on Barren Island in the Andaman Sea. The Deccan Traps, formed by massive lava flows around 66 million years ago, created the basalt plateau of western India and its black soils.

Key takeaways

  • Magnitude measures energy released; intensity measures local effects.
  • Most earthquakes and volcanoes occur at plate boundaries, especially the Ring of Fire.
  • Barren Island is India's only active volcano; the Deccan Traps are ancient lava flows.

Why it matters for UPSC: Earthquakes, tsunamis and volcanoes link geography with disaster management, a common Mains combination.

Practice question: Why is the Himalayan region highly prone to earthquakes? What measures can reduce the risk?
