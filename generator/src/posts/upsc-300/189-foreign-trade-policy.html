India's trade policy and export promotion

Note 189 · Theme 19: Public finance & external sector · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

India's trade policy has moved from import substitution, with high tariffs and import licensing before 1991, to greater openness after the reforms. Average tariffs fell sharply in the 1990s and 2000s, quantitative restrictions on imports were removed by 2001, and India signed free trade agreements with Sri Lanka, Singapore, ASEAN, South Korea and Japan. From the mid-2010s, policy turned more cautious: India raised some tariffs to protect domestic manufacturing, withdrew from RCEP in 2019, and later signed a new generation of agreements with the UAE (2022), Australia (2022), the European Free Trade Association (2024) and the United Kingdom (2025).

The Foreign Trade Policy 2023, which came into effect on 1 April 2023, has no fixed end date, unlike earlier five-year policies, and is updated as needed. It sets a goal of 2 trillion dollars in exports of goods and services by 2030. It shifts from incentives to remission of taxes and facilitation, promotes districts as export hubs, supports e-commerce exports, and encourages trade settlement in rupees.

Export promotion tools include the Remission of Duties and Taxes on Exported Products (RoDTEP) scheme, which refunds embedded taxes not otherwise credited, replacing the earlier MEIS scheme that was challenged at the WTO; the Export Promotion Capital Goods scheme; Advance Authorisation for duty-free inputs; special economic zones (under the SEZ Act, 2005); and export credit through the Export-Import Bank of India and ECGC. Production-linked incentive schemes have helped expand exports of electronics, especially mobile phones.

India's merchandise exports have been around 430–440 billion dollars a year, while services exports have grown faster, making total exports over 800 billion dollars. India's challenges include a persistent goods trade deficit, especially with China, limited participation in global value chains, quality and standards barriers, and rising protectionism, including high tariffs imposed by the United States in 2025.

Key takeaways

  • Trade policy moved from import substitution to openness after 1991, then to selective protection and new FTAs.
  • The Foreign Trade Policy 2023 has no end date and targets 2 trillion dollars in exports by 2030.
  • RoDTEP, SEZs and PLI schemes support exports; services now drive export growth.

Why it matters for UPSC: Trade policy and export promotion schemes are frequent GS III questions, especially in the context of FTAs and tariffs.

Practice question: What prevents India from becoming a major manufacturing exporter like the East Asian economies?
