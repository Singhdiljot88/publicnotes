Roads, highways and PM Gati Shakti

Note 204 · Theme 21: Industry, infrastructure & energy · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

India has one of the world's largest road networks, over 63 lakh km. National highways, built and maintained by the Union through the National Highways Authority of India (NHAI) and the Ministry of Road Transport and Highways, make up only about 2% of the length, roughly 1.46 lakh km, but carry around 40% of road traffic. State highways and rural roads make up the rest. Roads carry the majority of India's freight and passengers.

Highway building has accelerated since the early 2000s. The Golden Quadrilateral, linking Delhi, Mumbai, Chennai and Kolkata, was a flagship of the National Highways Development Project begun in 1998. The Bharatmala Pariyojana, approved in 2017, focuses on economic corridors, border and coastal roads, and expressways such as the Delhi-Mumbai Expressway. The Pradhan Mantri Gram Sadak Yojana, launched in 2000, connected most eligible rural habitations with all-weather roads, which studies link to better access to markets, schools and jobs. FASTag electronic toll collection became mandatory in 2021.

PM Gati Shakti, the National Master Plan for multimodal connectivity launched in October 2021, is a digital platform that brings together over a dozen ministries, including railways, roads, ports, telecom and power, and state governments, on a common GIS map. The idea is to plan infrastructure together, so that a new highway is not dug up later for a pipeline, and to cut delays in land acquisition and clearances.

Concerns include road safety (India records around 1.7 lakh road deaths a year, among the highest in the world), the quality of construction, rising toll costs, land acquisition disputes and environmental damage, especially in fragile hill areas.

Key takeaways

  • National highways are about 2% of road length but carry around 40% of traffic.
  • Bharatmala (2017) and PMGSY (2000) drove highway and rural road building.
  • PM Gati Shakti (2021) is a GIS-based master plan for coordinated infrastructure planning.

Why it matters for UPSC: Road infrastructure, Gati Shakti and road safety are regular GS III topics.

Practice question: How can PM Gati Shakti reduce cost and time overruns in Indian infrastructure projects?
