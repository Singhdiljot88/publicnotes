Hindustani and Carnatic music

Note 027 · Theme 3: Art & culture · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

Indian classical music is built on two core ideas. A raga is a melodic framework, a set of notes with rules about how to move between them and the mood they evoke. A tala is a rhythmic cycle. Ancient texts such as the Natyashastra and later Matanga's Brihaddeshi and Sharngadeva's Sangita Ratnakara (thirteenth century) laid the theoretical base.

From around the thirteenth and fourteenth centuries, two traditions developed. Hindustani music, in the north, absorbed Persian influences at the courts of the Sultanate and Mughals. Amir Khusrau is traditionally credited with innovations in qawwali and khayal, though some attributions are legendary. Tansen, one of the nine gems of Akbar's court, was a master of dhrupad, the oldest surviving form. Khayal, thumri, tappa and ghazal developed later. Hindustani music is organised in gharanas, family-based schools such as Gwalior, Agra, Kirana and Jaipur. Instruments include the sitar, sarod, tabla, santoor and shehnai; Bismillah Khan made the shehnai a concert instrument.

Carnatic music, in the south, is more composition-centred and closely tied to devotional lyrics. Purandara Dasa (sixteenth century) is called the father of Carnatic music for systematising its teaching. The trinity of Tyagaraja, Muthuswami Dikshitar and Syama Sastri composed thousands of kritis in the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries. The veena, mridangam, violin and ghatam are key instruments. M. S. Subbulakshmi became the first musician to receive the Bharat Ratna, in 1998.

Both systems share the raga-tala foundation, but differ in style, improvisation and repertoire.

Key takeaways

  • Raga (melody) and tala (rhythm) are the foundations of both traditions.
  • Hindustani music has gharanas and Persian influences; dhrupad is its oldest form.
  • Carnatic music centres on compositions, especially by Purandara Dasa and the trinity.

Why it matters for UPSC: Musicians, instruments, gharanas and composers appear in Prelims, often linked to awards.

Practice question: Compare the Hindustani and Carnatic traditions in their history, structure and performance style.
