Elephants, lions and cheetahs: flagship species

Note 244 · Theme 25: Biodiversity & conservation · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

Flagship species are charismatic animals used to build public support for conserving whole ecosystems. Besides the tiger, India runs programmes for several.

The Asian elephant is India's national heritage animal. Project Elephant was launched in 1992 to protect elephants, their habitats and corridors, and to reduce human-elephant conflict. India has more than half of the world's wild Asian elephants, spread across over 30 elephant reserves. Conflict is severe: hundreds of people and around a hundred elephants die each year from encounters, train collisions and electrocution.

The Asiatic lion survives in the wild only in Gujarat's Saurashtra region, centred on the Gir forest. Its population has recovered from a small number in the early twentieth century. The 16th lion census in May 2025 counted 891 lions, up from 674 in 2020, many now living outside protected areas in farmland and coastal areas. Scientists have long urged a second home to protect the species from disease or disasters; the Supreme Court in 2013 ordered translocation to Madhya Pradesh's Kuno, which Gujarat resisted.

The cheetah was declared extinct in India in 1952. Project Cheetah, the world's first intercontinental translocation of a large carnivore, brought eight cheetahs from Namibia to Kuno National Park in September 2022 and twelve from South Africa in 2023. Several died early from infections and other causes, but cubs have been born in India, a second site at Gandhi Sagar sanctuary in Madhya Pradesh was opened in 2025, and further animals have been brought from Botswana. By 2026, reported numbers were around fifty, including cubs born in India. Critics question whether India has enough large, prey-rich grasslands for a free-ranging population.

The Great Indian Bustard and the snow leopard are other species with dedicated programmes.

Key takeaways

  • Project Elephant (1992) protects elephants and corridors; India has over half of the world's wild Asian elephants.
  • The 2025 census counted 891 Asiatic lions, up from 674 in 2020.
  • Project Cheetah reintroduced cheetahs from 2022; numbers have grown, but habitat remains a concern.

Why it matters for UPSC: Species programmes and recent census figures are frequent Prelims topics, and Project Cheetah is often discussed in Mains.

Practice question: Should India prioritise reintroducing extinct species, such as the cheetah, or protecting existing threatened species?
