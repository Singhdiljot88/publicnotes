Harsha, the Pallavas, Chalukyas and Cholas

Note 010 · Theme 1: Ancient India · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

After the Guptas, Harshavardhana (ruled 606–647 CE) built a large kingdom in north India from his capital at Kannauj. His court poet Banabhatta wrote the Harshacharita, and the Chinese pilgrim Xuanzang described his assemblies at Kannauj and Prayag and his patronage of Nalanda. Harsha's march south was stopped on the Narmada by the Chalukya king Pulakeshin II, an event recorded in the Aihole inscription composed by Ravikirti.

In the Deccan and south, three powers competed. The Chalukyas of Badami built temples at Aihole, Badami and Pattadakal (a UNESCO site). The Pallavas of Kanchipuram built the rock-cut and structural monuments of Mahabalipuram, including the Shore Temple, under kings such as Narasimhavarman I. The Pallava-Chalukya rivalry continued for generations.

The Cholas rose in the ninth century and became the dominant southern power. Rajaraja I (985–1014) built the Brihadeeswara temple at Thanjavur, completed around 1010. His son Rajendra I marched to the Ganga, founded a new capital called Gangaikonda Cholapuram, and sent a naval expedition against the Srivijaya kingdom in South-East Asia. The Uttaramerur inscriptions of the tenth century describe village assemblies (sabhas) chosen partly by lot, an often-cited example of local self-government.

These kingdoms show that political power in early medieval India was spread across many regional centres, each with its own art and administration.

Key takeaways

  • Harsha ruled from Kannauj; Xuanzang and Banabhatta are key sources.
  • Pulakeshin II stopped Harsha on the Narmada; the Aihole inscription records this.
  • The Cholas combined temple building, naval power and village assemblies.

Why it matters for UPSC: Temple sites, inscriptions and Chola local administration are recurring Prelims and Mains topics.

Practice question: Describe the main features of local self-government under the Cholas. How does it compare with Panchayati Raj today?
