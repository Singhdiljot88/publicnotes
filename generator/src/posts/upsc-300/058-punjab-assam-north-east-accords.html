The 1980s: Punjab, Assam and the north-eastern accords

Note 058 · Theme 6: India after 1947 · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

The 1980s tested Indian unity in several regions. In Punjab, demands for greater state autonomy, set out in the Anandpur Sahib Resolution of 1973, became entangled with militancy led by Jarnail Singh Bhindranwale. In June 1984, the army carried out Operation Blue Star to remove armed militants from the Golden Temple in Amritsar, damaging the Akal Takht and causing many deaths. Indira Gandhi's assassination that October was followed by anti-Sikh violence in Delhi and elsewhere in which thousands of Sikhs were killed. The Rajiv Gandhi-Longowal Accord of 1985 sought a settlement, but Sant Harchand Singh Longowal was assassinated soon after, and militancy continued until the early 1990s.

In Assam, the All Assam Students' Union (AASU) led a movement from 1979 to 1985 against illegal immigration from Bangladesh, demanding the detection and deportation of foreigners. Violence included the Nellie massacre of 1983. The Assam Accord of August 1985 set 25 March 1971 as the cut-off date: people who entered after that date would be identified as foreigners. Section 6A of the Citizenship Act was inserted to implement this, and the Supreme Court upheld it in 2024. The accord also promised safeguards for Assamese identity.

In Mizoram, the Mizo National Front led by Laldenga had fought an insurgency since 1966. The Mizo Accord of 1986 ended it, Mizoram became a state in 1987, and Laldenga became chief minister. The accord is often cited as one of India's most successful peace settlements.

These accords showed both the potential and the limits of negotiated settlements: they brought peace where they were implemented in good faith, but left lasting disputes where commitments were incomplete.

Key takeaways

  • Operation Blue Star (1984) and the Rajiv-Longowal Accord (1985) marked the Punjab crisis.
  • The Assam Accord (1985) set 25 March 1971 as the cut-off date for identifying foreigners.
  • The Mizo Accord (1986) is considered among India's most successful peace deals.

Why it matters for UPSC: These accords are relevant to questions on internal security, federalism and citizenship.

Practice question: What lessons do the Assam and Mizo accords offer for resolving regional conflicts today?
