The World Trade Organization

Note 152 · Theme 16: International relations & organisations · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

The World Trade Organization (WTO) was set up on 1 January 1995, replacing the General Agreement on Tariffs and Trade (GATT) of 1947, after the Uruguay Round of negotiations. It has 166 members and is based in Geneva. Its highest decision-making body is the Ministerial Conference, which meets roughly every two years, and decisions are generally made by consensus. Its core principles are non-discrimination, through most-favoured-nation treatment (no favouring one trading partner over others, with exceptions for free trade agreements and developing countries) and national treatment (treating imported goods no worse than domestic goods once they enter the market).

Key agreements include those on goods (GATT), services (GATS), intellectual property (TRIPS) and agriculture (the Agreement on Agriculture, or AoA). The AoA classifies farm subsidies into boxes: the amber box (trade-distorting support, subject to limits), the blue box (support linked to production limits) and the green box (minimally distorting support, such as research, allowed without limits). India's minimum support price procurement for public food stocks risks breaching its amber box limits. At the Bali Ministerial in 2013, members agreed a "peace clause" protecting developing countries' public stockholding programmes from legal challenge until a permanent solution is found, and India has invoked it.

The WTO's dispute settlement system was once its strongest feature. But the Appellate Body, its appeals court, has been unable to function since December 2019, because the United States has blocked the appointment of new members. Countries can now appeal panel rulings "into the void", leaving disputes unresolved. A group of members created an interim appeal arrangement (MPIA), which India has not joined.

India's priorities at the WTO include protecting food security and farmers, preserving special and differential treatment for developing countries, resisting new issues such as investment facilitation, and fighting subsidies to fishing fleets of rich countries while protecting small fishers.

Key takeaways

  • The WTO (1995) has 166 members and works mainly by consensus.
  • The Agreement on Agriculture's boxes limit trade-distorting farm subsidies; the 2013 peace clause protects India's food stockholding.
  • The Appellate Body has been paralysed since December 2019.

Why it matters for UPSC: The WTO, especially agriculture and dispute settlement, is a recurring GS II and GS III topic.

Practice question: How does the WTO's Agreement on Agriculture affect India's food security policies?
