From the Simon Commission to Civil Disobedience

Note 046 · Theme 5: The freedom struggle · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

In 1927 the British government appointed the Simon Commission to review India's constitutional progress. It had no Indian members, which outraged Indian opinion. When it arrived in 1928, it was met with black flags and the slogan "Simon go back". In Lahore, Lala Lajpat Rai was injured in a police lathi charge and died weeks later. Indian leaders responded with their own constitutional proposal, the Nehru Report of 1928, which asked for dominion status.

Younger leaders such as Jawaharlal Nehru and Subhas Chandra Bose pressed for full independence. At the Lahore session in December 1929, the Congress declared purna swaraj, complete independence, as its goal, and 26 January 1930 was celebrated as Independence Day.

To launch the struggle, Gandhi chose salt, a necessity of every household, taxed by the government. On 12 March 1930 he set out from Sabarmati Ashram with 78 followers and walked about 385 km to Dandi on the Gujarat coast, where he broke the salt law on 6 April. Civil disobedience spread across India: salt was made illegally, foreign cloth and liquor shops were picketed, and peasants refused to pay taxes. Women took part in large numbers. In the North-West Frontier, Khan Abdul Ghaffar Khan's Khudai Khidmatgars joined, and in the North-East, the young Naga leader Rani Gaidinliu resisted.

The Gandhi-Irwin Pact of March 1931 suspended the movement, and Gandhi attended the Second Round Table Conference in London, which failed. The movement resumed in 1932 and was suppressed. The Communal Award of 1932 gave separate electorates to the depressed classes; Gandhi fasted against it, leading to the Poona Pact with B. R. Ambedkar, which replaced separate electorates with reserved seats.

Key takeaways

  • The all-white Simon Commission (1927) was boycotted, and the Nehru Report (1928) followed.
  • The Dandi March (12 March–6 April 1930) began Civil Disobedience.
  • The Gandhi-Irwin Pact (1931) and the Poona Pact (1932) were key outcomes.

Why it matters for UPSC: The Dandi March, Round Table Conferences and Poona Pact are frequently asked in both Prelims and Mains.

Practice question: Why was salt an effective symbol for a mass movement? What does this tell us about Gandhi's political strategy?
