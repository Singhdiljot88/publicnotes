The Mauryan empire and Ashoka's dhamma

Note 006 · Theme 1: Ancient India · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

Chandragupta Maurya overthrew the Nanda dynasty around 321 BCE, reportedly guided by Kautilya (also called Chanakya), who is traditionally credited with the Arthashastra, a treatise on statecraft. Chandragupta defeated Seleucus Nicator, a successor of Alexander, and gained territory in the north-west. Seleucus sent the envoy Megasthenes to the Mauryan capital at Pataliputra. Chandragupta's son Bindusara extended the empire into the Deccan.

Ashoka, Bindusara's son, is the best-documented ruler of ancient India because of his inscriptions. According to his own Rock Edict XIII, the conquest of Kalinga (in present-day Odisha), around 261 BCE, killed and displaced hundreds of thousands of people. Ashoka expressed remorse and turned to a policy he called dhamma: tolerance among sects, respect for elders and teachers, kindness to servants and animals, and moderation. He appointed officers called dhamma-mahamattas to spread these values and sent missions abroad, including to Sri Lanka.

His edicts were carved on rocks and pillars across the subcontinent, mostly in Prakrit in the Brahmi script, with Kharosthi in the north-west and Greek and Aramaic in Afghanistan. The lion capital from the Sarnath pillar is India's national emblem, and the wheel on it inspired the chakra on the national flag.

The empire declined within about fifty years of Ashoka's death in 232 BCE. The last Mauryan ruler was killed by his general Pushyamitra Shunga around 185 BCE.

Key takeaways

  • Chandragupta founded the empire around 321 BCE; Megasthenes described his court.
  • After Kalinga, Ashoka promoted dhamma, a moral code of tolerance and welfare, not a single religion.
  • Ashoka's edicts are a primary source, and his Sarnath lion capital is the national emblem.

Why it matters for UPSC: Mauryan administration and Ashoka's dhamma appear in both Prelims and Mains, often linked to ideas of ethical governance.

Practice question: Was Ashoka's dhamma a religious policy or a political strategy to hold a diverse empire together? Argue with evidence.
