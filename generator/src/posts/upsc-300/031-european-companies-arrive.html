The Europeans arrive: Portuguese, Dutch, English and French

Note 031 · Theme 4: Colonial India & 1857 · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

The search for a sea route to the spice trade brought Europeans to India. Vasco da Gama reached Calicut on the Malabar coast in 1498, welcomed by the local ruler, the Zamorin. The Portuguese built a network of fortified ports, and Afonso de Albuquerque captured Goa from the Bijapur sultanate in 1510. They used a system of passes (cartaz) to control shipping in the Indian Ocean, but their power declined in the seventeenth century as rivals arrived.

The Dutch East India Company, founded in 1602, focused on the spice islands of Indonesia but also traded in Indian textiles from factories on the Coromandel coast and in Bengal. The English East India Company received its charter from Queen Elizabeth I on 31 December 1600. After Sir Thomas Roe's embassy to Jahangir, it set up factories at Surat, then Madras (Fort St George, 1639), Bombay (received from the Portuguese as part of Catherine of Braganza's dowry in 1661) and Calcutta (1690). The French East India Company was formed in 1664, with Pondicherry as its main base.

In the eighteenth century, the English and French fought three Carnatic wars (1746–1763) in south India, mirroring their rivalry in Europe. The French governor Dupleix pioneered the strategy of supporting rival claimants to local thrones and using trained Indian soldiers (sepoys). The English, under Robert Clive and others, adopted the same methods. The decisive English victory at Wandiwash in 1760 ended French ambitions, leaving them only a few trading posts.

The lesson the Company learned in the Carnatic, that a small trained army could make and unmake rulers, it soon applied in Bengal.

Key takeaways

  • Vasco da Gama reached Calicut in 1498; the Portuguese took Goa in 1510.
  • The English Company was chartered in 1600 and built bases at Surat, Madras, Bombay and Calcutta.
  • The Carnatic wars and the Battle of Wandiwash (1760) ended French competition.

Why it matters for UPSC: The sequence of European arrivals and the Carnatic wars are common Prelims questions on modern history.

Practice question: Why did the English East India Company succeed where the Portuguese, Dutch and French failed in India?
