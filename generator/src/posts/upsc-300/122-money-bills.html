Money bills and financial bills

Note 122 · Theme 13: Legislatures & executives in detail · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

Article 110 defines a money bill as one that contains only provisions dealing with matters such as the imposition or abolition of taxes, government borrowing, the custody of and withdrawals from the Consolidated Fund of India, the appropriation of money from it, and declaring expenditure charged on it. A bill is not a money bill merely because it provides for fines or fees for services.

Money bills follow a special procedure. They can be introduced only in the Lok Sabha and only on the President's recommendation. The Rajya Sabha must return the bill within 14 days with or without recommendations, which the Lok Sabha can accept or reject. If it does not return the bill in time, the bill is deemed passed. The President cannot return a money bill for reconsideration, and there is no joint sitting on it. The Speaker of the Lok Sabha decides whether a bill is a money bill, and Article 110(3) says this decision is final.

Financial bills are wider. Under Article 117, a bill that includes money-bill matters along with other provisions is a financial bill of the first category; it can be introduced only in the Lok Sabha on the President's recommendation but otherwise follows the ordinary procedure, so the Rajya Sabha has full powers over it. A financial bill of the second category involves expenditure from the Consolidated Fund but no money-bill matters; it can be introduced in either house.

The use of the money-bill route has been controversial because it bypasses the Rajya Sabha. The Aadhaar Act, 2016 was passed as a money bill, and the Supreme Court upheld it in 2018, with Justice D. Y. Chandrachud dissenting that this was a "fraud on the Constitution". In Rojer Mathew (2019), concerning tribunal changes made through the Finance Act, 2017, a five-judge bench doubted the Aadhaar reasoning and referred the question to a larger bench, which has yet to give a final ruling.

Key takeaways

  • Article 110 strictly defines money bills; the Speaker certifies them.
  • The Rajya Sabha can only make recommendations on money bills within 14 days.
  • Passing non-financial laws as money bills is contested; a larger Supreme Court bench is examining the issue.

Why it matters for UPSC: The money bill procedure and the Aadhaar controversy are frequent Prelims and Mains polity questions.

Practice question: Should the Speaker's certification of a money bill be open to judicial review? Discuss.
