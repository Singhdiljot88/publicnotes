The American Revolution

Note 062 · Theme 7: World history · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

In the mid-eighteenth century, Britain had thirteen colonies along the Atlantic coast of North America. After the costly Seven Years' War (1756–63), Britain tried to raise revenue from the colonies through new taxes, including the Stamp Act of 1765 and the Townshend duties. Colonists objected that they had no representatives in the British Parliament, summed up in the slogan "No taxation without representation". The Boston Tea Party of 1773, in which colonists dumped British tea into Boston harbour, led to punitive laws and escalating conflict.

Fighting began at Lexington and Concord in 1775. On 4 July 1776, the Continental Congress adopted the Declaration of Independence, drafted mainly by Thomas Jefferson. It declared that all men are created equal and have unalienable rights to life, liberty and the pursuit of happiness, and that governments derive their powers from the consent of the governed. With crucial help from France, the colonial army under George Washington won the war; the British surrendered at Yorktown in 1781, and the Treaty of Paris of 1783 recognised American independence.

The Constitution of the United States, drafted in Philadelphia in 1787, created a federal republic with a separation of powers between the legislature, executive and judiciary, and checks and balances among them. The Bill of Rights, the first ten amendments, was added in 1791.

The revolution had limits: slavery continued, women and Native Americans were excluded, and voting was restricted to property-owning men. But its ideas of popular sovereignty, written constitutions and rights influenced the French Revolution and, later, India's Constitution, which borrowed the idea of a written bill of rights, judicial review and an independent judiciary.

Key takeaways

  • Taxation without representation led to conflict between Britain and its colonies.
  • The Declaration of Independence (4 July 1776) asserted natural rights and government by consent.
  • The US Constitution (1787) introduced federalism, separation of powers and judicial review.

Why it matters for UPSC: The American Revolution is a standard world history topic and a source of several Indian constitutional features.
