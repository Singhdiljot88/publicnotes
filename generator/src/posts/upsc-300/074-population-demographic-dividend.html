Population and the demographic dividend

Note 074 · Theme 8: Indian society · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

India's 2011 Census counted 121.1 crore (1.21 billion) people. United Nations estimates suggest India overtook China as the world's most populous country around April 2023, with over 1.4 billion people. The next census, delayed from 2021 by the pandemic, is being held in two phases: house-listing from April to September 2026 and population enumeration with a reference date of 1 March 2027 (1 October 2026 for snow-bound areas). It will be the first fully digital census and will record caste.

Fertility has fallen sharply. The total fertility rate (TFR), the average number of children a woman would have in her lifetime, fell to 2.0 in NFHS-5 (2019–21), below the replacement level of 2.1. There are large regional differences: southern states such as Kerala and Tamil Nadu have very low fertility, while states such as Bihar and Uttar Pradesh remain above replacement. This divergence affects debates on delimitation and the sharing of central taxes.

India's age structure creates a demographic dividend: a large share of the population is of working age, which can boost growth if people are healthy, educated and employed. The window is estimated to last until around the 2040s. Realising it depends on creating enough productive jobs, raising female labour force participation and improving skills. Otherwise, a youth bulge can mean unemployment and frustration.

India is also beginning to age. The share of people aged 60 and above, around 10% in 2021, is projected to nearly double by 2050, raising questions about pensions, health care and care for the elderly, especially women who outlive their spouses.

Key takeaways

  • The 2011 Census counted 121.1 crore people; India is now the most populous country.
  • The TFR fell to 2.0 in NFHS-5, with large north-south differences.
  • The demographic dividend requires jobs, skills and women's work, and India is also starting to age.

Why it matters for UPSC: Population trends feed into questions on economy, federalism (delimitation and finance) and social policy.

Practice question: What must India do to convert its demographic dividend into economic growth before the window closes?
