Self-help groups, NGOs and pressure groups

Note 138 · Theme 14: Governance & public policy · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

Beyond the state and the market, civil society organisations play a large role in Indian governance. They include non-governmental organisations (NGOs), self-help groups (SHGs), cooperatives, trade unions, professional associations and community-based organisations.

Self-help groups are small groups, usually of 10 to 20 women, who save regularly, lend to each other, and access bank credit as a group. The SHG-bank linkage programme was started by NABARD in 1992. The Deendayal Antyodaya Yojana-National Rural Livelihoods Mission (DAY-NRLM), launched in 2011, has mobilised around ten crore rural women into SHGs, supported with revolving funds, bank credit and training. SHGs have improved women's access to credit, confidence and participation in local governance, and Kerala's Kudumbashree is often cited as a model. The government's "Lakhpati Didi" initiative aims to help SHG women earn at least Rs 1 lakh a year. Challenges include over-indebtedness, low-value enterprises and limited market access.

NGOs work in service delivery, advocacy, research and rights. They have contributed to laws such as the RTI Act and MGNREGA. Foreign-funded NGOs are regulated under the Foreign Contribution (Regulation) Act, 2010, amended in 2020 to tighten controls, and thousands of FCRA licences have been cancelled, which critics say shrinks civic space while the government cites accountability and national interest.

Pressure groups, such as business associations (FICCI, CII), trade unions, farmers' organisations and caste associations, try to influence policy without seeking power themselves. They use lobbying, petitions, protests and the media. They provide expertise and represent interests, but can also favour well-organised groups over the unorganised.

Cooperatives, from dairy (Amul) to credit, are another important form; a separate Ministry of Cooperation was created in 2021.

Key takeaways

  • DAY-NRLM has mobilised around ten crore women into self-help groups.
  • NGOs have shaped key laws, but FCRA rules have tightened since 2020.
  • Pressure groups influence policy without seeking power; cooperatives have had a separate ministry since 2021.

Why it matters for UPSC: The role of SHGs, NGOs and pressure groups is part of the GS II syllabus and frequently asked.

Practice question: How have self-help groups changed the lives of rural women? What limits their potential?
