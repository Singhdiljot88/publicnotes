The Harappan civilisation: India's first cities

Note 002 · Theme 1: Ancient India · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

The Harappan or Indus Valley civilisation was one of the world's earliest urban cultures, alongside Mesopotamia and Egypt. Its mature phase is usually dated to about 2600–1900 BCE. Harappa was excavated in 1921 under Daya Ram Sahni and Mohenjo-daro in 1922 under R. D. Banerji. Sites now number in the thousands, spread across present-day Pakistan, north-west India and Gujarat, with major Indian sites at Dholavira, Lothal, Kalibangan, Rakhigarhi and Banawali.

The cities were planned. Streets ran in a rough grid, houses were built of baked bricks of standard proportions, and covered drains ran along the streets. Mohenjo-daro has the Great Bath, a waterproofed tank probably used for ritual bathing. Lothal in Gujarat has a structure widely interpreted as a dockyard. Kalibangan in Rajasthan preserves an early ploughed field, and Dholavira, with its elaborate water reservoirs, became a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2021.

Harappans used standardised weights, made seals of steatite carved with animals such as the "unicorn", and traded with Mesopotamia, where records mention a land called Meluhha. Their script, found on thousands of short inscriptions, has still not been deciphered, so we know nothing for certain about their language or political system. No grand palaces or royal tombs have been found.

The decline after about 1900 BCE was gradual. Explanations include shifting rivers, drying climate, declining trade and floods; no single cause is accepted.

Key takeaways

  • The mature Harappan phase dates to about 2600–1900 BCE.
  • Planned streets, drains, standard bricks and weights show strong civic organisation.
  • The script is undeciphered, and the decline had several likely causes.

Why it matters for UPSC: Harappan sites and their distinctive features are among the most frequently asked Prelims history facts.

Practice question: What does the town planning of Harappan cities suggest about the society and administration of the time?
