Mission LiFE and the Green Credit Programme

Note 240 · Theme 24: Environment & ecology · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

Most climate policy focuses on governments and industry. Mission LiFE (Lifestyle for Environment), announced by Prime Minister Narendra Modi at COP26 in Glasgow in 2021 and launched in October 2022, focuses on individuals and communities. It promotes "mindful and deliberate utilisation" instead of "mindless and destructive consumption". It lists simple actions in seven areas: saving energy, saving water, cutting single-use plastic, adopting sustainable food systems, reducing waste, adopting healthy lifestyles and reducing e-waste. India's updated climate pledge (NDC) of 2022 included the promotion of a sustainable lifestyle based on traditions of conservation, and the idea was reflected in G20 statements during India's presidency in 2023.

The Green Credit Programme is a market-based mechanism meant to reward voluntary environmental actions. Its rules were notified in October 2023 under the Environment (Protection) Act. Individuals, communities, companies and local bodies can earn tradable green credits for activities such as tree planting, water conservation, sustainable agriculture, waste management, reducing air pollution and mangrove conservation. The Indian Council of Forestry Research and Education administers it. Tree planting on degraded land was the first activity to be taken up, with methods for calculating credits issued in 2024.

The green credit system is separate from the carbon credit trading scheme under the Energy Conservation Act, which deals with greenhouse gas emissions. Supporters say green credits mobilise private money for conservation. Critics worry that counting trees planted rather than survival, or allowing credits to offset damage elsewhere, could lead to greenwashing, and that planting on "degraded" land may harm grasslands and the communities that use them.

Key takeaways

  • Mission LiFE (launched October 2022) targets individual behaviour across seven themes, such as saving energy and water.
  • The Green Credit Programme (rules of October 2023) rewards voluntary environmental actions with tradable credits.
  • Green credits differ from carbon credits; risks include greenwashing and planting on ecologically valuable grasslands.

Why it matters for UPSC: LiFE and green credits reflect India's push to make behaviour and markets part of climate policy, a likely GS III and essay theme.

Practice question: Can individual lifestyle change, as promoted by Mission LiFE, make a meaningful contribution to tackling climate change?
