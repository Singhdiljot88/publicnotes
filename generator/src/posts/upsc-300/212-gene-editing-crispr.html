Gene editing and CRISPR

Note 212 · Theme 22: Science & technology · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

Gene editing means making precise changes to an organism's DNA, such as deleting, inserting or altering a few bases. The most important tool is CRISPR-Cas9, adapted from a natural defence system that bacteria use against viruses. A guide RNA leads the Cas9 enzyme to a specific spot in the genome, where it cuts the DNA; the cell's repair process then disables the gene or inserts a new sequence. Emmanuelle Charpentier and Jennifer Doudna won the 2020 Nobel Prize in Chemistry for developing it. CRISPR is cheaper, faster and more precise than earlier methods.

In medicine, CRISPR offers treatments for genetic diseases. In 2023 the first CRISPR-based therapy, Casgevy, was approved in the United Kingdom and the United States for sickle cell disease and beta thalassaemia. India, which has a large number of sickle cell patients, launched the National Sickle Cell Anaemia Elimination Mission in 2023, and Indian scientists are developing a low-cost CRISPR therapy. In agriculture, gene editing can produce crops resistant to drought, disease and pests.

India distinguishes between kinds of gene editing. In 2022 the government exempted SDN-1 and SDN-2 genome-edited plants, which involve small changes to the plant's own genes without inserting foreign DNA, from the strict biosafety rules that apply to genetically modified (GM) organisms. In May 2025, India released two genome-edited rice varieties developed by the Indian Council of Agricultural Research: DRR Dhan 100 (Kamala), with higher yield, and Pusa DST Rice 1, with better tolerance of drought and salinity. Critics say the exemption removes needed safety checks.

The biggest ethical concern is editing human embryos, whose changes would pass to future generations. In 2018 a Chinese scientist, He Jiankui, announced the birth of gene-edited babies and was jailed. India's guidelines prohibit germline editing for reproduction.

Key takeaways

  • CRISPR-Cas9 cuts DNA at a precise spot guided by RNA; it won the 2020 Nobel Prize.
  • India exempted SDN-1 and SDN-2 edited plants from GM rules in 2022 and released two genome-edited rice varieties in 2025.
  • Germline editing of human embryos is prohibited in India and widely condemned.

Why it matters for UPSC: CRISPR and India's genome-edited crops are very likely to appear in Prelims and Mains science questions.
