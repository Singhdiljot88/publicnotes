The IMF, the World Bank and new development banks

Note 153 · Theme 16: International relations & organisations · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

The International Monetary Fund (IMF) and the World Bank were created at the Bretton Woods conference in the United States in 1944, and are sometimes called the Bretton Woods institutions. India was a founding member.

The IMF promotes international monetary cooperation and financial stability. It monitors countries' economic policies (surveillance, through annual Article IV consultations), lends to countries facing balance of payments crises, usually with policy conditions, and provides technical assistance. India borrowed from the IMF during the 1991 crisis. The IMF also issues Special Drawing Rights (SDRs), an international reserve asset whose value is based on a basket of five currencies: the US dollar, euro, Chinese renminbi, Japanese yen and British pound. Voting power depends on quotas, which reflect countries' economic size; the United States has over 16% of votes, enough to veto major decisions that require an 85% majority. Reforms to give emerging economies a larger share have been slow. The IMF publishes the World Economic Outlook and the Global Financial Stability Report.

The World Bank Group finances development. Its main arms are the International Bank for Reconstruction and Development (IBRD), lending to middle-income countries, and the International Development Association (IDA), providing low-cost loans and grants to the poorest. The group also includes the International Finance Corporation (lending to the private sector), MIGA (investment guarantees) and ICSID (investment disputes). India is among the largest cumulative borrowers. By tradition, the World Bank's president is American and the IMF's managing director is European.

Dissatisfaction with Western dominance led to new institutions. The BRICS-led New Development Bank, headquartered in Shanghai, began operations in 2015, with India's K. V. Kamath as its first president. The Asian Infrastructure Investment Bank (AIIB), headquartered in Beijing, began in 2016; India is its second-largest shareholder after China and one of its largest borrowers.

Key takeaways

  • The IMF and World Bank were created at Bretton Woods in 1944.
  • The IMF lends for balance of payments crises and issues SDRs; votes depend on quotas.
  • The New Development Bank (2015) and AIIB (2016) give emerging economies more voice.

Why it matters for UPSC: Bretton Woods institutions and newer banks are standard Prelims and Mains IR topics.
