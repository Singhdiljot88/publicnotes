SAARC and BIMSTEC: regionalism in South Asia

Note 154 · Theme 16: International relations & organisations · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

The South Asian Association for Regional Cooperation (SAARC) was founded in Dhaka in December 1985 by Bangladesh, Bhutan, India, the Maldives, Nepal, Pakistan and Sri Lanka; Afghanistan joined in 2007. Its secretariat is in Kathmandu. Its charter excludes bilateral and contentious issues and requires unanimous decisions. It created the South Asian Free Trade Area (SAFTA), in force from 2006, the SAARC Development Fund and the South Asian University in New Delhi.

SAARC has achieved little. Intra-regional trade remains around 5% of the region's total trade, far below ASEAN's. The main obstacle has been tension between India and Pakistan. The last SAARC summit was held in Kathmandu in 2014. The 2016 summit, due to be held in Islamabad, was cancelled after India and several other members pulled out following the terrorist attack on an army camp at Uri.

India has turned instead to BIMSTEC, the Bay of Bengal Initiative for Multi-Sectoral Technical and Economic Cooperation, founded in 1997 through the Bangkok Declaration. It has seven members: five from South Asia (Bangladesh, Bhutan, India, Nepal and Sri Lanka) and two from South-East Asia (Myanmar and Thailand), linking India's Neighbourhood First and Act East policies. Its secretariat is in Dhaka. The BIMSTEC Charter was adopted at the Colombo summit in 2022 and came into force in 2024, giving the grouping a legal personality. The sixth summit was held in Bangkok in April 2025. Cooperation has been organised into seven pillars, with India leading security, including counter-terrorism and disaster management.

BIMSTEC also faces challenges: a free trade agreement has been under negotiation since 2004 without conclusion, and instability in Myanmar and political changes in Bangladesh complicate cooperation.

Key takeaways

  • SAARC (1985) has eight members but has not held a summit since 2014.
  • India-Pakistan tensions, especially after Uri in 2016, have paralysed SAARC.
  • BIMSTEC (1997) has seven members; its charter came into force in 2024.

Why it matters for UPSC: SAARC and BIMSTEC are frequently asked in Prelims and in Mains questions on regional cooperation.

Practice question: Can BIMSTEC succeed where SAARC failed? Discuss the opportunities and obstacles.
