Nationalism and the unification of Italy and Germany

Note 064 · Theme 7: World history · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

After Napoleon's defeat, the Congress of Vienna (1814–15), led by the Austrian statesman Metternich, tried to restore old monarchies and suppress liberal and nationalist movements. But nationalism, the idea that people sharing a language, culture and history should form their own state, spread across Europe. Revolutions broke out in many countries in 1848, and though most failed, they kept nationalist hopes alive.

Italy was divided into several states, some under Austrian control. Its unification, the Risorgimento, combined three figures. Giuseppe Mazzini, through his Young Italy movement, gave it an idealistic, republican vision. Count Camillo di Cavour, prime minister of Piedmont-Sardinia, used diplomacy and alliance with France to defeat Austria. Giuseppe Garibaldi led his volunteer Red Shirts to conquer Sicily and Naples in 1860, then handed them to Piedmont's king. Victor Emmanuel II was proclaimed King of Italy in 1861. Venice was added in 1866 and Rome in 1870.

Germany was a loose confederation of 39 states, dominated by Austria and Prussia. Otto von Bismarck, appointed Prussian minister-president in 1862, unified Germany through "blood and iron". Prussia won three short wars: against Denmark (1864), Austria (1866) and France (1870–71). In January 1871 the German Empire was proclaimed in the Hall of Mirrors at Versailles, with the Prussian king as Kaiser Wilhelm I.

German unification changed the European balance of power. France's loss of Alsace-Lorraine left lasting bitterness, and rivalry among the great powers grew, contributing to the alliances that led to the First World War.

Key takeaways

  • The Congress of Vienna tried to suppress nationalism, but the 1848 revolutions kept it alive.
  • Mazzini, Cavour and Garibaldi unified Italy by 1861, completed in 1870.
  • Bismarck unified Germany through three wars, culminating in the German Empire in 1871.

Why it matters for UPSC: The unification of Italy and Germany is a standard world history question, often compared with Indian nationalism.

Practice question: Compare the roles of diplomacy and war in the unification of Italy and Germany.
