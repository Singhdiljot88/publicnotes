Nuclear policy and export control regimes

Note 160 · Theme 16: International relations & organisations · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

India conducted its first nuclear test in 1974 and a series of five tests, Operation Shakti, in May 1998, after which it declared itself a nuclear weapons state. India has not signed the Nuclear Non-Proliferation Treaty (NPT) of 1968, which recognises only five nuclear weapons states (those that tested before 1967) and requires others to give up nuclear weapons; India regards it as discriminatory. India has also not signed the Comprehensive Nuclear-Test-Ban Treaty (1996), but maintains a voluntary moratorium on testing.

India's nuclear doctrine, adopted in 2003, is based on credible minimum deterrence and no first use: India will not use nuclear weapons first, but will retaliate massively against a nuclear attack on Indian territory or forces anywhere. India retains the option of nuclear retaliation against a major biological or chemical attack. Political control lies with the Nuclear Command Authority, headed by the Prime Minister. India's nuclear triad was completed when the submarine INS Arihant carried out its first deterrence patrol in 2018.

Export control regimes are informal groups of countries that coordinate controls on sensitive goods and technology. India joined the Missile Technology Control Regime (MTCR) in 2016, the Wassenaar Arrangement on conventional arms and dual-use goods in 2017, and the Australia Group on chemical and biological weapons in 2018. It has not been admitted to the Nuclear Suppliers Group (NSG), which controls nuclear trade, mainly because China insists that non-NPT members be treated uniformly, linking India's case to Pakistan's. However, the NSG granted India a special waiver in September 2008, allowing civil nuclear trade, after the India-US deal and India's separation of civil and military facilities under IAEA safeguards.

India has civil nuclear cooperation agreements with several countries, including the US, France, Russia, Japan and Australia, which supplies uranium.

Key takeaways

  • India tested in 1974 and 1998; it has not signed the NPT or CTBT.
  • India's doctrine is credible minimum deterrence and no first use, under the Nuclear Command Authority.
  • India joined the MTCR (2016), Wassenaar (2017) and Australia Group (2018), but not the NSG.

Why it matters for UPSC: Nuclear doctrine and export control regimes are frequently tested in Prelims and Mains.

Practice question: Why does India seek membership of the Nuclear Suppliers Group despite already having a waiver?
