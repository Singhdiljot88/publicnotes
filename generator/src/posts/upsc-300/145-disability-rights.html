Rights of persons with disabilities

Note 145 · Theme 15: Social justice & welfare · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

The 2011 Census counted about 2.68 crore persons with disabilities, 2.21% of the population, though experts believe this is a significant undercount; global estimates suggest around 15% of people have some form of disability. India ratified the UN Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities (UNCRPD) in 2007.

The Rights of Persons with Disabilities Act, 2016 replaced a 1995 law and adopted a rights-based approach. It expanded the list of recognised disabilities from 7 to 21, adding conditions such as acid attack survivors, autism spectrum disorder, specific learning disabilities, thalassaemia, haemophilia, Parkinson's disease, multiple sclerosis and speech and language disabilities. It raised reservation in government jobs for persons with benchmark disabilities (at least 40%) from 3% to 4%, and in higher education from 3% to at least 5%. It guarantees free education for children with benchmark disabilities aged six to eighteen, makes discrimination punishable, and requires public buildings, transport and services to be accessible within set timelines. A Chief Commissioner and state commissioners monitor implementation.

The Accessible India Campaign (Sugamya Bharat Abhiyan), launched in 2015, aimed to make buildings, transport and digital information accessible. The Unique Disability ID (UDID) card provides a single national identity for persons with disabilities. Indian Sign Language has been promoted through a research and training centre.

The courts have pushed implementation. In Vikash Kumar (2021), the Supreme Court recognised the right to reasonable accommodation, and in 2024 it held that accessibility rules must be mandatory, not merely aspirational, directing the government to make them enforceable.

Gaps remain large: many public buildings and websites are still inaccessible, reservation posts go unfilled, and disabled people face high rates of unemployment and social stigma.

Key takeaways

  • The 2011 Census counted 2.21% of people as disabled, likely an undercount.
  • The RPwD Act (2016) recognises 21 disabilities, with 4% job and 5% higher education reservation.
  • The Supreme Court has recognised reasonable accommodation and enforceable accessibility.

Why it matters for UPSC: Disability rights are part of the vulnerable sections topic in GS II and also appear in ethics questions.

Practice question: What are the main barriers to the inclusion of persons with disabilities in India? Suggest measures.
