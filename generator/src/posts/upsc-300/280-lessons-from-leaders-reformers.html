Lessons from leaders, reformers and administrators

Note 280 · Theme 28: Ethics: foundations · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

The syllabus asks candidates to draw lessons from the lives and teachings of great leaders, reformers and administrators. The aim is not to memorise biographies but to show how values were put into practice under pressure.

Among national leaders, Mahatma Gandhi showed that the means matter as much as the ends, and that personal conduct can carry moral authority. Sardar Patel combined firmness with negotiation in integrating the princely states and defended a neutral civil service. Lal Bahadur Shastri resigned as Railway Minister in 1956 taking moral responsibility for a rail accident, an example of accountability. Dr A. P. J. Abdul Kalam was known for simplicity, humility and commitment to young people.

Reformers offer lessons in moral courage. Raja Ram Mohan Roy, Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar and Savitribai and Jyotirao Phule challenged powerful social customs at personal risk. B. R. Ambedkar turned personal experience of discrimination into a constitutional vision of equality. Baba Amte devoted his life to people affected by leprosy.

Administrators provide models closer to the civil servant's own work. T. N. Seshan, as Chief Election Commissioner (1990–96), enforced the model code of conduct strictly and showed how an institution can be strengthened from within. E. Sreedharan led the Konkan Railway and the Delhi Metro to completion on time and with integrity. Armstrong Pame, an IAS officer in Manipur, built a 100 km road through community contributions. Officers such as Satyendra Dubey, killed in 2003 after exposing corruption in a highway project, show the cost of integrity and the need for whistleblower protection.

International figures such as Nelson Mandela (reconciliation over revenge) and Abraham Lincoln (leading through moral conviction in crisis) are also often cited.

In answers, the best examples are specific: a decision, the value it reflected and the lesson it holds for administration today.

Key takeaways

  • Gandhi, Patel and Shastri show the importance of means, firmness and moral responsibility.
  • Reformers such as the Phules and Ambedkar show moral courage against social pressure.
  • Administrators such as T. N. Seshan and E. Sreedharan show how integrity can transform institutions.

Why it matters for UPSC: Examples from leaders and administrators are directly asked and strengthen answers throughout GS IV.

Practice question: Choose an administrator you admire and explain the values their career demonstrates and their relevance today.
