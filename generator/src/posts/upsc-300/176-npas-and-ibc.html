Bad loans, the twin balance sheet problem and the IBC

Note 176 · Theme 18: Money, banking & markets · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

A non-performing asset (NPA) is a loan on which interest or principal has remained overdue for more than 90 days. Banks must set aside provisions against such loans, which reduces their profits and capital.

In the 2000s boom, banks, especially public sector banks, lent heavily to infrastructure, power, steel and telecom companies. Many projects ran into delays, cost overruns, falling prices and policy problems. Banks often "evergreened" bad loans by restructuring them rather than recognising losses. In 2015, the RBI's Asset Quality Review forced banks to recognise stressed loans. The gross NPA ratio of scheduled commercial banks rose to a peak of over 11% in March 2018. The Economic Survey 2016-17 called this the twin balance sheet problem: over-indebted companies could not invest, and weakened banks could not lend, holding back growth.

The response had several parts, often summarised as the "4Rs": recognition, resolution, recapitalisation and reform. The Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC), 2016 created a time-bound process in which creditors take control of a defaulting company through a committee of creditors and either approve a resolution plan or send it to liquidation, through the National Company Law Tribunal. It shifted power from debtors to creditors, summed up in the phrase "creditor in control". The government injected large amounts of capital into public sector banks, merged weaker banks, and set up the National Asset Reconstruction Company Limited (NARCL), a "bad bank", in 2021 to take over large stressed loans.

The results have been significant. According to the RBI's Financial Stability Report of June 2026, the gross NPA ratio of scheduled commercial banks had fallen to around 2%, a multi-decade low. The IBC has recovered substantial sums, though recovery rates for creditors in many cases are low and delays in tribunals often exceed the legal timelines.

Key takeaways

  • An NPA is a loan overdue by more than 90 days; gross NPAs peaked above 11% in March 2018.
  • The IBC (2016) created a creditor-led, time-bound insolvency process through the NCLT.
  • Recognition, recapitalisation and resolution brought the gross NPA ratio down to around 2% by 2026.

Why it matters for UPSC: NPAs, the twin balance sheet problem and the IBC are core GS III banking topics.
