The Cold War and decolonisation

Note 070 · Theme 7: World history · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

The Cold War (1947–1991) was a rivalry between the United States and the Soviet Union and their allies, fought through ideology, arms races, propaganda and proxy wars rather than direct conflict. The US policy of containment, expressed in the Truman Doctrine (1947) and the Marshall Plan for European recovery, aimed to stop the spread of communism. Military alliances formed: NATO in 1949 and the Warsaw Pact in 1955. Germany was divided, and the Berlin Wall was built in 1961.

The Cold War turned hot in Asia. The Korean War (1950–53) ended in stalemate, and the Vietnam War ended with the fall of Saigon in 1975. The Cuban Missile Crisis of October 1962 brought the world closest to nuclear war. Both superpowers built vast nuclear arsenals, later limited by treaties such as the Nuclear Non-Proliferation Treaty (1968), which India has not signed, viewing it as discriminatory.

Decolonisation happened alongside. India and Pakistan became independent in 1947, Indonesia in 1949, Ghana in 1957 and most of Africa by the 1960s. The year 1960 alone saw seventeen African countries become independent. Some decolonisation was peaceful; some, as in Algeria and Kenya, was violent. Many new states joined the Non-Aligned Movement to avoid choosing sides.

The Cold War ended as the Soviet economy stagnated. Mikhail Gorbachev's reforms of glasnost (openness) and perestroika (restructuring) loosened control. The Berlin Wall fell in November 1989, Germany reunified in 1990, and the Soviet Union dissolved in December 1991. India, which had close ties with the Soviet Union, had to rethink its foreign and economic policy, contributing to the 1991 reforms and closer ties with the United States.

Key takeaways

  • The Cold War was a US-Soviet rivalry from 1947 to 1991, with alliances, arms races and proxy wars.
  • Decolonisation created dozens of new states, many of which joined NAM.
  • The fall of the Berlin Wall (1989) and the Soviet collapse (1991) ended the Cold War and reshaped India's policies.

Why it matters for UPSC: The Cold War and decolonisation are essential context for India's foreign policy and the NAM.

Practice question: How did the end of the Cold War change India's foreign and economic policies?
