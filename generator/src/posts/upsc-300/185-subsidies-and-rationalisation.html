Subsidies: food, fertiliser and fuel

Note 185 · Theme 19: Public finance & external sector · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

A subsidy is a payment or price support by the government that lowers the cost of a good or service for consumers or producers. Subsidies can serve important goals: protecting the poor from high food prices, keeping farm inputs affordable, or encouraging socially valuable activities. But badly designed subsidies are costly, distort prices, encourage overuse and often benefit the better-off more than the poor.

India's three major explicit subsidies are food, fertiliser and petroleum. The food subsidy funds the procurement, storage and distribution of grain by the Food Corporation of India and states under the National Food Security Act. Since January 2024, about 81 crore beneficiaries receive free foodgrains under the Pradhan Mantri Garib Kalyan Anna Yojana, extended for five years. The food subsidy is the largest single subsidy.

The fertiliser subsidy keeps prices low for farmers. Urea prices are fixed by the government, while phosphatic and potassic fertilisers are subsidised under a Nutrient Based Subsidy scheme introduced in 2010. Because urea is much cheaper relative to other fertilisers, farmers overuse nitrogen, damaging soils and increasing imports. Neem-coated urea (2015) reduced diversion to industrial use, and the government has promoted nano urea and the "One Nation One Fertiliser" Bharat brand. Global price shocks, such as those after the Ukraine war, sharply raise the subsidy bill.

Petroleum subsidies have shrunk since petrol prices were deregulated in 2010 and diesel in 2014. Subsidies now mainly cover LPG for poorer households, including under the Pradhan Mantri Ujjwala Yojana, launched in 2016 to provide free connections to women from poor households.

Implicit subsidies, through free electricity for farmers and below-cost services, are large but often hidden in state budgets and the losses of power distribution companies. Direct Benefit Transfer and better targeting are the main tools for rationalising subsidies.

Key takeaways

  • Food, fertiliser and petroleum are India's main explicit subsidies; food is the largest.
  • Cheap urea encourages nitrogen overuse; the Nutrient Based Subsidy scheme covers other fertilisers.
  • Petrol and diesel were deregulated in 2010 and 2014; LPG subsidies target poorer households.

Why it matters for UPSC: Subsidy design and reform appear in GS III questions on public finance, agriculture and the environment.
