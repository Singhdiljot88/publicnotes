Measuring poverty in India

Note 165 · Theme 17: Growth, development & inflation · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

Poverty can be measured in different ways. Absolute poverty uses a fixed line, such as the cost of a minimum basket of food and essentials. Relative poverty compares people with others in the same society. Multidimensional poverty looks at deprivations in health, education and living standards rather than income alone.

India's official poverty lines were set by a series of expert groups. The Lakdawala committee (1993) based lines on calorie needs. The Tendulkar committee (2009) moved to a broader consumption basket including health and education; by its method, poverty fell from 37.2% in 2004-05 to 21.9% in 2011-12. The Rangarajan committee (2014) proposed a higher line, estimating poverty at 29.5% in 2011-12, but its method was never formally adopted. Because consumption surveys were not released between 2011-12 and 2022-23, India had no official poverty estimate for over a decade.

The Household Consumption Expenditure Surveys of 2022-23 and 2023-24 filled the gap. Using them, the World Bank estimated in June 2025 that India's extreme poverty, at its revised international line of 3 dollars a day in 2021 purchasing power parity, fell from 27.1% in 2011-12 to 5.3% in 2022-23. The World Bank noted that poverty at higher lines, appropriate for middle-income countries, remains much more widespread.

NITI Aayog's National Multidimensional Poverty Index uses twelve indicators, including nutrition, child mortality, schooling, cooking fuel, sanitation, drinking water, housing, electricity, assets and bank accounts. Its discussion paper of January 2024 estimated that about 24.8 crore people escaped multidimensional poverty between 2013-14 and 2022-23. Critics debate survey methods and comparability, but most estimates agree that poverty fell substantially, while vulnerability and low incomes just above the line remain widespread.

Key takeaways

  • Tendulkar's method put poverty at 21.9% in 2011-12; there was then no official estimate for a decade.
  • The World Bank estimated extreme poverty at 5.3% in 2022-23 at its new 3-dollar line.
  • NITI Aayog's MPI estimated that about 24.8 crore people escaped multidimensional poverty between 2013-14 and 2022-23.

Why it matters for UPSC: Poverty measurement methods and recent estimates are standard GS III and GS II questions.
