Quantum technology and nanotechnology

Note 217 · Theme 22: Science & technology · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

Quantum technology uses the strange properties of particles at the smallest scales. Two ideas are central. Superposition means a quantum bit, or qubit, can be in a combination of 0 and 1 at the same time, unlike an ordinary bit. Entanglement links two particles so that measuring one instantly affects the other, however far apart they are. Quantum computers could solve certain problems, such as simulating molecules for drug discovery or optimising complex systems, far faster than classical computers. They also threaten today's encryption, which relies on problems that are hard for classical computers, which is why governments are developing "post-quantum" cryptography. Quantum communication uses entangled photons to create keys that reveal any eavesdropping, and quantum sensors can measure time, gravity and magnetic fields with extreme precision.

India approved the National Quantum Mission in April 2023 with an outlay of about Rs 6,003 crore for 2023-24 to 2030-31. It aims to develop quantum computers with 50–1,000 physical qubits over eight years, satellite-based secure quantum communication over 2,000 km between ground stations, inter-city quantum key distribution, and quantum sensors and materials. Four thematic hubs have been set up at leading institutions, including IISc Bengaluru and IIT Madras. The 2025 Nobel Prize in Physics was awarded for discoveries in quantum effects in electric circuits, the basis of many quantum computers.

Nanotechnology works with materials at the scale of 1 to 100 nanometres (a nanometre is a billionth of a metre). At this scale, materials behave differently: gold nanoparticles change colour, and carbon forms super-strong nanotubes and graphene. Applications include targeted drug delivery, faster electronics, water filters, stronger materials and better solar cells. India's Nano Mission was launched in 2007. Concerns include the unknown health and environmental effects of nanoparticles.

Key takeaways

  • Qubits use superposition and entanglement; quantum computers could break current encryption.
  • The National Quantum Mission (2023, about Rs 6,003 crore) targets quantum computers, communication and sensors.
  • Nanotechnology works at 1–100 nm, where materials gain new properties.

Why it matters for UPSC: Quantum technology and nanotechnology are frequent Prelims topics, and the Quantum Mission is a likely Mains question.
