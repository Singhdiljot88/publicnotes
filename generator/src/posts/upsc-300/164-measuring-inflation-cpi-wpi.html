Measuring inflation: CPI, WPI and the new 2024 series

Note 164 · Theme 17: Growth, development & inflation · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

India uses several price indices. The Wholesale Price Index (WPI), compiled by the Office of the Economic Adviser in the Ministry of Commerce, measures prices at the wholesale or producer level. Its current base year is 2011-12. It covers goods only, not services, and gives heavy weight to manufactured products. It was once India's headline inflation measure.

The Consumer Price Index (CPI), compiled by the National Statistics Office, measures the prices of a basket of goods and services bought by households. Since 2014, on the recommendation of the Urjit Patel Committee, CPI-Combined has been the main measure of inflation, and since 2016 the RBI's inflation target has been defined in terms of it. Separate CPIs for industrial workers, agricultural labourers and rural labourers, compiled by the Labour Bureau, are used to revise wages and dearness allowance.

In February 2026, MoSPI launched a new CPI series with base year 2024, replacing the 2012 base. The weights now come from the Household Consumption Expenditure Survey 2023-24, reflecting how spending patterns have changed. The weight of food and beverages fell from about 45.9% to about 36.8%, as households now spend relatively more on services, housing and other items. The basket expanded from 299 to 358 items, including more services. The new series follows the international COICOP 2018 classification, includes rural housing for the first time, uses tablets for data collection, and adds online prices from e-commerce platforms and data on airfares and streaming subscriptions. The first reading, for January 2026, showed retail inflation of 2.75%.

The GDP deflator is a broader measure covering all goods and services produced. Producer price indices, long recommended, are being developed to replace the WPI.

Key takeaways

  • The WPI covers only goods at the wholesale level; CPI-Combined is the headline measure used for the RBI's target.
  • The new CPI series (base 2024) cut the food weight from about 45.9% to 36.8% and expanded the basket to 358 items.
  • January 2026 inflation under the new series was 2.75%.

Why it matters for UPSC: Differences between CPI and WPI, and the new CPI series, are high-probability Prelims questions.

Practice question: How does lowering the weight of food in the CPI affect the conduct of monetary policy?
