The United Nations and Security Council reform

Note 151 · Theme 16: International relations & organisations · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

The United Nations was founded on 24 October 1945, when its Charter came into force; India was among the 51 founding members. It has 193 member states. Its six principal organs are the General Assembly, where every member has one vote; the Security Council, responsible for international peace and security; the Economic and Social Council; the Trusteeship Council, inactive since 1994; the International Court of Justice at The Hague; and the Secretariat, headed by the Secretary-General, currently António Guterres, whose second term ends on 31 December 2026.

The Security Council has 15 members: five permanent members with veto power (the United States, Russia, China, the United Kingdom and France) and ten non-permanent members elected for two-year terms by the General Assembly, with five elected each year. Its resolutions under Chapter VII of the Charter are binding on all members. India has served eight terms as a non-permanent member, most recently in 2021–22, and has announced its candidacy for 2028–29.

India argues that the Council reflects the world of 1945, not today's. Africa and Latin America have no permanent members, and Asia has only China. India, Brazil, Germany and Japan, known as the G4, support each other's bids for permanent seats. India is also part of the L.69 group of developing countries seeking reform. Africa's Common African Position, the Ezulwini Consensus, demands two permanent seats with veto. The Uniting for Consensus group, including Pakistan, Italy and Argentina, opposes new permanent members. Reform requires amending the Charter, which needs the approval of two-thirds of UN members and ratification by all five permanent members, so progress through the intergovernmental negotiations has been slow.

India is one of the largest contributors of troops to UN peacekeeping, having sent over 2.9 lakh personnel to more than 50 missions since the 1950s.

Key takeaways

  • The UN has 193 members; the Security Council has five veto-holding permanent members and ten elected members.
  • India has served eight terms on the Council, most recently in 2021–22.
  • The G4 seeks permanent seats, but Charter amendment requires P5 ratification.

Why it matters for UPSC: UN structure and Security Council reform are among the most frequently asked IR topics.
