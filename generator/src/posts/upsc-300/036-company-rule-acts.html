From Company to Crown: the constitutional acts

Note 036 · Theme 4: Colonial India & 1857 · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

As the Company became a territorial power, the British Parliament stepped in to regulate it through a series of laws, which also shaped India's later constitutional system.

The Regulating Act of 1773 was Parliament's first attempt at control. It made the Governor of Bengal the Governor-General of Bengal (Warren Hastings was the first), created an executive council, and set up a Supreme Court at Calcutta in 1774. Pitt's India Act of 1784 created a Board of Control in London to supervise the Company's political affairs, establishing dual control by the Company and the Crown.

The Charter Act of 1813 ended the Company's trade monopoly in India, except in tea and trade with China, and set aside one lakh rupees a year for education. The Charter Act of 1833 made the Governor-General of Bengal the Governor-General of India (Lord William Bentinck was the first), ended the Company's commercial functions entirely, and centralised law-making. The Charter Act of 1853 separated legislative and executive functions and introduced open competition for the civil services.

After the revolt of 1857, the Government of India Act 1858 ended Company rule. Power passed to the British Crown, exercised through a Secretary of State for India in the British cabinet. The Governor-General also became the Viceroy, the Crown's representative; Lord Canning was the first. Queen Victoria's proclamation of 1 November 1858 promised religious non-interference and respect for the rights of Indian princes.

Later acts, from the Indian Councils Acts to the Government of India Act 1935, gradually introduced Indian representation.

Key takeaways

  • The Regulating Act (1773) and Pitt's India Act (1784) began parliamentary control over the Company.
  • The Charter Acts of 1813, 1833 and 1853 ended the trade monopoly, centralised power and opened the civil services to competition.
  • The Government of India Act 1858 transferred power to the Crown.

Why it matters for UPSC: These acts are a standard part of both modern history and the "historical underpinnings" section of polity.

Practice question: Trace how British parliamentary control over the East India Company increased between 1773 and 1858.
