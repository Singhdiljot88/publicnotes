Biotechnology and the bioeconomy: BioE3 policy

Note 216 · Theme 22: Science & technology · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

Biotechnology uses living organisms or their parts to make products and solve problems. It is often grouped by colour: red biotechnology for health (vaccines, diagnostics, biologic drugs), green for agriculture (improved seeds, biofertilisers), white for industry (enzymes, biofuels, biodegradable materials) and blue for marine resources.

India's bioeconomy, the value of goods and services produced using biotechnology, was estimated by the Biotechnology Industry Research Assistance Council (BIRAC) at about 165 billion US dollars in 2024, and the government aims for 300 billion dollars by 2030. The sector has grown with India's strength in vaccines and generic biologic medicines (biosimilars), and the number of biotech start-ups has risen sharply. The Department of Biotechnology (1986) and BIRAC (2012) fund research and start-ups.

In August 2024 the Union Cabinet approved the BioE3 policy, short for Biotechnology for Economy, Environment and Employment. It aims to promote high-performance biomanufacturing, using engineered microbes and cells to produce chemicals, materials, food and medicines in place of processes based on fossil fuels. Its focus areas include bio-based chemicals and enzymes, smart proteins and functional foods, precision biotherapeutics, climate-resilient agriculture, carbon capture, and marine and space research. It plans biomanufacturing hubs, bio-foundries and Bio-AI hubs that use artificial intelligence in biology.

Other initiatives include the National Biopharma Mission, the Biotech-KISAN programme to connect scientists with farmers, and support for ethanol blending in petrol, which reached the target of 20% (E20) in 2025.

Challenges include low private investment in research, regulatory delays, a shortage of manufacturing facilities for advanced products and limited links between universities and industry.

Key takeaways

  • Biotechnology is grouped as red (health), green (agriculture), white (industry) and blue (marine).
  • India's bioeconomy was about 165 billion dollars in 2024, with a target of 300 billion by 2030.
  • The BioE3 policy (August 2024) promotes biomanufacturing to replace fossil-based processes.

Why it matters for UPSC: Biotechnology policy and the bioeconomy are emerging GS III topics.

Practice question: How can biomanufacturing help India's economy and environment at the same time?
