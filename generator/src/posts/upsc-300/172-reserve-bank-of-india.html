The Reserve Bank of India: roles and independence

Note 172 · Theme 18: Money, banking & markets · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

The Reserve Bank of India was established on 1 April 1935 under the Reserve Bank of India Act, 1934, following the recommendations of the Hilton Young Commission. It began as a privately owned bank and was nationalised in 1949. Its headquarters moved from Calcutta to Mumbai in 1937. It is governed by a central board of directors headed by the Governor; the present Governor is Sanjay Malhotra, who took office in December 2024.

The RBI has many functions. It is the monetary authority, responsible for keeping prices stable while keeping growth in mind. It is the issuer of currency notes. It is the banker to the government, managing the accounts of the Union and state governments and their borrowing programmes. It is the bankers' bank, holding banks' reserves and acting as lender of last resort. It regulates and supervises banks, non-banking financial companies and payment systems, under laws such as the Banking Regulation Act, 1949 and the Payment and Settlement Systems Act, 2007. It manages foreign exchange reserves and the exchange rate under the Foreign Exchange Management Act, 1999. It also has developmental roles, such as promoting financial inclusion and directing credit to priority sectors.

The RBI's independence has been tested. In 2018, tensions with the government over its surplus reserves, lending restrictions on weak banks and liquidity for non-bank lenders led to reports that the government might use Section 7 of the RBI Act, which allows it to give directions to the Bank, and Governor Urjit Patel resigned. The Bimal Jalan committee (2019) then set a framework for transferring the RBI's surplus to the government. The RBI has since paid large annual dividends to the government, a significant source of non-tax revenue.

Most economists argue that an independent central bank produces lower inflation and greater credibility, while accepting that it must be accountable to elected governments.

Key takeaways

  • The RBI was set up in 1935 and nationalised in 1949; Sanjay Malhotra has been Governor since December 2024.
  • It is monetary authority, currency issuer, banker to the government and banks, regulator and forex manager.
  • The 2018 dispute and the Jalan committee framework highlighted questions of central bank independence.

Why it matters for UPSC: The RBI's functions and independence are standard Prelims and Mains topics.
