The Manipur conflict

Note 264 · Theme 27: Internal security · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

Manipur's population includes the Meiteis, mostly Hindu and concentrated in the Imphal valley, which makes up about a tenth of the state's area, and tribal communities, mainly the Kuki-Zo and Naga groups, who are largely Christian and live in the surrounding hills. Land laws restrict non-tribals from buying land in the hills, while tribal people can live in the valley, a sore point for many Meiteis.

Violence broke out on 3 May 2023, after a tribal solidarity march protesting a Manipur High Court direction to the state to consider recommending Scheduled Tribe status for the Meiteis. Clashes quickly spread. Homes, churches and temples were burnt, weapons were looted from police armouries, and the state was effectively divided into Meitei and Kuki-Zo zones separated by buffer zones manned by central forces. A video of two Kuki women being paraded naked, which surfaced in July 2023, caused national outrage, and the Supreme Court set up a committee of former High Court judges to oversee relief and rehabilitation. By official figures given to the state assembly, more than 250 people were killed, and the state government said in 2026 that over 58,000 people had been displaced since May 2023.

The causes were complex: competition for land and jobs, the Scheduled Tribe demand, a government campaign against poppy cultivation and "illegal immigrants" that tribal groups felt targeted them, refugees from the Myanmar civil war, armed groups on both sides, and deep mistrust. Kuki-Zo groups demanded a separate administration.

Chief Minister N. Biren Singh resigned in February 2025, and President's rule was imposed on 13 February 2025. It was revoked on 4 February 2026, when Yumnam Khemchand Singh was sworn in as chief minister. Rebuilding trust between communities, disarming militias, returning displaced people and addressing land and identity questions remain major tasks.

Key takeaways

  • Violence between Meiteis and Kuki-Zo communities began on 3 May 2023 over the ST status issue and deeper land and identity disputes.
  • Over 250 people were killed by official figures, and over 58,000 displaced.
  • President's rule (February 2025) ended in February 2026 with a new chief minister.

Why it matters for UPSC: Manipur is a major recent internal security and governance issue, likely to appear in Mains.
