The Vedic age: from the Rigveda to the later Vedic period

Note 003 · Theme 1: Ancient India · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

The Vedic period is named after the Vedas, a body of Sanskrit texts first passed down orally. The Rigveda, the oldest, is usually dated to about 1500–1000 BCE and contains 1,028 hymns arranged in ten books called mandalas. The other Vedas are the Samaveda (melodies for chanting), the Yajurveda (sacrificial formulas) and the Atharvaveda (spells and everyday concerns). Each Veda has attached texts: Brahmanas, Aranyakas and Upanishads.

Early Vedic society, centred in the land of the seven rivers in the north-west, was pastoral. Cattle were wealth, and tribes (jana) were led by a chief (rajan) advised by assemblies such as the sabha and samiti. Gods such as Indra, Agni and Varuna were praised in the hymns. The Rigveda mentions the Battle of the Ten Kings, fought on the Parushni (Ravi) river.

In the later Vedic period, roughly 1000–600 BCE, settlement moved east into the Ganga plain. Iron came into use, agriculture expanded, and the archaeological culture known as Painted Grey Ware spread. Kingdoms grew larger, rituals such as the Rajasuya and Ashvamedha displayed royal power, and the varna order became more rigid. Women's participation in assemblies appears to have declined.

The Upanishads, composed late in this period, turned from ritual to philosophy, asking about the self (atman) and ultimate reality (brahman). Their ideas shaped later Indian thought, including the debates of Buddhism and Jainism.

Key takeaways

  • The Rigveda has 1,028 hymns in ten mandalas and is the oldest Veda.
  • Society moved from pastoral tribes to agrarian kingdoms in the Ganga plain.
  • The Upanishads shifted attention from ritual to philosophy.

Why it matters for UPSC: Questions on Vedic texts, assemblies and the early-to-later Vedic transition appear often in Prelims.

Practice question: Trace the changes in political and social life between the early and later Vedic periods.
