Rocks, weathering and landforms

Note 084 · Theme 9: Physical geography · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

Rocks are grouped into three types by how they form. Igneous rocks form from cooled magma or lava: granite cools slowly underground and has large crystals, while basalt cools quickly at the surface. Sedimentary rocks form from layers of sediment compressed over time: sandstone, shale and limestone are examples, and they often contain fossils. Coal is a sedimentary rock formed from plant material. Metamorphic rocks form when existing rocks are changed by heat and pressure: limestone becomes marble, shale becomes slate, and granite becomes gneiss. Over time, rocks move between these types in the rock cycle.

Weathering breaks rocks down in place. Mechanical weathering, such as frost wedging or the expansion and contraction caused by daily temperature changes in deserts, breaks rocks physically. Chemical weathering, such as the dissolving of limestone by slightly acidic rainwater, changes their composition. Erosion then removes and transports the material through running water, glaciers, wind and waves.

Each agent creates characteristic landforms. Rivers carve V-shaped valleys, gorges and waterfalls in their upper course, form meanders and oxbow lakes in their middle and lower course, and build floodplains and deltas near the sea. Glaciers carve U-shaped valleys, cirques and fjords, and leave moraines. Wind in deserts shapes sand dunes, including crescent-shaped barchans, and mushroom rocks. Waves shape cliffs, sea caves, arches and stacks, and build beaches and spits. In limestone areas, chemical weathering creates karst landforms such as sinkholes, caves, stalactites and stalagmites, as seen in the Borra caves of Andhra Pradesh and the caves of Meghalaya.

Key takeaways

  • Igneous, sedimentary and metamorphic rocks are linked through the rock cycle.
  • Weathering breaks rocks in place; erosion transports material.
  • Rivers, glaciers, wind, waves and solution each create distinctive landforms.

Why it matters for UPSC: Rock types and landforms are frequent Prelims questions, often with Indian examples.

Practice question: Describe the landforms created by a river from its source to its mouth.
