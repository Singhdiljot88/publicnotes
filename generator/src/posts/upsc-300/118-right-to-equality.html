The right to equality and reservations

Note 118 · Theme 12: Constitutional framework · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

Articles 14 to 18 make up the right to equality. Article 14 guarantees equality before the law and the equal protection of the laws. The first idea, from Britain, means no one is above the law; the second, from the United States, means equals should be treated equally, which permits reasonable classification. Courts test classifications by asking whether they are based on an intelligible difference with a rational link to the law's purpose, and, since the E. P. Royappa case (1974), whether the action is arbitrary.

Article 15 prohibits discrimination by the state on grounds of religion, race, caste, sex or place of birth, but allows special provisions for women and children (15(3)), for socially and educationally backward classes, SCs and STs (15(4), added by the First Amendment in 1951 after the Champakam Dorairajan case), for their admission to private educational institutions (15(5), 2005), and for economically weaker sections (15(6), 2019). Article 16 guarantees equality of opportunity in public employment, with similar exceptions, including reservation in promotions for SCs and STs under 16(4A). Article 17 abolishes untouchability, Article 18 abolishes titles other than military and academic distinctions; national awards such as the Bharat Ratna were held not to be titles in the Balaji Raghavan case (1995).

Reservations have been shaped by the courts. In Indra Sawhney (1992), the Supreme Court upheld 27% reservation for OBCs, excluded the creamy layer, and set a general ceiling of 50%. The 103rd Amendment (2019) introduced up to 10% reservation for economically weaker sections among those not covered by other reservations. In Janhit Abhiyan (2022), a five-judge bench upheld it by 3–2, accepting that economic criteria alone can justify reservation and that the 50% ceiling is not inviolable. In 2024, a seven-judge bench allowed states to sub-classify Scheduled Castes to give priority to the most disadvantaged among them.

Key takeaways

  • Article 14 permits reasonable classification but forbids arbitrariness.
  • Articles 15 and 16 allow reservations; the 50% ceiling from Indra Sawhney was relaxed for EWS in Janhit Abhiyan (2022).
  • In 2024, the Supreme Court allowed sub-classification of Scheduled Castes.

Why it matters for UPSC: Equality articles and reservation judgments are among the most important topics in GS II polity.

Practice question: Does reservation on economic criteria alone fit the original idea of reservations in the Constitution? Discuss.
