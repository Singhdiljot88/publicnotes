India's climate targets and net zero

Note 254 · Theme 26: Climate change & disaster management · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

India's first Nationally Determined Contribution, submitted in 2015, pledged to reduce the emissions intensity of its GDP by 33–35% from 2005 levels by 2030, to reach 40% of installed electricity capacity from non-fossil sources by 2030, and to create an additional carbon sink of 2.5–3 billion tonnes of CO2 equivalent through forests and trees.

At COP26 in Glasgow in 2021, Prime Minister Modi announced the "Panchamrit" (five nectar elements), including a target of net-zero emissions by 2070. India's updated NDC of 2022 raised the targets to a 45% reduction in emissions intensity by 2030 from 2005 levels, and 50% of installed electricity capacity from non-fossil sources by 2030. India reached the 50% non-fossil capacity mark in 2025, five years early, though fossil fuels still generate most of its electricity because solar and wind produce power only part of the time. India also submitted a long-term low-emission development strategy in 2022.

In March 2026 the Union Cabinet approved India's next NDC for 2031–2035. It commits to reducing emissions intensity of GDP by 47% from 2005 levels by 2035, raising non-fossil sources to 60% of installed electricity capacity by 2035, and creating an additional carbon sink of 3.5–4 billion tonnes of CO2 equivalent.

India's position rests on equity. Its per capita emissions are well below the world average and a fraction of those of the United States, and its cumulative historical emissions are small relative to its population. India argues that it needs room for development, and that developed countries must cut faster and provide finance and technology. At the same time, India is the world's third-largest emitter in total, so its choices on coal, transport and industry matter globally. India also promotes Mission LiFE (Lifestyle for Environment), launched in 2022, which encourages sustainable consumption by individuals.

Key takeaways

  • India's 2022 NDC targeted a 45% cut in emissions intensity and 50% non-fossil capacity by 2030; the capacity target was met in 2025.
  • India aims for net zero by 2070.
  • The 2035 NDC approved in March 2026 targets a 47% intensity cut and 60% non-fossil capacity.

Why it matters for UPSC: India's climate targets and their updates are among the most likely environment questions in both stages.

Practice question: How does India balance its development needs with its climate commitments? Evaluate its NDC targets.
