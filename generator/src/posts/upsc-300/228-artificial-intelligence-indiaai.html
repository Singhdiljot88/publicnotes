Artificial intelligence and the IndiaAI Mission

Note 228 · Theme 23: Space, defence technology & digital India · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

Artificial intelligence (AI) refers to computer systems that perform tasks normally requiring human intelligence, such as recognising images, understanding language and making predictions. Machine learning, in which systems learn patterns from data, drives most modern AI. Generative AI, such as large language models, can produce text, images and code. These systems require huge amounts of data and computing power, especially graphics processing units (GPUs).

The IndiaAI Mission, approved in March 2024 with an outlay of about Rs 10,372 crore over five years, aims to build an AI ecosystem. Its pillars include a large computing infrastructure, with tens of thousands of GPUs made available to researchers and start-ups at subsidised rates; IndiaAI datasets (AIKosh); support for developing Indian foundation models, including in Indian languages; skilling; start-up financing; and safe and trusted AI. Bhashini, a government platform, provides AI translation across Indian languages.

India's approach to AI regulation is "light-touch". In November 2025, the Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology released the India AI Governance Guidelines, which favour principles, voluntary measures and existing laws over a new standalone AI law, while proposing institutions such as an AI Safety Institute and an AI governance group to coordinate policy. India hosted the AI Impact Summit in New Delhi in February 2026, after earlier summits in the UK, South Korea and France.

AI offers large benefits in health diagnostics, agriculture advisories, education, weather forecasting and public services. But it raises serious concerns: job losses in routine work, including the IT services and business process sectors that employ millions of Indians; bias in algorithms; deepfakes and misinformation; privacy; copyright disputes over training data; concentration of power in a few global companies; and the energy and water demands of data centres.

Key takeaways

  • AI needs large datasets and computing power, especially GPUs.
  • The IndiaAI Mission (2024, about Rs 10,372 crore) funds compute, datasets, foundation models and skills.
  • India's November 2025 AI Governance Guidelines take a light-touch approach; India hosted the AI Impact Summit in 2026.

Why it matters for UPSC: AI policy, regulation and its social impact are among the most important current GS III topics.

Practice question: Should India adopt a dedicated law to regulate artificial intelligence? Discuss.
