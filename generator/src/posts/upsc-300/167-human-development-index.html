Human development and the HDI

Note 167 · Theme 17: Growth, development & inflation · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

The idea of human development, associated with the economists Mahbub ul Haq and Amartya Sen, holds that development is about expanding people's capabilities and freedoms, the real opportunities they have to live lives they value, rather than about income alone. Sen's book Development as Freedom (1999) set out this capability approach.

The United Nations Development Programme (UNDP) has published the Human Development Report since 1990. Its Human Development Index (HDI) combines three dimensions: a long and healthy life (life expectancy at birth), knowledge (expected and mean years of schooling) and a decent standard of living (gross national income per capita at purchasing power parity). Each dimension is turned into an index between 0 and 1, and the HDI is their geometric mean.

According to the Human Development Report 2025, India ranked 130th of 193 countries, with an HDI value of 0.685 for 2023, placing it in the medium human development category, close to the threshold for high human development. India's HDI has risen by more than half since 1990, driven by rising life expectancy, schooling and incomes. The report highlighted that inequality and gender gaps pull India's score down.

UNDP publishes related indices. The Inequality-adjusted HDI discounts the HDI for inequality in each dimension. The Gender Development Index compares HDI values for women and men, and the Gender Inequality Index measures gaps in reproductive health, empowerment and labour market participation. The global Multidimensional Poverty Index, developed with Oxford University, counts overlapping deprivations.

In India, NITI Aayog's SDG India Index ranks states on the Sustainable Development Goals, and states such as Kerala consistently show that high human development is possible at moderate income levels, an idea often called the Kerala model.

Key takeaways

  • Human development focuses on capabilities and freedoms, following Sen and ul Haq.
  • The HDI combines life expectancy, schooling and income per head.
  • India ranked 130th of 193 in the HDR 2025, with an HDI of 0.685 for 2023.

Why it matters for UPSC: The HDI's components and India's rank are common Prelims questions; the capability approach is useful in essays and GS IV.

Practice question: Why does India's HDI rank lag behind its GDP rank? What policies could close the gap?
