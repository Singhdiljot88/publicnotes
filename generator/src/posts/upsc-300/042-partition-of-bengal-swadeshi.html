Partition of Bengal and the Swadeshi movement

Note 042 · Theme 5: The freedom struggle · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

In 1905, Viceroy Lord Curzon partitioned Bengal, officially for administrative convenience, as the province was very large. Nationalists saw it as an attempt to divide Bengali Hindus and Muslims and weaken the centre of Indian nationalism. The partition took effect on 16 October 1905, observed as a day of mourning; Rabindranath Tagore asked people to tie rakhis to each other as a symbol of unity.

The Swadeshi movement that followed used new methods: boycott of British goods, especially cloth, public burning of foreign goods, promotion of Indian-made products, national schools and colleges, and self-help organisations called samitis. Indian enterprises grew, including the Bengal Chemicals factory of P. C. Ray. Songs such as Tagore's Amar Sonar Bangla, now Bangladesh's national anthem, spread the message.

The movement brought the extremists, or assertive nationalists, to the fore: Bal Gangadhar Tilak, Bipin Chandra Pal, Lala Lajpat Rai (together known as Lal-Bal-Pal) and Aurobindo Ghosh. They demanded swaraj and mass action rather than petitions. Tilak's slogan "Swaraj is my birthright and I shall have it" became famous. Differences between moderates and extremists led to a split at the Surat session of the Congress in 1907.

The government responded with repression: Tilak was sentenced to six years in Mandalay in 1908. Under continued pressure, the partition was annulled in 1911, and the capital was moved from Calcutta to Delhi. The movement had limits, as it reached few peasants and Muslims, but it introduced methods that Gandhi later used on a much larger scale.

Key takeaways

  • Curzon's 1905 partition of Bengal triggered the Swadeshi and boycott movement.
  • The Congress split into moderates and extremists at Surat in 1907.
  • The partition was annulled in 1911, and the capital moved to Delhi.

Why it matters for UPSC: The Swadeshi movement's methods and the Surat split are standard questions in modern history.

Practice question: How did the Swadeshi movement anticipate the methods of Gandhian mass movements?
