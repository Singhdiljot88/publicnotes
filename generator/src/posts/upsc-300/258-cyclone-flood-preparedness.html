Cyclones and floods: what preparedness looks like

Note 258 · Theme 26: Climate change & disaster management · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

Odisha's experience shows how preparedness saves lives. The super cyclone of October 1999 killed about 10,000 people. Odisha then created the Odisha State Disaster Management Authority, built hundreds of multipurpose cyclone shelters, trained community volunteers and improved early warning. When Cyclone Phailin, of similar strength, struck in October 2013, around a million people were evacuated and the death toll was under 50. Cyclone Fani in 2019 saw a similar large-scale evacuation. The UN praised Odisha's approach as a model.

Several factors made the difference. The India Meteorological Department's cyclone forecasts have become far more accurate, with track errors falling sharply over two decades. Warnings now reach people through mobile alerts, sirens, radio and local volunteers. The National Cyclone Risk Mitigation Project built shelters, embankments and evacuation roads in coastal states. Clear protocols assign responsibilities to officials before a storm arrives.

Floods are a more persistent problem. Assam, Bihar, Uttar Pradesh and West Bengal face floods almost every year from the Brahmaputra and Ganga systems, and floods cause the largest share of disaster-related economic losses in India. Responses include embankments, which can fail or shift flooding elsewhere; flood forecasting by the Central Water Commission; floodplain zoning, which few states have enforced; and dam operations, which can worsen floods if reservoirs release water suddenly, as some argue happened in Kerala in 2018.

Urban floods are growing, as seen in Mumbai (2005), Chennai (2015 and 2023), Bengaluru and Delhi. They are caused by intense rain, encroached lakes and drains, paved surfaces that stop water soaking in, and poor drainage planning. The NDMA has issued guidelines on urban flooding, and the 2025 amendment to the Disaster Management Act provides for urban disaster management authorities.

Key takeaways

  • Odisha cut cyclone deaths from about 10,000 (1999) to under 50 (Phailin, 2013) through shelters, warnings and evacuation.
  • Riverine floods in Assam, Bihar and the Ganga plain cause the largest disaster losses.
  • Urban floods result from encroachment, paving and poor drainage.

Why it matters for UPSC: Cyclone and flood management, especially the Odisha model, are popular Mains examples.

Practice question: What lessons can other states learn from Odisha's cyclone preparedness? Can they be applied to urban floods?
