The planning era: five-year plans and the mixed economy

Note 053 · Theme 6: India after 1947 · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

After independence, India chose a mixed economy, with a large public sector alongside private enterprise, and state planning to guide investment. The Planning Commission was set up by a Cabinet resolution in March 1950, chaired by the Prime Minister. It was not a constitutional or statutory body. The National Development Council, formed in 1952, brought chief ministers into the planning process.

The First Five-Year Plan (1951–56), based on the Harrod-Domar model, focused on agriculture and irrigation, and broadly met its targets. The Second Plan (1956–61), designed by the statistician P. C. Mahalanobis, emphasised rapid industrialisation through heavy industry in the public sector, such as steel plants at Bhilai, Rourkela and Durgapur. The Industrial Policy Resolution of 1956 reserved key industries for the state.

The Third Plan (1961–66) was disrupted by the 1962 war with China, the 1965 war with Pakistan and droughts, leading to a "plan holiday" of three annual plans (1966–69). Later plans focused on poverty (the Fifth Plan's "garibi hatao"), employment and, from the Eighth Plan (1992–97), liberalisation. Plans became increasingly indicative rather than directive. The Twelfth Plan (2012–17) was the last.

On 1 January 2015, the Planning Commission was replaced by the NITI Aayog (National Institution for Transforming India), a think tank meant to promote cooperative federalism. Unlike the Planning Commission, it does not allocate funds to states. Supporters say this fits a market economy; critics say it removed a forum for states to bargain for resources.

Key takeaways

  • The Planning Commission (1950) was set up by Cabinet resolution, not the Constitution.
  • The Mahalanobis model of the Second Plan prioritised heavy industry.
  • NITI Aayog replaced the Planning Commission on 1 January 2015 and does not allocate funds.

Why it matters for UPSC: The history of planning and the shift to NITI Aayog appear in both GS I and GS III questions.

Practice question: Compare the roles of the Planning Commission and NITI Aayog in India's federal system.
