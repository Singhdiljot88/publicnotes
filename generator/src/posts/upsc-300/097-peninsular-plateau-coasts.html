The Peninsular Plateau, coasts and islands

Note 097 · Theme 10: Geography of India · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

The Peninsular Plateau is the oldest landmass in India, made of ancient crystalline, igneous and metamorphic rocks that were part of Gondwana. It is roughly triangular, bounded by the Aravallis in the north-west, the Rajmahal hills in the east, the Western Ghats in the west and the Eastern Ghats in the east. The Narmada, flowing west through a rift valley, divides it into the Central Highlands to the north and the Deccan plateau to the south.

The Central Highlands include the Malwa plateau, Bundelkhand, Baghelkhand and the Chotanagpur plateau, which is rich in coal, iron ore and other minerals. The Aravallis are among the oldest fold mountains in the world, heavily eroded; Guru Shikhar on Mount Abu is their highest point. The Deccan plateau rises from east to west. The Western Ghats (Sahyadri) are continuous and higher, with Anamudi (2,695 m) in Kerala as the highest peak of peninsular India. They are a UNESCO World Heritage Site and a biodiversity hotspot. The Eastern Ghats are lower and broken by rivers; their highest point is in Andhra Pradesh, given as Jindhagada or Arma Konda depending on the source.

India's western coastal plain is narrow and divided into the Konkan (Maharashtra and Goa), the Kanara (Karnataka) and the Malabar coast (Kerala), with lagoons called kayals or backwaters. The eastern coastal plain is wider, with the deltas of the Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna and Kaveri and the Coromandel coast in Tamil Nadu. Lakes such as Chilika in Odisha (the largest brackish water lagoon in India) and Pulicat lie along it.

India has over 1,300 islands. The Andaman and Nicobar Islands are separated by the Ten Degree Channel, and Lakshadweep from the Maldives by the Eight Degree Channel.

Key takeaways

  • The Peninsular Plateau is India's oldest landmass and is rich in minerals.
  • Anamudi (2,695 m) is the highest peak in peninsular India; the Western Ghats are a UNESCO site.
  • The western coast is narrow; the eastern coast is wide with large deltas and lagoons such as Chilika.

Why it matters for UPSC: Plateau regions, ghats, coasts and island channels are high-frequency Prelims topics.

Practice question: Compare the Western and Eastern Ghats in their origin, structure and ecological importance.
