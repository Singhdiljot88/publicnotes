MSMEs: the backbone of Indian industry

Note 202 · Theme 21: Industry, infrastructure & energy · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

Micro, small and medium enterprises (MSMEs) are central to India's economy. They contribute roughly 30% of GDP, around 45% of exports and employ more than 20 crore people, making them the largest source of jobs after agriculture. Most are tiny, informal and family-run.

The Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises Development Act, 2006 defines them. In 2020 the definition was changed to use both investment in plant and machinery and annual turnover, removing the old distinction between manufacturing and services. The Union Budget 2025-26 raised the limits from April 2025: micro enterprises now have investment up to Rs 2.5 crore and turnover up to Rs 10 crore; small enterprises up to Rs 25 crore and Rs 100 crore; and medium enterprises up to Rs 125 crore and Rs 500 crore. Firms register online on the Udyam portal, and informal micro units can register through the Udyam Assist platform.

MSMEs face well-known problems: lack of affordable credit and dependence on informal lenders, delayed payments from large buyers and governments, outdated technology, poor access to markets, and the burden of compliance. Many prefer to stay small to avoid labour and tax rules, a problem economists call "dwarfism" or the "missing middle".

Government support includes the Credit Guarantee Fund Trust for Micro and Small Enterprises (CGTMSE), which guarantees collateral-free loans; the Emergency Credit Line Guarantee Scheme during the pandemic; the MUDRA loans under the Pradhan Mantri Mudra Yojana; the Trade Receivables Discounting System (TReDS) to get faster payment on invoices; a rule under the Income Tax Act, from 2024, that payments to micro and small suppliers must be made within 45 days to be deductible; and public procurement rules requiring government buyers to source 25% of purchases from MSEs.

Key takeaways

  • MSMEs contribute about 30% of GDP and 45% of exports and are the second-largest employer.
  • The 2025 definitions raised investment and turnover limits (micro: Rs 2.5 crore and Rs 10 crore).
  • Credit, delayed payments and the "missing middle" are key problems; CGTMSE, MUDRA and TReDS help.

Why it matters for UPSC: MSMEs are a perennial GS III topic, linked to employment, credit and exports.

Practice question: Why do so many Indian firms remain small? Suggest policy measures to help MSMEs grow.
