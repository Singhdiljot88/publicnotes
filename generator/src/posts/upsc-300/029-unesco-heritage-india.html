India's UNESCO World Heritage and intangible heritage

Note 029 · Theme 3: Art & culture · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

UNESCO's World Heritage List, created under the 1972 World Heritage Convention, recognises places of outstanding universal value. India's first sites were inscribed in 1983: the Ajanta caves, Ellora caves, Agra Fort and Taj Mahal. With the addition of Sarnath in July 2026, India has 45 World Heritage Sites, among the highest totals of any country. Most are cultural sites; natural sites include Kaziranga, Manas, Keoladeo, the Sundarbans, Nanda Devi and Valley of Flowers, the Western Ghats and the Great Himalayan National Park, while Khangchendzonga National Park is a mixed site.

Recent additions show the range of Indian heritage. Dholavira and the Ramappa temple in Telangana were added in 2021. Santiniketan and the Hoysala temples were added in 2023. The Moidams of the Ahom dynasty were added in 2024, and the Maratha Military Landscapes, a group of twelve forts, in 2025. India hosted the World Heritage Committee's 46th session in New Delhi in July 2024.

UNESCO separately keeps lists of intangible cultural heritage: living traditions, performing arts, rituals and crafts. Indian elements include Vedic chanting, Ramlila, Kutiyattam, Chhau, yoga, the Kumbh Mela, Durga Puja in Kolkata, Garba of Gujarat and, from December 2025, Deepavali.

A listing does not transfer ownership or control to UNESCO. The state remains responsible for protection and must report on the site's condition. Sites can be placed on the List of World Heritage in Danger if they are threatened.

Key takeaways

  • India has 45 World Heritage Sites after Sarnath's inscription in July 2026.
  • Recent additions include Dholavira, Ramappa, Santiniketan, the Hoysala temples, the Moidams and the Maratha forts.
  • Deepavali joined UNESCO's intangible heritage list in December 2025.

Why it matters for UPSC: Heritage sites, their states and years of inscription are among the most-asked culture questions in Prelims.

Practice question: Does UNESCO recognition help or harm a heritage site? Discuss with reference to tourism and local communities.
