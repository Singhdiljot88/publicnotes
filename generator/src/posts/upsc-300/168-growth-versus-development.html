Economic growth versus development, and the SDGs

Note 168 · Theme 17: Growth, development & inflation · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

Economic growth is an increase in a country's output, usually measured by real GDP growth. Economic development is broader: it includes improvements in health, education, living standards, equality, freedom and environmental quality. Growth can happen without much development, for example if its benefits are concentrated among a few or if it damages the environment. Development usually needs growth, because growth provides resources for public services and jobs.

Several ideas qualify the growth-first view. The trickle-down theory claimed that growth would automatically benefit the poor; evidence shows that this depends on the kind of growth and on public policy. Inclusive growth, a phrase used in India's Eleventh Five-Year Plan (2007-12), means growth that is broad-based across sectors, regions and social groups and creates productive jobs. Sustainable development, defined by the Brundtland Commission in 1987 as development that meets the needs of the present without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs, adds the environment. Green GDP adjusts output for environmental damage and resource depletion.

The United Nations adopted the 2030 Agenda for Sustainable Development in 2015, with 17 Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) and 169 targets. They range from ending poverty and hunger to quality education, gender equality, clean energy, decent work, reduced inequality, climate action and peace. Unlike the earlier Millennium Development Goals (2000-2015), they apply to all countries, not only developing ones.

In India, NITI Aayog coordinates the SDGs and publishes the SDG India Index, which ranks states and union territories. Kerala and Uttarakhand have topped recent editions, while states such as Bihar have ranked lowest, though most states have improved. India presents Voluntary National Reviews of its progress at the United Nations.

Key takeaways

  • Growth is rising output; development includes health, education, equality and environment.
  • Inclusive growth and sustainable development broaden the goal beyond GDP.
  • The 17 SDGs (2015–2030) apply to all countries; NITI Aayog ranks Indian states on them.

Why it matters for UPSC: The growth-development distinction and the SDGs are common in Prelims and Mains, including the essay paper.

Practice question: "Growth is necessary but not sufficient for development." Discuss with reference to India.
