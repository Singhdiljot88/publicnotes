The India–New Zealand free trade agreement

Note 027 · Theme 4: Trade deals & tariffs · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

India and New Zealand signed a free trade agreement on 27 April 2026 at Bharat Mandapam in New Delhi. Commerce Minister Piyush Goyal and New Zealand's Trade Minister Todd McClay signed it. Negotiations, relaunched in March 2025, were concluded in December 2025 in about nine months, one of the fastest trade negotiations India has completed.

Under the agreement, tariffs on 95% of New Zealand's exports to India will be cut or removed, including seafood, iron, steel, scrap aluminium and wool, with more than half becoming duty-free immediately and wine tariffs falling over ten years. Indian textiles, leather, pharmaceuticals, engineering goods and automobiles are expected to gain duty-free access to New Zealand. New Zealand will open market access in 118 services sectors and offers a quota of 5,000 temporary employment visas for Indian professionals, 1,000 working holiday visas and easier post-study work rights for Indian students. Goyal said New Zealand had committed to investment of 20 billion dollars, a claim reported by ThePrint but not independently detailed.

Dairy was the most sensitive issue, since it is New Zealand's largest export sector and India protects its millions of small dairy farmers. India kept dairy, along with coffee, sugar, spices, edible oils and rubber, largely out of its commitments. It granted limited access: dairy ingredients imported for re-export become duty-free, and some high-value products such as bulk infant formula are phased in over seven years. New Zealand's dairy industry expressed disappointment.

The deal still needs New Zealand's parliamentary approval. Prime Minister Modi visited New Zealand in July 2026 as part of a tour that also took him to Indonesia and Australia.

Key takeaways

  • India and New Zealand signed an FTA on 27 April 2026 after talks concluded in December 2025.
  • Tariffs on 95% of New Zealand's exports to India are cut; India keeps dairy largely protected.
  • Indian professionals and students get new visa quotas and work rights.

Why it matters for UPSC: The deal shows how India protects agriculture, especially dairy, while opening trade, a recurring GS III theme.

Discussion question: Why is dairy the most sensitive sector in India's trade negotiations? Discuss with reference to the India–New Zealand FTA.
