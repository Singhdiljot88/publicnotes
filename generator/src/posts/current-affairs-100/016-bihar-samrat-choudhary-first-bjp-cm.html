Bihar: Nitish Kumar steps down and Samrat Choudhary becomes chief minister

Note 016 · Theme 2: Elections & states · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

On 5 March 2026, Nitish Kumar, Bihar's longest-serving chief minister, announced that he would move to the Rajya Sabha. He was elected to the Upper House in the March biennial polls and resigned as chief minister on 14 April 2026. The NDA had won the Bihar assembly election in November 2025 and he had been sworn in again after it.

On 15 April 2026, Samrat Choudhary of the BJP was sworn in as chief minister, making him the first BJP chief minister of Bihar. Reports named Brijendra Prasad Yadav and Vijay Kumar Chaudhary, both of the Janata Dal (United), as deputy chief ministers. The arrangement reverses the long-standing pattern in which the BJP was the junior partner in the Bihar NDA and the JD(U) held the top post.

The change happened without an election. Under Article 164, the chief minister is appointed by the governor and holds office during the governor's pleasure, which in practice means as long as he or she has the confidence of the assembly. When a chief minister resigns, the whole council of ministers goes with him or her, and the governor invites the person elected leader by the majority to form a new government.

Taken with West Bengal, it means the BJP had chief ministers in two big eastern states for the first time in 2026.

Key takeaways

  • Nitish Kumar resigned as Bihar chief minister on 14 April 2026 after his election to the Rajya Sabha.
  • Samrat Choudhary became Bihar's first BJP chief minister on 15 April 2026.
  • The JD(U) took the deputy chief minister posts in the new arrangement.

Why it matters for UPSC: It shows how a change of chief minister mid-term works under Article 164, and a major power shift within a coalition.
