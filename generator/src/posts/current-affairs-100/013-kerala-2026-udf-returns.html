Kerala 2026: the UDF returns after a decade

Note 013 · Theme 2: Elections & states · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

Kerala kept its habit of voting out the incumbent in 2026, after breaking it in 2021. The Congress-led United Democratic Front (UDF) defeated the Left Democratic Front (LDF), which had governed for ten years under Pinarayi Vijayan. The LDF was reduced to 35 of the 140 seats. Within the UDF, the Congress won 63 seats and the IUML 22. On the Left, the CPI(M) won 26 and the CPI 8. Reports differ slightly on the UDF's exact total, but all put it at about a hundred seats, a large majority.

The BJP won three seats: Rajeev Chandrasekhar in Nemom, V. Muraleedharan in Kazhakoottam and B. B. Gopakumar in Chathamangalam, with about 11.4% of the vote. This is its best result in the state; its only previous assembly win was Nemom in 2016.

The Congress legislature party chose V. D. Satheesan, the Leader of the Opposition in the outgoing assembly, as its leader on 14 May. He was sworn in as chief minister on 18 May 2026, with 20 ministers taking oath at the same time, so the full cabinet was in place from day one.

The loss also left the Left without a government in any Indian state for the first time in decades, since Kerala was the last state it ruled. This makes the result significant beyond Kerala, for the future of the Left parties in national politics.

Key takeaways

  • The UDF won about a hundred of 140 seats; the LDF fell to 35.
  • V. D. Satheesan was sworn in as chief minister on 18 May 2026 with a full cabinet of 20.
  • The BJP won three seats, its best ever in Kerala.

Why it matters for UPSC: Kerala's result ends the Left's last state government and tests the anti-incumbency pattern in Indian state politics.

Discussion question: Examine the pattern of anti-incumbency in Indian state elections. Why do some states alternate governments while others re-elect them?
