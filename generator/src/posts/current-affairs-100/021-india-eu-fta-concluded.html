The India–EU free trade agreement: the "mother of all deals"

Note 021 · Theme 4: Trade deals & tariffs · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

On 27 January 2026, at the 16th India–EU Summit in New Delhi, Prime Minister Narendra Modi, European Council President António Costa and European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen announced the conclusion of negotiations on an India–European Union free trade agreement. Talks had first begun in 2007, stalled in 2013 and restarted in 2022, so the deal ended almost two decades of on-and-off negotiation.

The scale is large. Bilateral trade is about €190 billion a year, roughly €120 billion in goods and over €80 billion in services, according to figures reported by Down To Earth. Reported terms say the EU will remove duties on about 90% of Indian goods on the first day, with Indian goods getting zero-duty access on over 93% of trade once phase-outs are complete. India will remove duties on about 30% of European goods immediately and on 93% over ten years. Labour-intensive Indian exports such as textiles, apparel, leather, footwear, marine products, gems and jewellery, and toys stand to gain most; the government estimates about 33 billion US dollars of such exports benefit. India cuts duties on European cars, from as high as 110% to as low as 10% within quotas, and on wine and olive oil. Dairy, rice, sugar, beef and poultry are excluded. In services, the EU opens 144 sub-sectors to India.

A major unresolved concern is the EU's Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM), a charge on the carbon content of imports such as steel, aluminium and cement. The deal reportedly includes only a most-favoured-nation assurance on CBAM flexibilities, and analysts estimate compliance costs for Indian exporters of 2 to 4 billion dollars a year.

The agreement still needs legal review, translation, approval by the European Council and consent of the European Parliament, and Indian ratification before it can come into force, which is expected in 2027.

Key takeaways

  • Negotiations concluded on 27 January 2026 after talks first began in 2007.
  • Most Indian goods get duty-free access; India opens gradually, excluding dairy, rice and sugar.
  • The EU's carbon border tax (CBAM) remains a cost for Indian steel and aluminium exporters.

Why it matters for UPSC: The India–EU FTA is India's biggest trade deal and links GS II (international relations) with GS III (trade and climate policy).

Discussion question: Does the India–EU FTA adequately protect Indian exporters from the EU's Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism? Discuss.
