Karnataka: D. K. Shivakumar replaces Siddaramaiah

Note 017 · Theme 2: Elections & states · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

Karnataka saw a change of chief minister within the ruling Congress in 2026. Siddaramaiah, who had led the government since the Congress won the 2023 assembly election, resigned. On 30 May 2026, the Congress legislature party elected D. K. Shivakumar, the state Congress president and deputy chief minister, as its leader.

Shivakumar was sworn in as chief minister on 3 June 2026. G. Parameshwara, a senior Dalit leader and former home minister, became deputy chief minister. Reports said 13 ministers also took oath at the ceremony.

The change brought to an end a long-running contest between the two leaders. In 2023, after the Congress won, both were contenders for the top post; Siddaramaiah became chief minister and Shivakumar his deputy, and there was frequent speculation about a power-sharing understanding, which party leaders never formally confirmed.

Constitutionally, the process followed the usual pattern. The legislature party chooses its leader, often with the national leadership's say, and the governor appoints that leader as chief minister under Article 164. The episode is a reminder that in India's parliamentary system the people elect legislators, not a chief minister directly, so a ruling party can change its leader between elections without a fresh vote.

Key takeaways

  • Siddaramaiah resigned and D. K. Shivakumar was elected Congress legislature party leader on 30 May 2026.
  • Shivakumar was sworn in on 3 June 2026, with G. Parameshwara as deputy chief minister.
  • The legislature party, not the voters directly, chooses the chief minister.

Why it matters for UPSC: Mid-term leadership changes show how the parliamentary system works at state level.
