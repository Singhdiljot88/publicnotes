Medieval India — about 1200 to 1707

Sultanates, Vijayanagara and the Deccan, the Mughals, and the rise of regional powers.

The timeline

Date / periodEvent / person / milestone — why it matters
1206–1526Delhi Sultanate — Qutb-ud-din Aibak (1206), Iltutmish, Razia (1236–1240), Balban, Khaljis, Tughlaqs, Lodis.
Delhi becomes a political centre; coinage, iqta revenue assignments and Persianate administration reshape the north.
1336–1565Vijayanagara Empire — founded 1336, Krishnadevaraya (1509–1529), capital Hampi; Battle of Talikota 1565.
A southern empire of equal scale to Delhi, with its own temple, trade and nayaka system.
1347 onwardsBahmani kingdom (1347) and the later Deccan sultanates — Bijapur, Golconda, Ahmadnagar.
The Deccan is its own political world, not merely a frontier of Delhi or Vijayanagara.
c. 1469–1539Guru Nanak — founder of Sikhism, teaching one God, honest work and service.
The start of a tradition that later builds a distinct community and, under later Gurus, a political presence in Punjab.
1526First Battle of Panipat — Babur defeats Ibrahim Lodi; Mughal rule begins.
Gunpowder, Central Asian links and a new imperial court enter north Indian politics.
1556–1605Akbar — sulh-i-kul (universal tolerance), mansabdari system, expansion, Akbarnama by Abu'l Fazl.
An attempt to run a diverse empire by inclusion and administration, not by religion alone.
1605–1707Jahangir, Shah Jahan (Taj Mahal, 1632–1653) and Aurangzeb — Mughal peak, then over-extension.
Monuments and court culture at the peak; costly Deccan wars and regional assertion strain the empire.
1674Shivaji crowned at Raigad — Maratha state-building in the Deccan.
A regional power that outlasts the Mughals and reshapes 18th-century India.
1699Khalsa founded by Guru Gobind Singh at Anandpur.
A defining moment for Sikh identity, discipline and community organisation.
1707Death of Aurangzeb — Mughal central authority weakens; Marathas, regional states and European companies fill the space.
The 18th century is not a vacuum — it is a century of competing Indian states before British dominance.

Key takeaways

  • Medieval India has several centres — Delhi, Vijayanagara, the Deccan, Punjab — not one story.
  • Administration, trade and religion matter as much as battles.
  • Regional states in the 1700s are the bridge to the colonial period.

Why it matters: It explains the institutions, languages and sacred geographies that the modern period inherits.

Discussion / revision question: Was Akbar's sulh-i-kul a political strategy, a religious idea, or both?
