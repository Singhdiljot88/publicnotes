The opposition's motion to remove the Lok Sabha Speaker

Note 010 of 100 · 2 min read · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

On 11 March 2026, the Lok Sabha rejected by voice vote a resolution moved by the Congress to remove Speaker Om Birla. The notice had been signed by 118 opposition members. It was a rare event: motions to remove a Speaker have been moved only a handful of times since independence, and none has succeeded.

Under Article 94(c) of the Constitution, the Speaker can be removed by a resolution of the Lok Sabha passed by a majority of all the then members of the House, after at least 14 days' notice. While such a resolution is under consideration, the Speaker cannot preside, though he can speak in the House and vote in the first instance. Om Birla stepped away from presiding until the motion was considered.

The opposition's resolution accused the Speaker of partisanship. It alleged that he had prevented the Leader of the Opposition, Rahul Gandhi, from speaking, made unwarranted remarks about women MPs, and suspended opposition members for entire sessions for raising issues of public concern. Ruling party members defended the Speaker as having conducted the House fairly in difficult circumstances. After the motion was defeated, Birla said he would continue to act impartially.

Given the government's majority, the motion was never likely to pass. Its significance was political, as a statement of the opposition's complaints about how Parliament functions. It also revived debate on the neutrality of presiding officers, an issue raised in many parliamentary democracies, and on proposals such as the British convention of a Speaker who leaves party politics.

Key takeaways

  • The Lok Sabha rejected a resolution to remove Speaker Om Birla by voice vote on 11 March 2026.
  • Article 94(c) requires a majority of all members and 14 days' notice to remove the Speaker.
  • The opposition accused the Speaker of partisanship; the motion highlighted concerns about the functioning of Parliament.

Why it matters for UPSC: The Speaker's role and removal procedure are standard polity topics, and this case is a current example.

Discussion question: How can the neutrality of the Speaker's office be strengthened in India?
