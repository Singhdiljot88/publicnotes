Manipur: President's rule ends after a year

Note 018 of 100 · 2 min read · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

President's rule was imposed in Manipur on 13 February 2025, after the resignation of Chief Minister N. Biren Singh following months of ethnic violence between the Meitei and Kuki-Zo communities that began in May 2023. The state assembly was kept in suspended animation rather than dissolved, which left open the option of a new government being formed without an election.

That happened on 4 February 2026. President Droupadi Murmu signed the order revoking President's rule, and hours later Governor Ajay Bhalla administered the oath to Yumnam Khemchand Singh of the BJP as chief minister at Lok Bhavan in Imphal. Two deputy chief ministers were sworn in with him: Nemcha Kipgen and Losii Dikho. The assembly met for its session the next day.

The constitutional mechanism matters. Under Article 356, the President can take over a state's government if satisfied that it cannot be carried on in accordance with the Constitution. A proclamation must be approved by both Houses of Parliament within two months and lasts six months at a time, up to a maximum of three years. Extension beyond one year normally requires a national emergency or an Election Commission certificate that elections cannot be held, under Article 356(5). Revoking it before the one-year mark avoided that test.

The appointment of deputy chief ministers from different communities was seen as an effort at reconciliation, although the new government still faces the task of restoring trust and resettling displaced people.

Key takeaways

  • President's rule in Manipur, imposed on 13 February 2025, was revoked on 4 February 2026.
  • Yumnam Khemchand Singh was sworn in as chief minister the same day, with two deputy chief ministers.
  • Article 356(5) restricts extending President's rule beyond one year.

Why it matters for UPSC: Manipur is the most recent use of Article 356 and shows suspended animation and revocation in practice.

Discussion question: Examine the safeguards against misuse of Article 356 in light of the S. R. Bommai judgment and recent cases.
