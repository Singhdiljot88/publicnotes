Tamil Nadu 2026: Vijay's TVK breaks the Dravidian duopoly

Note 012 of 100 · 2 min read · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

In the 2026 Tamil Nadu assembly election, actor Vijay's Tamilaga Vettri Kazhagam (TVK), contesting its first election, emerged as the largest party with 108 of 234 seats. The ruling Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam (DMK) won 59, the AIADMK 47 and the Congress 5. The majority mark is 118, so the TVK was ten short. Chief Minister M. K. Stalin lost his own seat, Kolathur, to the TVK's V. S. Babu by 8,795 votes.

Because Vijay won from two constituencies and must give up one, the party's effective strength was 107. He staked a claim with letters of support from 121 MLAs. These came from the Congress (5), the CPI (2), the CPI(M) (2), the Viduthalai Chiruthaigal Katchi (VCK) and the Indian Union Muslim League (IUML), all parties that had contested the election as part of the DMK-led alliance. Vijay was asked to prove his majority on the floor of the House by 13 May.

He was sworn in on 10 May 2026 at the Jawaharlal Nehru Stadium in Chennai, along with nine ministers. Reports described it as the first government in nearly six decades that is not led by either the DMK or the AIADMK, which have alternated in power since 1967.

The episode also illustrates the governor's discretion in a hung assembly: the largest party was invited and given a deadline for a floor test, in line with the principle the Supreme Court set out in the S. R. Bommai case (1994) that majority must be tested on the floor of the House.

Key takeaways

  • The TVK won 108 of 234 seats in its first election; the DMK fell to 59.
  • Vijay formed the government with outside support from former DMK allies and took oath on 10 May 2026.
  • It is the first non-DMK, non-AIADMK government in Tamil Nadu since 1967.

Why it matters for UPSC: A hung assembly, post-poll support and a floor test are core polity topics; Tamil Nadu 2026 is a live example.

Discussion question: Discuss the role of the governor in government formation when no party has a majority, with reference to recent examples.
