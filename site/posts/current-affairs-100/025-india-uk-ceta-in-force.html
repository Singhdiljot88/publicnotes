The India–UK trade agreement comes into force

Note 025 of 100 · 2 min read · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

The India–United Kingdom Comprehensive Economic and Trade Agreement (CETA) came into force on 15 July 2026. It had been signed in London on 25 July 2025 in the presence of Prime Ministers Narendra Modi and Keir Starmer, and both countries completed ratification in 2026. The two leaders announced the start date at the G7 summit in June. It is India's first bilateral free trade agreement with a major Western economy.

Under the agreement, Indian exporters get immediate duty-free access on 99% of tariff lines, according to Commerce Minister Piyush Goyal. UK tariffs on Indian processed food, marine products, engineering goods, leather, footwear, textiles and clothing fall to zero. India, in turn, will cut tariffs on about 90% of its tariff lines in stages, with 85% eliminated within a decade. Duties on British whisky fall from about 150% to 40% over time, and a quota of British cars can enter at 10% instead of up to 100%. Sensitive agricultural sectors such as dairy, cereals and oilseeds are protected by exclusion lists. The agreement has 30 chapters and includes, for the first time in an Indian bilateral deal, a chapter on government procurement.

A separate Double Contribution Convention, which came into force on the same date, exempts Indian workers temporarily posted in the UK, and their employers, from paying UK social security contributions for up to three years, saving Indian firms an estimated Rs 4,000 crore. The agreement also eases temporary movement for business visitors and professionals.

UK government estimates suggest annual GDP gains of about £5.1 billion for India and £4.8 billion for the UK, and a long-run increase in bilateral trade of about £25.5 billion a year.

Key takeaways

  • The India–UK CETA, signed on 25 July 2025, came into force on 15 July 2026.
  • Indian exports get duty-free access on 99% of tariff lines; India cuts whisky and car duties within limits.
  • A Double Contribution Convention spares Indian workers in the UK from paying social security twice.

Why it matters for UPSC: The CETA is a model for India's new generation of trade deals and is likely to feature in GS II and GS III questions.

Discussion question: What are the benefits and risks for Indian industry and agriculture from the India–UK trade agreement?
