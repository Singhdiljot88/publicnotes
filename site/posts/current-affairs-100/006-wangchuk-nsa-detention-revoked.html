Sonam Wangchuk's detention and its revocation

Note 006 of 100 · 2 min read · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

Sonam Wangchuk, an engineer, education reformer and climate activist from Ladakh, has been a leading voice in Ladakh's demands for statehood and protection under the Sixth Schedule of the Constitution, which gives tribal areas autonomous councils with powers over land and culture. Ladakh became a union territory without a legislature in 2019.

On 24 September 2025, protests in Leh turned violent. Two days later, on 26 September, the District Magistrate of Leh detained Wangchuk under the National Security Act, 1980 (NSA), "with a view to maintaining public order", and he was moved to Jodhpur Central Jail in Rajasthan. The NSA allows preventive detention, meaning detention to prevent a person from acting in a way that harms security or public order, for up to twelve months without a criminal charge. Article 22 of the Constitution permits preventive detention but requires that the grounds be communicated and that an advisory board review it.

His wife, Dr Gitanjali Angmo, filed a habeas corpus petition in the Supreme Court, arguing among other things that all the material used to justify the detention had not been given to him. On 14 March 2026, days before the case was due to be heard, the Union government revoked the detention, saying the aim was to restore peace and create an atmosphere for dialogue in Ladakh, and noting he had already served nearly half of the maximum period. He was released after about six months.

The case revived debate on preventive detention laws, which critics say are used against peaceful dissent and which the Supreme Court has repeatedly said must be used sparingly.

Key takeaways

  • Wangchuk was detained under the NSA on 26 September 2025 after violence in Leh.
  • The Union government revoked the detention on 14 March 2026, before a Supreme Court hearing.
  • The case highlighted Ladakh's demands for statehood and Sixth Schedule status, and concerns about preventive detention.

Why it matters for UPSC: Preventive detention under Article 22 and the NSA, and Ladakh's constitutional status, are important GS II topics.

Discussion question: Are preventive detention laws compatible with democratic freedoms? Discuss with reference to Article 22.
