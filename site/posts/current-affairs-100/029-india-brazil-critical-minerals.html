India and Brazil sign a critical minerals pact

Note 029 of 100 · 2 min read · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

On 21 February 2026, Prime Minister Narendra Modi and Brazilian President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva met at Hyderabad House in New Delhi and signed an agreement to expand cooperation on critical minerals and rare earth elements. Modi called it "a major step towards building resilient supply chains". Nine other agreements and memoranda were signed, covering digital cooperation, health and entrepreneurship. Lula had come to India to attend the AI Impact Summit, where he called for an inclusive, multilateral framework for governing artificial intelligence.

Critical minerals and rare earths are essential for electric vehicle batteries, magnets in motors and wind turbines, electronics and defence equipment. China dominates the processing of most of these minerals and the production of rare earth magnets, and its export controls in 2025 caused shortages for Indian manufacturers. Brazil has some of the world's largest rare earth reserves, along with large deposits of niobium, graphite and nickel. For India, the agreement is part of a wider strategy of diversifying supplies through partnerships with countries such as Australia, Argentina, Chile and Canada, alongside the National Critical Mineral Mission launched in 2025.

The two countries also set economic targets. Bilateral trade exceeded 15 billion dollars in 2025, and Modi said they aim to take it past 20 billion dollars within five years. Brazil's main exports to India are sugar, crude oil, vegetable oils, cotton and iron ore. The Brazilian aircraft maker Embraer and the Adani Group announced plans to build aircraft in India, and both leaders highlighted growing defence cooperation.

India and Brazil are partners in BRICS, the G20 and IBSA (with South Africa), and both seek permanent seats on the UN Security Council as part of the G4.

Key takeaways

  • Modi and Lula signed a critical minerals and rare earths agreement on 21 February 2026.
  • The pact aims to reduce India's dependence on China for minerals vital to clean energy and electronics.
  • India and Brazil aim to raise trade from over 15 billion dollars to above 20 billion dollars in five years.

Why it matters for UPSC: Critical minerals diplomacy combines GS II (bilateral relations) and GS III (energy security and industry).

Discussion question: Why are partnerships with resource-rich countries like Brazil essential for India's clean energy transition?
