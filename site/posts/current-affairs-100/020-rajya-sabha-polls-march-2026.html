The March 2026 Rajya Sabha elections

Note 020 of 100 · 2 min read · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

Elections to 37 Rajya Sabha seats in ten states were scheduled for 16 March 2026, as members' six-year terms ended. Rajya Sabha members are elected indirectly by the elected members of state assemblies, using proportional representation by means of the single transferable vote, under Article 80. About one-third of members retire every two years, which is why these are called biennial elections.

Of the 37 seats, 26 candidates were elected unopposed because the number of candidates equalled the number of seats. Voting took place for the remaining 11 seats in Bihar (5), Odisha (4) and Haryana (2). Reports by 17 March said the NDA had won 21 seats, the INDIA bloc 13 and the Biju Janata Dal 1, with counting in Haryana held up by a complaint.

The Bihar contest drew attention. The NDA was expected to win four of five seats but won all five after three Congress MLAs and one RJD MLA did not vote with their alliance. Those elected included former chief minister Nitish Kumar and BJP president Nitin Nabin. In Odisha, there were allegations of horse-trading around the fourth seat.

Rajya Sabha voting is by open ballot: since a 2003 amendment, party MLAs must show their marked ballot to their party's authorised agent. The Supreme Court upheld this in Kuldip Nayar v. Union of India (2006). Cross-voting does not attract disqualification under the Tenth Schedule, because a Rajya Sabha election is not a vote in the House, though parties may take disciplinary action.

Key takeaways

  • 37 seats were up in March 2026; 26 were filled unopposed and 11 went to a vote.
  • The NDA won all five seats in Bihar after cross-voting by opposition MLAs.
  • Rajya Sabha polls use an open ballot for party MLAs, upheld in Kuldip Nayar (2006).

Why it matters for UPSC: Rajya Sabha numbers decide whether the government can pass bills and constitutional amendments.

Discussion question: Should the open ballot system in Rajya Sabha elections be extended to prevent cross-voting, or does it undermine the secrecy of the vote?
