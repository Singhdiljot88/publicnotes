India–Oman CEPA: a gateway to the Gulf

Note 026 of 100 · 2 min read · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

The India–Oman Comprehensive Economic Partnership Agreement (CEPA) came into force on 1 June 2026. It was signed in Muscat on 18 December 2025, after negotiations that began in 2023. It is India's second trade agreement with a Gulf country, after the India–UAE CEPA of 2022, and Oman's first such comprehensive agreement with any country since its free trade agreement with the United States.

According to reported terms, Oman gives zero-duty access on 98% of its tariff lines, covering about 99% of India's exports to Oman by value, removing duties of up to 5% on Indian exports worth about 3.64 billion dollars. Sectors expected to benefit include pharmaceuticals, engineering goods, chemicals, gems and jewellery, textiles, leather, plastics, marine products and automobiles. India offers tariff concessions on nearly 78% of its tariff lines, while keeping sensitive items such as dairy products, edible oils, oilseeds, fruits and vegetables, spices, coffee, tea and rubber outside liberalisation. On the first day, around ten consignments of agricultural products and gems and jewellery were shipped from Mumbai, Kolkata and Chennai under the new preferential rates.

Bilateral trade reached 11.18 billion dollars in 2025–26, up from 10.61 billion dollars the year before, according to official figures reported by Business Standard. Oman is strategically important to India: it sits at the mouth of the Strait of Hormuz, hosts a large Indian community, and gives the Indian Navy access to the port of Duqm. Indian officials see the CEPA as a step towards a wider agreement with the Gulf Cooperation Council.

Key takeaways

  • The India–Oman CEPA was signed on 18 December 2025 and came into force on 1 June 2026.
  • Oman gives duty-free access for about 99% of India's exports by value; India protects dairy and edible oils.
  • Oman's location at the Strait of Hormuz makes the partnership strategic as well as economic.

Why it matters for UPSC: The CEPA links India's trade strategy with its West Asia and maritime policy, frequent GS II themes.

Discussion question: How do trade agreements with Gulf countries serve India's economic and strategic interests?
