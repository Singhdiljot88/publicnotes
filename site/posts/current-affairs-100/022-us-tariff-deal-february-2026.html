From 50% to 18%: the India–US tariff deal of February 2026

Note 022 of 100 · 2 min read · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

In August 2025 the United States raised tariffs on most Indian goods to 50%. This combined a 25% "reciprocal" tariff with an additional 25% penalty that President Donald Trump linked to India's purchases of Russian oil. It was one of the highest US tariff rates on any major trading partner and hit labour-intensive Indian exports such as textiles, gems and jewellery, and shrimp.

On 2 February 2026, President Trump announced a trade deal with India. According to Al Jazeera and Reuters reporting, the reciprocal tariff would fall from 25% to 18%, and the White House said the 25% Russian-oil penalty would be withdrawn. Trump said Prime Minister Modi had agreed to stop buying Russian oil, to buy oil from the United States and possibly Venezuela, to lower trade barriers, and to buy more than 500 billion dollars of American energy, technology, farm and other products. Modi welcomed the lower tariff on social media and thanked Trump, but did not repeat the claims about lowering Indian tariffs on US goods. A joint statement on 6 February set out the proposed 18% rate.

Experts questioned whether this was a full trade agreement or only a tariff arrangement, and the details of India's commitments remained unclear. India's imports of Russian crude were already falling, from about 1.2 million barrels a day in January towards a projected 800,000 in March, according to Reuters figures cited by Al Jazeera.

The proposed 18% rate was overtaken within weeks. On 20 February the US Supreme Court struck down the legal basis for the reciprocal tariffs, and the US moved to a temporary 10% global tariff instead (see the next note).

Key takeaways

  • US tariffs on India peaked at 50% in August 2025, including a 25% Russian-oil penalty.
  • On 2 February 2026, Trump announced a cut to 18% and the withdrawal of the oil penalty.
  • India's commitments, including on Russian oil, were stated by Trump and not fully confirmed by India.

Why it matters for UPSC: The episode shows how energy choices, sanctions and trade are linked in India–US relations, a frequent GS II theme.

Discussion question: How should India balance its energy security needs with pressure from trading partners over Russian oil?
