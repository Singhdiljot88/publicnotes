Australia agrees to sell uranium to India

Note 030 of 100 · 2 min read · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

On 9 July 2026, Prime Minister Narendra Modi and Australian Prime Minister Anthony Albanese met in Melbourne and signed an agreement allowing long-term exports of Australian uranium to India for India's civilian nuclear power programme. According to their joint statement, the uranium will be used for "exclusively peaceful purposes" and will be subject to International Atomic Energy Agency safeguards. Albanese said the arrangement "facilitates Australian uranium exports to India" to help expand non-fossil-fuel power.

The deal ends a long stalemate. Australia holds a large share of the world's uranium resources (Al Jazeera put its share of world supply at about 28%), but for years refused to sell to India because India has not signed the Nuclear Non-Proliferation Treaty. Australia's governing Labor Party lifted the ban in 2011, and the two countries signed a civil nuclear cooperation agreement in 2014, but actual exports remained limited because of concerns about safeguards. India aims to reach 100 gigawatts of nuclear power capacity by 2047, and needs reliable fuel supplies; it also signed a uranium supply deal with Canada's Cameco in March 2026.

The leaders also agreed to strengthen defence cooperation and critical minerals supply chains, and to set up a temporary space tracking terminal on Australia's Cocos (Keeling) Islands to support Indian space missions, including human spaceflight. Australia, which is seeking to diversify its trade away from China, counts India as its fifth-largest trading partner, with two-way trade of 54.4 billion Australian dollars in 2024–25. The two countries are linked by an interim trade agreement (ECTA) in force since 2022 and are both members of the Quad.

Key takeaways

  • Australia and India signed a uranium export agreement on 9 July 2026, under IAEA safeguards.
  • The deal ends years of limited exports despite a 2014 civil nuclear agreement.
  • India's nuclear target of 100 GW by 2047 makes secure uranium supplies essential.

Why it matters for UPSC: The deal links India's nuclear energy goals, non-proliferation politics and Indo-Pacific partnerships (GS II and GS III).

Discussion question: How have India's civil nuclear agreements helped it overcome the constraints of not signing the NPT?
