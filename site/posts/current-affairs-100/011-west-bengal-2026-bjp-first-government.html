West Bengal 2026: the BJP forms its first government

Note 011 of 100 · 2 min read · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

The results of the West Bengal assembly election were counted on 4 May 2026. The Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP) won 206 seats and the All India Trinamool Congress (TMC) won 80, out of 293 seats counted; polling in one seat took place later. The result ended 15 years of TMC rule under Mamata Banerjee. Before that, the Left Front had governed the state for 34 years, from 1977 to 2011.

The most symbolic contest was Bhabanipur in Kolkata, the chief minister's own seat. Banerjee lost it to the BJP's Suvendu Adhikari by about 15,000 votes, according to reported counting figures. Adhikari had also defeated her at Nandigram in 2021. He was once a senior TMC leader and a minister in her government from 2016 to 2020, before joining the BJP in 2020.

Adhikari was sworn in on 9 May 2026 as the first BJP chief minister of West Bengal. Commentators, including the Institute of South Asian Studies in Singapore, described the result as a turning point: the BJP had never governed the state, and its rise from a marginal force to a two-thirds majority took about a decade.

The result also shows how quickly a dominant regional party can be displaced once a major leader defects and the main opposition vote consolidates behind one party.

Key takeaways

  • The BJP won 206 seats to the TMC's 80 in May 2026, ending 15 years of TMC rule.
  • Mamata Banerjee lost Bhabanipur to Suvendu Adhikari.
  • Adhikari became West Bengal's first BJP chief minister on 9 May 2026.

Why it matters for UPSC: West Bengal had never had a BJP government; the result reshapes politics in eastern India and the Rajya Sabha arithmetic over time.

Discussion question: What factors explain the decline of dominant regional parties in Indian states? Illustrate with West Bengal 2026.
