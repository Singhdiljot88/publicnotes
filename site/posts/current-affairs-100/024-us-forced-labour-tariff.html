The US "forced labour" tariff of July 2026

Note 024 of 100 · 2 min read · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

As the temporary Section 122 tariffs neared their 150-day limit, the US Trade Representative (USTR) concluded an investigation under Section 301 of the Trade Act of 1974 into 60 economies that, in its view, did not adequately ban or enforce restrictions on imports of goods made with forced labour. On 23 July 2026, USTR Jamieson Greer announced the results, and the new duties took effect on 24 July, the day the Section 122 tariffs expired.

India was placed in the lower tier. India, along with Canada, the United Kingdom, Bangladesh, Pakistan and others, 17 economies in all, faces a 10% tariff, down from 12.5% proposed in the USTR's draft. Forty-three other economies, including China, Vietnam, Thailand, the UAE, Brazil and South Africa, face 12.5%. USTR cited India's amendment to its Foreign Trade Policy on 14 June 2026, which banned imports of goods made with forced or compulsory labour, in giving it the lower rate. Goods covered by Section 232 national security tariffs, such as steel, aluminium and copper, continue to face separate rates of 25% or 50%.

According to an analysis reported by Rural Voice, about 70% of Indian exports, including engineering goods, textiles, chemicals, gems and jewellery and leather, face the 10% duty on top of normal tariffs, while about 8% fall under Section 232. Reactions in India differed. The Global Trade Research Initiative (GTRI) argued the tariff "lacks a credible factual basis" because the US had not shown that India exports forced-labour goods, and said its real purpose was to preserve US tariff levels. The Federation of Indian Export Organisations said the lower rate gave Indian exporters an edge over many competitors.

The episode shows how trade measures are increasingly justified on grounds such as labour standards, national security and the environment.

Key takeaways

  • Section 301 forced-labour tariffs took effect on 24 July 2026, replacing the temporary 10% global duty.
  • India got the lower 10% rate after banning forced-labour imports in June 2026; 43 economies face 12.5%.
  • Indian experts disagree on whether the tariff is justified or a way to keep US tariffs in place.

Why it matters for UPSC: Linking trade to labour standards is a recurring theme in WTO and trade questions in GS II and GS III.

Discussion question: Are labour and environmental standards legitimate grounds for trade restrictions, or disguised protectionism? Discuss with reference to India.
