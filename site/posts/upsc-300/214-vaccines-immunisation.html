Vaccines and India's immunisation programme

Note 214 of 300 · 2 min read · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

A vaccine trains the immune system to recognise and fight a disease-causing organism without causing the illness. Types include live attenuated vaccines (weakened organisms, such as the measles and oral polio vaccines), inactivated vaccines (killed organisms, such as injectable polio vaccine and Covaxin), subunit and conjugate vaccines (using pieces of the organism, such as hepatitis B and HPV vaccines), viral vector vaccines (such as Covishield, which used a harmless adenovirus to carry instructions), and mRNA vaccines, which instruct cells to make a viral protein. India approved its first indigenous mRNA vaccine, Gemcovac, for COVID-19 in 2022.

India's Universal Immunisation Programme (UIP), started in 1985 from the Expanded Programme on Immunisation of 1978, is one of the largest in the world. It now provides free vaccines against twelve diseases, including tuberculosis (BCG), polio, diphtheria, pertussis, tetanus, hepatitis B, Haemophilus influenzae type b, measles, rubella, rotavirus diarrhoea, pneumococcal pneumonia and, in some districts, Japanese encephalitis. Mission Indradhanush, launched in 2014, targeted children who had missed vaccines, and full immunisation coverage rose to about 76% in NFHS-5 (2019–21). The U-WIN digital platform records vaccinations.

India is often called the "pharmacy of the world" and is the largest vaccine producer by volume; companies such as the Serum Institute of India supply a large share of the world's vaccines. In 2022 India approved Cervavac, the first indigenous vaccine against human papillomavirus (HPV), which causes cervical cancer, a leading cancer among Indian women.

During COVID-19, India administered over 220 crore doses, mainly of Covishield and Covaxin, through the CoWIN platform, and exported vaccines under Vaccine Maitri. Challenges include vaccine hesitancy, reaching urban slums and remote areas, and maintaining the cold chain.

Key takeaways

  • Vaccines may be live attenuated, inactivated, subunit, viral vector or mRNA.
  • The Universal Immunisation Programme (1985) covers twelve diseases; full coverage was about 76% in NFHS-5.
  • India is the world's largest vaccine producer by volume; Cervavac is its first indigenous HPV vaccine.

Why it matters for UPSC: Vaccine types and India's immunisation programmes are standard Prelims science and health questions.

Practice question: How can India close the remaining gaps in childhood immunisation coverage?
