Project Tiger and tiger conservation

Note 243 of 300 · 2 min read · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

India launched Project Tiger on 1 April 1973, after surveys showed that tiger numbers had fallen to under 2,000 because of hunting and habitat loss. Nine tiger reserves were set up initially, including Corbett. Today there are more than 55 tiger reserves, managed through core areas free of human activity and buffer areas where people live alongside wildlife. The National Tiger Conservation Authority (NTCA), a statutory body created by the 2006 amendment to the Wildlife (Protection) Act, oversees the programme.

India estimates its tiger population every four years using camera traps, sign surveys and statistical models. The 2022 estimate, released in 2023, put the number at an average of 3,682 tigers (within a range of 3,167 to 3,925), about 75% of the world's wild tigers. Madhya Pradesh had the most, followed by Karnataka, Uttarakhand and Maharashtra. Numbers in central India and the Shivalik hills grew, while those in the Western Ghats were stable or declining and the North-East was poorly covered. The 2026 estimation exercise has begun and, for the first time, extends more systematically to areas outside reserves and corridors.

India also hosts global efforts. In April 2023 it launched the International Big Cat Alliance, covering the seven big cats: tiger, lion, leopard, snow leopard, cheetah, jaguar and puma.

Challenges remain. Many reserves are near their carrying capacity, so tigers spill into human-dominated landscapes, leading to conflict. Corridors linking reserves are cut by highways, railways, mines and canals. Poaching for skins and body parts continues, and relocating villages out of core areas must be voluntary and fairly compensated.

Key takeaways

  • Project Tiger began on 1 April 1973; the NTCA has overseen it since 2006.
  • The 2022 estimate was 3,682 tigers on average, about 75% of the world's wild tigers.
  • Corridors, conflict and carrying capacity are the main challenges; India launched the International Big Cat Alliance in 2023.

Why it matters for UPSC: Tiger conservation is a perennial topic, and census figures are frequently tested.

Practice question: Project Tiger is often called a conservation success. What are its limits, and what should come next?
