Protecting heritage: the ASI, heritage laws and GI tags

Note 030 of 300 · 2 min read · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

The Archaeological Survey of India (ASI), founded in 1861 with Alexander Cunningham as its first Director-General, is the main agency for archaeology and monument protection. It works under the Ministry of Culture and looks after more than 3,600 centrally protected monuments. John Marshall, its Director-General from 1902, led the excavations that revealed the Harappan civilisation.

The legal framework rests on the Ancient Monuments and Archaeological Sites and Remains Act, 1958 (AMASR Act). It declares monuments of national importance, sets a prohibited area of 100 metres and a regulated area of a further 200 metres around them, and controls construction there. The National Monuments Authority, created by a 2010 amendment, oversees these zones. The Antiquities and Art Treasures Act, 1972 regulates trade in antiquities, and India has used diplomacy and international law to recover hundreds of stolen artefacts from abroad in recent years. Under the Constitution, Article 49 directs the state to protect monuments, and Article 51A(f) makes it a fundamental duty of citizens to value and preserve the country's composite culture.

Geographical Indication (GI) tags protect products whose quality comes from their place of origin. India's GI Act was passed in 1999 and came into force in 2003. Darjeeling tea was the first Indian product to receive a GI tag, in 2004. Hundreds of products now have GI status, from Banarasi sarees and Mysore silk to Kashmir saffron and Madhubani paintings. GI tags help artisans and farmers by preventing imitation, though enforcement and marketing remain weak.

Key takeaways

  • The ASI, founded in 1861, protects more than 3,600 central monuments under the AMASR Act, 1958.
  • Article 49 and fundamental duty 51A(f) give heritage protection a constitutional base.
  • The GI Act (1999, in force 2003) protects place-linked products; Darjeeling tea was the first.

Why it matters for UPSC: Heritage institutions, laws and GI tags are recurring Prelims topics and useful in Mains answers on culture and economy.

Practice question: How can heritage protection laws be balanced with the needs of urban development? Suggest measures.
