Scheduled Tribes, forest rights and PVTGs

Note 142 of 300 · 2 min read · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

Scheduled Tribes are notified under Article 342. The 2011 Census counted about 10.4 crore ST people, 8.6% of the population, with the largest numbers in Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra, Odisha, Rajasthan, Gujarat and Jharkhand; in several north-eastern states and Lakshadweep they form a majority. Constitutional safeguards include reservation, the Fifth and Sixth Schedules, and the National Commission for Scheduled Tribes (Article 338A).

The Scheduled Tribes and Other Traditional Forest Dwellers (Recognition of Forest Rights) Act, 2006, usually called the Forest Rights Act (FRA), sought to correct what its preamble called a "historical injustice": forest communities' rights had never been recorded when forests were declared state property. It recognises individual rights to land cultivated before 13 December 2005 (up to four hectares), and community rights to use and manage forest resources, including minor forest produce, grazing and water. Gram sabhas play the central role in verifying claims. Community forest resource rights allow villages to protect and manage their forests; Mendha-Lekha in Maharashtra became a famous example. Implementation has been uneven, with large numbers of claims rejected; in 2019 the Supreme Court ordered evictions of rejected claimants but stayed the order after protests.

Among STs, 75 groups are classified as Particularly Vulnerable Tribal Groups (PVTGs) because of pre-agricultural technology, very low literacy, stagnant or declining population and subsistence economies. Odisha has the largest number. They include the Jarawa and Sentinelese of the Andaman Islands, the Baiga of central India and the Toda of the Nilgiris. The PM-JANMAN mission, launched in November 2023, aims to provide PVTG habitations with housing, roads, water, electricity, health and education.

Tribal communities face displacement by dams, mines and protected areas, land alienation, poor health and education outcomes, and conflicts that have fuelled left-wing extremism.

Key takeaways

  • STs were 8.6% of the population in 2011.
  • The Forest Rights Act (2006) recognises individual and community forest rights through gram sabhas.
  • 75 PVTGs are covered by the PM-JANMAN mission launched in 2023.

Why it matters for UPSC: The FRA, PVTGs and tribal welfare are among the most frequently asked social justice topics.

Practice question: How far has the Forest Rights Act corrected the "historical injustice" done to forest dwellers?
