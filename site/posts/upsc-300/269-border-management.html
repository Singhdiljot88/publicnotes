UPSC 300 · 🛡️ Theme 27: Internal security · GS III

Border management

Note 269 of 300 · 2 min read · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

India has land borders of about 15,200 km with seven countries: Bangladesh (the longest, about 4,097 km), China, Pakistan, Nepal, Myanmar, Bhutan and Afghanistan (through Pakistan-occupied territory). Each border poses different problems. The Pakistan border and Line of Control face infiltration, cross-border firing, smuggling of weapons and drugs, and, increasingly, drones dropping arms and narcotics in Punjab. The China border, the Line of Actual Control, is not demarcated, leading to standoffs such as at Galwan in 2020. The Bangladesh border, running through rivers and densely populated areas, faces illegal migration, cattle and drug smuggling, and human trafficking. The open borders with Nepal and Bhutan, based on treaties allowing free movement, can be misused by criminals and insurgents. The Myanmar border, with a Free Movement Regime allowing border residents to travel a set distance without visas, has been used by insurgents and drug traffickers; in 2024 the government announced it would fence the entire Myanmar border and scrap the regime, a move opposed by communities in Mizoram and Nagaland with ties across the border.

Border management follows the principle of "one border, one force": the BSF guards the Pakistan and Bangladesh borders, the ITBP the China border, the SSB the Nepal and Bhutan borders, and the Assam Rifles the Myanmar border. The army manages the LoC and parts of the LAC. In 2021, the BSF's jurisdiction for search and seizure was extended to 50 km from the border in Punjab, West Bengal and Assam, which those states' governments opposed.

Technology is increasingly used: the Comprehensive Integrated Border Management System (CIBMS) uses sensors, cameras and radars to create "smart fences" in difficult terrain. Integrated check posts handle legal trade and travel. The Vibrant Villages Programme, launched in 2023 for villages along the northern border with China, and its second phase approved in 2025 for other borders, aims to improve infrastructure and stop out-migration from border villages, which act as the first line of security.

Key takeaways

  • India's land borders total about 15,200 km; the Bangladesh border is the longest.
  • Each border has a dedicated force under the "one border, one force" principle.
  • Smart fencing, integrated check posts and the Vibrant Villages Programme strengthen border management.

Why it matters for UPSC: Border management is a named GS III topic, with recent changes on the Myanmar border and BSF jurisdiction often asked.

Practice question: What challenges does India face in managing its border with Myanmar? Evaluate the decision to fence it.
