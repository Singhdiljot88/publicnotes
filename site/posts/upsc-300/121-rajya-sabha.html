The Rajya Sabha: the Council of States

Note 121 of 300 · 2 min read · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

The Rajya Sabha is Parliament's upper house. The Constitution sets its maximum strength at 250: up to 238 representatives of the states and union territories and 12 members nominated by the President for their expertise in literature, science, art and social service. Its present strength is 245. Seats are allocated to states under the Fourth Schedule, broadly in proportion to population, so Uttar Pradesh has 31 seats and several small states have one. Members are elected by the elected members of state legislative assemblies through proportional representation by the single transferable vote, with open ballots since 2003 to curb cross-voting. The minimum age is 30.

The Rajya Sabha is a permanent house and is never dissolved. One-third of its members retire every two years, and each member serves six years. The Vice-President is its ex-officio chairman.

Its powers are mostly equal to those of the Lok Sabha in ordinary legislation and constitutional amendments, which must pass both houses separately by the required majority. It is weaker on money matters: a money bill can only be introduced in the Lok Sabha, and the Rajya Sabha can only recommend changes within 14 days, which the Lok Sabha may ignore. The Council of Ministers is responsible only to the Lok Sabha. But the Rajya Sabha has special powers: under Article 249, it can authorise Parliament to make laws on a state subject in the national interest by a two-thirds majority, and under Article 312, it can authorise the creation of new All India Services.

Supporters see the Rajya Sabha as a house of revision that protects states' interests and brings expertise into lawmaking. Critics note that the residency requirement for members was removed in 2003, weakening the link with states, and that parties sometimes use it to accommodate leaders who lose elections.

Key takeaways

  • The Rajya Sabha has a maximum of 250 members, 12 of them nominated; its present strength is 245.
  • It is permanent, with one-third retiring every two years.
  • It has limited money powers but special powers under Articles 249 and 312.

Why it matters for UPSC: Rajya Sabha composition and powers are high-frequency Prelims questions and relevant to federalism debates.

Practice question: Does the Rajya Sabha effectively represent the interests of states? Suggest reforms.
