Social and religious reform movements of the nineteenth century

Note 038 of 300 · 2 min read · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

Nineteenth-century India saw reformers question social practices and reinterpret religion, often in response to Western criticism and new ideas of reason and equality.

Raja Ram Mohan Roy founded the Brahmo Samaj in 1828. He opposed idol worship and campaigned against sati, which Governor-General William Bentinck banned in 1829. Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar campaigned for widow remarriage, leading to the Hindu Widows' Remarriage Act of 1856, and worked for girls' education. In western India, the Prarthana Samaj worked on similar lines.

Swami Dayananda Saraswati founded the Arya Samaj in 1875, calling for a return to the Vedas, opposing idol worship and caste by birth, and starting the shuddhi movement and DAV schools. Swami Vivekananda, a disciple of Ramakrishna, spoke at the Parliament of the World's Religions in Chicago in 1893 and founded the Ramakrishna Mission in 1897, linking spirituality with social service.

Anti-caste movements challenged hierarchy from below. Jyotirao Phule founded the Satyashodhak Samaj in 1873 and, with Savitribai Phule, opened a school for girls in Pune in 1848. Sree Narayana Guru in Kerala worked for the Ezhava community with the message "one caste, one religion, one god for humanity". Among Muslims, Sir Syed Ahmad Khan founded the Muhammadan Anglo-Oriental College at Aligarh in 1875 to promote modern education. The Singh Sabha movement reformed Sikh institutions, and the Parsi Rahnumai Mazdayasnan Sabha reformed Parsi practice.

These movements laid the social foundations of the national movement, though most were led by upper-caste, educated men and reached limited sections of society.

Key takeaways

  • Ram Mohan Roy (Brahmo Samaj, 1828) and Vidyasagar fought sati and for widow remarriage.
  • Dayananda (Arya Samaj, 1875) and Vivekananda (Ramakrishna Mission, 1897) reinterpreted Hinduism.
  • Phule, Narayana Guru and Sir Syed Ahmad Khan addressed caste and education for marginalised groups.

Why it matters for UPSC: Reformers, their organisations and dates are high-frequency Prelims topics.

Practice question: Distinguish between reformist and revivalist trends in nineteenth-century socio-religious movements.
