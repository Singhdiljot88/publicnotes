Critical minerals and the new resource race

Note 103 of 300 · 2 min read · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

Critical minerals are those that are essential for modern technologies but face supply risks. They include lithium, cobalt, nickel and graphite for batteries; rare earth elements for magnets in electric motors and wind turbines; and others such as gallium, germanium and silicon for electronics and solar panels. Supply is highly concentrated: China dominates the refining of most critical minerals and the production of rare earth magnets, and has used export controls on some of them.

In June 2023 the Ministry of Mines released a list of 30 critical minerals for India. The MMDR Act was amended in 2023 to allow the Union government to auction concessions for critical and strategic minerals and to open some previously reserved atomic minerals, such as lithium, to private exploration. In February 2023 the Geological Survey of India reported inferred lithium resources of 5.9 million tonnes at Salal-Haimana in the Reasi district of Jammu and Kashmir, though early auctions of the block drew weak interest.

The National Critical Mineral Mission, approved in January 2025, aims to boost domestic exploration, mining and processing, recycling, and the acquisition of mineral assets abroad. Khanij Bidesh India Ltd (KABIL), a joint venture of public sector companies, has acquired lithium exploration blocks in Argentina's Catamarca province. India has signed critical mineral partnerships with countries such as Australia, Argentina, Chile and Brazil, and is part of the US-led Minerals Security Partnership. A scheme to promote domestic manufacturing of rare earth permanent magnets was approved in 2025.

The challenge is not only finding minerals but building processing capacity, which requires technology, investment and environmental safeguards.

Key takeaways

  • Critical minerals such as lithium, cobalt and rare earths are essential for clean energy and electronics.
  • India listed 30 critical minerals in 2023 and launched the National Critical Mineral Mission in 2025.
  • China's dominance in processing makes diversification and international partnerships a priority.

Why it matters for UPSC: Critical minerals sit at the intersection of geography, economy, energy and foreign policy, a favourite recent theme.

Practice question: Why are critical minerals a strategic concern for India? What steps has India taken to secure them?
