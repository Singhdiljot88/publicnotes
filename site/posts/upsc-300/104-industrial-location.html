Factors in the location of industries

Note 104 of 300 · 2 min read · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

Industries locate where they can minimise costs and maximise access to markets. The economist Alfred Weber's classical theory argued that industries using heavy, weight-losing raw materials, such as iron and steel or cement, locate near raw materials, while those whose products gain weight or are perishable locate near markets. Other factors include power, water, labour, transport, capital, government policy and agglomeration benefits, the advantages firms gain from clustering together.

India's iron and steel industry shows raw material orientation. The first modern steel plant, the Tata Iron and Steel Company at Jamshedpur (1907), was located near iron ore and coal in the Chotanagpur region, with water from the Subarnarekha river. Public sector plants at Bhilai, Rourkela, Durgapur and Bokaro were built in the same mineral belt. Coastal plants such as Visakhapatnam use imported coal and export steel.

The cotton textile industry began in Mumbai in 1854, drawing on raw cotton from the Deccan's black soils, a humid climate that kept threads from breaking, a port for importing machinery and exporting goods, and capital from merchants. Ahmedabad became the "Manchester of India". Later, many mills moved inland and to Tamil Nadu, while the Mumbai mills closed after the 1982 textile strike.

Modern industries are often footloose, meaning they are not tied to raw materials. The information technology industry clusters in Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune, Chennai and the National Capital Region because of skilled labour, educational institutions, good connectivity and supportive policy. The automobile industry has clusters around Chennai, Pune, Gurugram-Manesar and Sanand. Government policies, such as special economic zones, industrial corridors and production-linked incentives, now play a large role in location.

Key takeaways

  • Weight-losing raw materials pull industries towards their sources; perishable goods pull them to markets.
  • Iron and steel cluster in the Chotanagpur belt; cotton textiles began in Mumbai and Ahmedabad.
  • Footloose industries such as IT cluster where skills and infrastructure are available.

Why it matters for UPSC: Industrial location factors, with Indian examples, are a standard Mains geography question.

Practice question: Why is the IT industry concentrated in a few Indian cities? What could spread it more widely?
