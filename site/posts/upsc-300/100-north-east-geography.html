The geography of North-East India

Note 100 of 300 · 2 min read · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

North-East India comprises eight states: Arunachal Pradesh, Assam, Manipur, Meghalaya, Mizoram, Nagaland, Tripura and Sikkim. It covers about 8% of India's area and has under 4% of its population. About 98% of its borders are international, with China, Bhutan, Myanmar, Bangladesh and Nepal, and it is connected to the rest of India by the Siliguri Corridor, a strip of land about 22 km wide at its narrowest, often called the "chicken's neck".

The region's physical geography is varied. The Eastern Himalayas in Arunachal Pradesh and Sikkim rise to Kangchenjunga. The Brahmaputra valley in Assam is a broad floodplain, and the river is one of the world's largest by water flow and sediment load; it contains Majuli, among the largest river islands in the world. The Meghalaya plateau, an extension of the peninsular plateau, receives some of the highest rainfall on Earth at Mawsynram and Cherrapunji. The Purvanchal hills along the Myanmar border include the Patkai, Naga and Mizo hills. Manipur's Loktak lake has floating islands of vegetation called phumdis, including Keibul Lamjao, the world's only floating national park.

The North-East is part of the Indo-Burma and Himalaya biodiversity hotspots, with Kaziranga (home to most of the world's one-horned rhinos) and Manas as UNESCO World Heritage Sites. Its rivers have huge hydropower potential, but dams raise concerns about displacement, earthquakes and downstream effects.

Floods and erosion in Assam, landslides in the hills and earthquake risk are major hazards. Connectivity projects, such as the Bogibeel bridge, the Kaladan multimodal project through Myanmar and rail links to state capitals, aim to end the region's isolation, as part of the Act East Policy.

Key takeaways

  • The North-East has eight states, mostly international borders and the narrow Siliguri Corridor.
  • It includes the Brahmaputra valley, the Meghalaya plateau and the Purvanchal hills.
  • Rich biodiversity, hydropower potential and serious flood and landslide hazards define the region.

Why it matters for UPSC: North-East geography is important for questions on security, connectivity, biodiversity and the Act East Policy.

Practice question: How does the geography of the North-East shape its development challenges and strategic importance?
