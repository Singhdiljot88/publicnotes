How values are formed: family, society and education

Note 272 of 300 · 2 min read · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

Values are deep, relatively stable beliefs about what is important and desirable, such as honesty, fairness, compassion or loyalty. They guide choices and judgements. The GS IV syllabus asks about the role of family, society and educational institutions in inculcating values.

The family is the first school of values. Children learn far more from what parents do than from what they say: honesty at home, how domestic workers are treated, whether rules are bent for convenience. Psychologists describe this as socialisation through imitation, reinforcement and identification. A family can also transmit prejudices, such as caste or gender bias, so values learnt early are not always good ones.

Society widens the circle. Religion, community, neighbourhood, peer groups, the media and now social media present role models and norms. When society celebrates success regardless of means, or tolerates petty corruption as normal, it weakens values learnt at home. Conversely, social movements, from the freedom struggle to campaigns for transparency, have spread values such as non-violence, equality and accountability.

Educational institutions shape values through the curriculum, through teachers as role models, and through the hidden curriculum of school culture: whether cheating is punished, whether students of all backgrounds are treated equally, whether questioning is encouraged. The National Education Policy 2020 stresses ethical and constitutional values, critical thinking and service. Value education works best when it is lived in daily practice rather than taught only as a separate subject.

Values formed in these settings are tested later in workplaces. Strong institutions with clear codes, fair leadership and consequences for misconduct help people act on good values rather than abandon them under pressure.

Key takeaways

  • Values are stable beliefs about what matters; they guide choices.
  • Families teach mainly by example, and can pass on both virtues and prejudices.
  • Schools shape values through teachers, culture and the hidden curriculum, not only lessons.

Why it matters for UPSC: The role of family, society and education in value formation is a direct syllabus item and a frequent short-answer question.

Practice question: Why do values taught in school often fail to translate into behaviour in adult life? Suggest remedies.
