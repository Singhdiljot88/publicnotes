UPSC 300 · 🛡️ Theme 27: Internal security · GS III

Insurgency and peace accords in the North-East

Note 263 of 300 · 2 min read · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

The North-East has seen insurgencies since soon after independence, driven by distinct ethnic identities, fear of demographic change from migration, a sense of neglect by the Centre, difficult terrain and porous borders. The Naga movement, led by the Naga National Council under A. Z. Phizo, demanded independence even before 1947. Later conflicts involved Mizo, Manipuri, Assamese, Bodo, Tripuri and other groups.

The Armed Forces (Special Powers) Act, 1958 (AFSPA) gives the armed forces special powers in areas declared "disturbed", including to search, arrest without warrant and, in some circumstances, use lethal force, with protection from prosecution without central sanction. Human rights groups have long criticised it, and the Justice Jeevan Reddy Committee (2005) recommended its repeal. The Supreme Court in 2016 held that even in disturbed areas, excessive or retaliatory force is not allowed. Since 2022, the government has reduced the areas under AFSPA in Assam, Nagaland, Manipur and Arunachal Pradesh as violence declined, though it remains in force in parts of these states.

Peace has often come through accords. The Mizo Accord of 1986 is the most successful example. The Naga Framework Agreement of 2015 with the NSCN (Isak-Muivah) has not led to a final settlement, mainly because of disagreement over a separate Naga flag and constitution. Recent accords include the Bodo Accord (2020), which expanded the Bodoland Territorial Region; the Bru-Reang agreement (2020) to settle displaced Bru families in Tripura; the Karbi Anglong agreement (2021); the agreement with the pro-talks faction of ULFA (December 2023); and an agreement with Tripura's NLFT and ATTF (2024). Many insurgent groups have laid down arms.

Challenges remain: hardline factions based in Myanmar, such as ULFA (Independent), the unresolved Naga question, inter-state border disputes, and illegal migration fears.

Key takeaways

  • North-East insurgencies stem from identity, migration fears, neglect and porous borders.
  • AFSPA gives the forces special powers; its coverage has been reduced since 2022.
  • Accords with Mizo, Bodo, Karbi, ULFA and Tripura groups have brought peace; the Naga settlement is still pending.

Why it matters for UPSC: North-East insurgency, AFSPA and the accords are frequently asked in GS III internal security.

Practice question: Should AFSPA be repealed in the North-East? Weigh security needs against human rights concerns.
