Missiles and strategic systems

Note 226 of 300 · 2 min read · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

India's missile programme took shape under the Integrated Guided Missile Development Programme (IGMDP), launched in 1983 and led by A. P. J. Abdul Kalam at the Defence Research and Development Organisation (DRDO). It produced five missiles: Prithvi (short-range surface-to-surface), Agni (longer-range ballistic), Akash (medium-range surface-to-air), Trishul (short-range surface-to-air, later shelved) and Nag (anti-tank).

The Agni series forms the land-based leg of India's nuclear deterrent. Agni-V, an intercontinental-range ballistic missile with a range over 5,000 km, was first tested in 2012. In March 2024, in Mission Divyastra, DRDO tested Agni-V with MIRV technology (multiple independently targetable re-entry vehicles), allowing one missile to carry several warheads aimed at different targets. Agni-Prime is a newer, lighter missile; in September 2025 it was test-fired from a rail-based mobile launcher. India's sea-based deterrent rests on nuclear-powered ballistic missile submarines of the Arihant class, armed with K-series missiles.

BrahMos, a supersonic cruise missile developed by a joint venture of DRDO and Russia's NPO Mashinostroyeniya, flies at nearly three times the speed of sound and can be launched from land, ships, submarines and aircraft. Its range has been extended. India signed its first major export deal for BrahMos with the Philippines in 2022. In November 2024, DRDO flight-tested a long-range hypersonic missile, which flies at more than five times the speed of sound.

India also develops missile defence. Its two-tier Ballistic Missile Defence programme intercepts missiles at high and low altitudes, and India operates Russia's S-400 air defence system alongside indigenous systems such as Akash. During Operation Sindoor in May 2025, these air defence systems were widely reported to have intercepted Pakistani drones and missiles.

Key takeaways

  • The IGMDP (1983) produced Prithvi, Agni, Akash and Nag.
  • Agni-V was tested with MIRV technology in March 2024; Agni-Prime was tested from a rail launcher in 2025.
  • BrahMos is a supersonic Indo-Russian cruise missile, exported to the Philippines; India tested a hypersonic missile in 2024.

Why it matters for UPSC: Missile systems and tests are among the most frequently asked defence technology questions in Prelims.

Practice question: How does MIRV technology strengthen India's nuclear deterrence?
