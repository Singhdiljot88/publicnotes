After the Mauryas: Shungas, Kushanas and Satavahanas

Note 007 of 300 · 2 min read · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

The five centuries between the Mauryas and the Guptas are sometimes called a dark age, but they were a time of trade, art and cultural mixing. In the Ganga valley, the Shungas and then the Kanvas ruled smaller kingdoms. In the north-west, a series of rulers came from Central Asia and beyond. The Indo-Greek king Menander (Milinda) is remembered in the Buddhist text Milindapanho, a dialogue with the monk Nagasena. The Indo-Greeks issued the first Indian coins bearing clear portraits of kings.

The Shakas followed. Their western branch ruled Gujarat and Malwa, and Rudradaman's Junagadh inscription of about 150 CE is the first long inscription in classical Sanskrit, recording the repair of the Sudarshana lake. The Kushanas, from Central Asia, built an empire stretching from Central Asia to the Ganga. Kanishka, their best-known king, is traditionally linked to the start of the Shaka era in 78 CE, which India's national calendar uses. He patronised Buddhism and the fourth Buddhist council.

Kushana rule saw two schools of sculpture flourish: the Gandhara school, with Greco-Roman influence and grey schist stone, and the Mathura school, in local red sandstone. Both produced some of the earliest images of the Buddha in human form.

In the Deccan, the Satavahanas ruled from about the first century BCE. Gautamiputra Satakarni, praised in his mother's Nashik inscription, defeated the Shakas. Satavahana kings issued land grants to Brahmins and Buddhist monks, a practice that grew in later centuries.

Key takeaways

  • The period saw invasions from the north-west but also rich trade and cultural exchange.
  • Rudradaman's Junagadh inscription is the first long Sanskrit inscription; the Shaka era begins in 78 CE.
  • Gandhara and Mathura schools of art flourished under the Kushanas.

Why it matters for UPSC: Art schools, inscriptions and the Shaka era are frequent Prelims topics.

Practice question: Compare the Gandhara and Mathura schools of sculpture in style, material and influences.
