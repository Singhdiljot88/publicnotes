The founding of the Congress and the moderate phase

Note 041 of 300 · 1 min read · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

The Indian National Congress held its first session in Bombay in December 1885, with 72 delegates and W. C. Bonnerjee as president. A retired British civil servant, Allan Octavian Hume, helped organise it. Some historians once argued that Hume meant it as a "safety valve" to release Indian discontent harmlessly; most now see the Congress as the product of growing nationalist networks, such as the Indian Association of Surendranath Banerjea and the Poona Sarvajanik Sabha, which used Hume's help to gain official tolerance.

The early leaders, called moderates, included Dadabhai Naoroji, Pherozeshah Mehta, Gopal Krishna Gokhale, Surendranath Banerjea and M. G. Ranade. They believed in British justice and used constitutional methods: petitions, speeches, resolutions and lobbying in Britain. Their demands included more Indian representation in legislative councils, holding the civil service examination in India as well as London, separating the judiciary from the executive, reducing military spending and land revenue, and protecting Indian industry.

Their greatest contribution was economic. Naoroji's drain theory and the work of R. C. Dutt and Ranade exposed the economic exploitation behind British rule, undermining the claim that colonialism benefited India. The moderates also built a national platform that brought together leaders from different regions.

Their achievements in policy were limited: the Indian Councils Act of 1892 slightly enlarged councils and allowed indirect election. Critics, especially younger nationalists, argued that petitioning had failed and that a more assertive approach was needed.

Key takeaways

  • The Congress first met in Bombay in 1885 under W. C. Bonnerjee.
  • Moderates used constitutional methods and trusted British fairness.
  • Their economic critique of colonialism was their most lasting contribution.

Why it matters for UPSC: The founding of the Congress and the moderates' methods and achievements are frequently tested.

Practice question: Evaluate the contribution of the moderates to the Indian national movement.
