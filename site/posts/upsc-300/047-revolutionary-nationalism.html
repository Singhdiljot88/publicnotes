UPSC 300 · 🇮🇳 Theme 5: The freedom struggle · GS I

Revolutionary nationalists

Note 047 of 300 · 2 min read · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

Alongside the Gandhian movement, revolutionary groups believed armed action was needed to end British rule. The first wave emerged after the partition of Bengal. Groups such as the Anushilan Samiti and Jugantar in Bengal and the Abhinav Bharat society founded by V. D. Savarkar in Maharashtra carried out attacks on officials. Khudiram Bose was hanged in 1908, aged eighteen, after a failed attempt on a British magistrate. The Alipore bomb case put Aurobindo Ghosh on trial; he was acquitted.

Abroad, the Ghadar Party was founded in 1913 on the Pacific coast of North America by Indian immigrants, with Lala Har Dayal and Sohan Singh Bhakna among its leaders. During the First World War, it tried to start an uprising in Punjab, which failed. Its newspaper, Ghadar, spread revolutionary ideas among the diaspora.

A second wave followed the end of Non-Cooperation. The Hindustan Republican Association carried out the Kakori train robbery in 1925; Ram Prasad Bismil, Ashfaqulla Khan and others were executed. In 1928 it became the Hindustan Socialist Republican Association (HSRA) under Chandrashekhar Azad and Bhagat Singh, adding socialist goals. Bhagat Singh and Sukhdev, with Rajguru, killed police officer J. P. Saunders in 1928 to avenge Lajpat Rai's death. In April 1929, Bhagat Singh and Batukeshwar Dutt threw harmless bombs in the Central Legislative Assembly and courted arrest to use the trial as propaganda. Bhagat Singh, Sukhdev and Rajguru were hanged on 23 March 1931. Azad died in a gunfight in Allahabad in 1931. In Bengal, Surya Sen led the Chittagong armoury raid in 1930.

The revolutionaries did not overthrow British rule, but their courage inspired many, and Bhagat Singh's writings on socialism and atheism remain widely read.

Key takeaways

  • Early revolutionaries included Anushilan Samiti, Jugantar and Abhinav Bharat; the Ghadar Party was founded abroad in 1913.
  • The HSRA under Azad and Bhagat Singh combined revolution with socialism.
  • Bhagat Singh, Sukhdev and Rajguru were executed on 23 March 1931.

Why it matters for UPSC: Revolutionary organisations, events and leaders appear regularly in Prelims.

Practice question: Assess the contribution of revolutionary nationalism to India's freedom struggle.
