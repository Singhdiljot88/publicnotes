The road to independence and Partition

Note 050 of 300 · 2 min read · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

After the Second World War, the new Labour government in Britain wanted to leave India. Elections in 1945–46 showed that the Congress dominated the general seats and the Muslim League had won almost all Muslim seats, strengthening its demand for Pakistan, first formally made in the Lahore resolution of 1940.

The Cabinet Mission of 1946 proposed a united India with a weak centre and groups of provinces with considerable autonomy. Both parties initially accepted it, but disagreed on its interpretation. The League called for "Direct Action" on 16 August 1946, which led to large-scale communal killings in Calcutta, followed by violence in Noakhali, Bihar and elsewhere. An interim government led by Nehru took office in September 1946, and the Constituent Assembly first met in December 1946, boycotted by the League.

Lord Mountbatten, the last Viceroy, arrived in March 1947. His plan of 3 June 1947 accepted Partition. Punjab and Bengal would be divided, and the date of transfer was brought forward to 15 August 1947. The Indian Independence Act was passed by the British Parliament in July 1947. Princely states were free to join India or Pakistan. Sir Cyril Radcliffe, who had never visited India, drew the boundaries in about five weeks; the awards were announced only on 17 August, after independence.

Partition caused one of the largest migrations in history, with around 15 million people displaced and between several hundred thousand and two million killed, by different estimates. Gandhi spent independence day in Calcutta trying to stop communal violence, and was assassinated on 30 January 1948.

Key takeaways

  • The Cabinet Mission (1946) failed, and Direct Action Day (16 August 1946) triggered communal violence.
  • The Mountbatten Plan (3 June 1947) and the Indian Independence Act led to independence and Partition on 15 August 1947.
  • Partition displaced around 15 million people amid massive violence.

Why it matters for UPSC: The sequence from the Cabinet Mission to the Independence Act is a standard Prelims topic, and Partition is a frequent Mains theme.

Practice question: Was Partition inevitable by 1946? Examine the roles of the Congress, the Muslim League and the British.
