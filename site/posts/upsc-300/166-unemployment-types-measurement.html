Unemployment: types and how India measures it

Note 166 of 300 · 2 min read · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

Unemployment means that people who are willing and able to work cannot find work. Economists distinguish several types. Frictional unemployment is temporary, while people move between jobs. Structural unemployment arises when workers' skills do not match available jobs, often because of technological change. Cyclical unemployment rises in economic downturns. Seasonal unemployment is common in agriculture, where work is concentrated in sowing and harvest seasons. Disguised unemployment, typical in Indian farming, occurs when more people work on a farm than are needed, so removing some would not reduce output; their marginal productivity is near zero. Underemployment means people work fewer hours or in lower-skilled jobs than they want.

India measures employment mainly through the Periodic Labour Force Survey (PLFS), launched by the National Statistics Office in 2017-18. It uses two approaches. The usual status measures a person's main activity over the previous 365 days. The current weekly status (CWS) measures activity in the previous seven days; it captures short-term unemployment and is closer to international practice. Key indicators are the labour force participation rate (the share of people working or seeking work), the worker population ratio, and the unemployment rate (the share of the labour force without work).

From January 2025, the PLFS was revamped to release monthly estimates of unemployment by the current weekly status for both rural and urban areas, and quarterly estimates for rural areas, alongside annual reports. Monthly unemployment for people aged 15 and above has generally been around 5% in 2025 and 2026, with youth unemployment, especially among educated urban youth, much higher.

India's employment challenge is less about open unemployment than about the quality of work: most workers are self-employed or casual, informality is high, and the shift of workers out of agriculture into productive manufacturing jobs has been slow.

Key takeaways

  • Unemployment types include frictional, structural, cyclical, seasonal and disguised.
  • The PLFS measures usual status and current weekly status; monthly bulletins began in 2025.
  • India's main problem is low-quality, informal work and high youth unemployment.

Why it matters for UPSC: Types of unemployment and PLFS concepts are frequently tested, and jobs data appear in many Mains answers.

Practice question: Why does India's unemployment rate appear low despite widespread concern about jobs? Explain.
