Women in Indian society

Note 073 of 300 · 2 min read · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

Women's status in India has improved in many ways since independence, yet deep inequalities remain. The Constitution guarantees equality (Articles 14 and 15) and allows special provisions for women (Article 15(3)). The 73rd and 74th Amendments reserved one-third of seats in panchayats and municipalities for women, and many states have raised this to half. The Constitution (106th Amendment) Act, 2023, the Nari Shakti Vandan Adhiniyam, reserves one-third of seats in the Lok Sabha and state assemblies, but it takes effect only after the next census and delimitation.

Some indicators have improved. According to the National Family Health Survey 2019–21 (NFHS-5), the sex ratio of the total population reached 1,020 women per 1,000 men, though the sex ratio at birth (929 girls per 1,000 boys) still suggests sex-selective practices. Female literacy rose from about 9% in 1951 to 65% in 2011. Girls now enrol in higher education in roughly equal numbers to boys. Female labour force participation, long among the lowest in the world, has risen in recent Periodic Labour Force Surveys, though much of the increase is in self-employment and unpaid family work in rural areas.

Challenges remain severe. Violence against women, including dowry deaths, domestic violence and sexual assault, is widely reported; laws such as the Protection of Women from Domestic Violence Act, 2005, the 2013 criminal law amendments after the Delhi gang rape case, and the Sexual Harassment of Women at Workplace Act, 2013 have strengthened protection, but enforcement is uneven. Women carry most unpaid care work, own a small share of land and are under-represented in politics and senior positions.

Feminist scholars stress that gender intersects with caste, class and religion: a Dalit or Adivasi woman faces different barriers from an urban upper-caste woman.

Key takeaways

  • The Constitution and laws guarantee equality; one-third reservation in local bodies has brought many women into politics.
  • NFHS-5 shows an overall sex ratio of 1,020, but a sex ratio at birth of 929.
  • Violence, unpaid care work and low representation remain major challenges.

Why it matters for UPSC: Women's issues appear every year in GS I society and GS II social justice, and in the essay paper.

Practice question: Why has rising education among Indian women not translated into equally high participation in paid work?
