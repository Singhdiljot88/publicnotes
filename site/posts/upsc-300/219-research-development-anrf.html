Research and development: ANRF and India's innovation system

Note 219 of 300 · 2 min read · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

India's gross expenditure on research and development (GERD) is about 0.64–0.7% of GDP, far below China (over 2%), the United States (over 3%), Israel and South Korea (around 5%). Government accounts for most of India's research spending, through agencies such as the Defence Research and Development Organisation, the Department of Atomic Energy, the Indian Space Research Organisation, the Council of Scientific and Industrial Research and the Indian Council of Agricultural Research. Private companies contribute a much smaller share than in leading research economies, where industry funds most research.

India's research output has grown. It is now among the top three countries in the number of scientific publications and has risen in the Global Innovation Index, reaching 38th place in 2025 according to WIPO. But concerns include low numbers of researchers per million people, weak links between universities and industry, slow research funding and the quality of some publications.

The Anusandhan National Research Foundation (ANRF) was established by an act of Parliament in 2023, replacing the Science and Engineering Research Board. Chaired by the Prime Minister, it aims to fund research in universities and colleges, including those that have had little research funding, and to mobilise private and philanthropic money. The planned budget was about Rs 50,000 crore over five years, with a large share expected from non-government sources.

In July 2025, the Cabinet approved the Research, Development and Innovation (RDI) scheme with a corpus of Rs 1 lakh crore to give long-term, low-cost or zero-interest finance to private companies for research in strategic and sunrise sectors. Other programmes include the Atal Innovation Mission (2016), which set up Atal Tinkering Labs in schools and incubators, and the Prime Minister's Research Fellowship for doctoral students.

Key takeaways

  • India spends about 0.64–0.7% of GDP on R&D, mostly by government.
  • The ANRF (2023) replaced SERB to fund university research and attract private money.
  • The RDI scheme (2025) created a Rs 1 lakh crore corpus for private sector research.

Why it matters for UPSC: India's research ecosystem is a recurring GS III question, often linked to innovation and self-reliance.

Practice question: Why is private sector investment in R&D low in India? How can it be raised?
