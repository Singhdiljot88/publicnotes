UPSC 300 · 🤝 Theme 15: Social justice & welfare · GS II

Social security, the labour codes and gig workers

Note 150 of 300 · 2 min read · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

More than 80% of India's workers are in informal employment, without written contracts, paid leave or social security. Social security has traditionally covered mainly formal-sector workers through the Employees' Provident Fund Organisation (EPFO) and the Employees' State Insurance Corporation (ESIC).

Parliament passed four labour codes in 2019–2020 to consolidate 29 central labour laws: the Code on Wages (2019), the Industrial Relations Code (2020), the Code on Social Security (2020), and the Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions Code (2020). After years of delay while states framed rules, the government brought all four into force on 21 November 2025. The codes introduce a universal minimum wage and a national floor wage, a uniform definition of wages, fixed-term employment, and higher thresholds for government permission for layoffs (from 100 to 300 workers). The Code on Social Security, for the first time, recognises gig workers and platform workers, such as delivery and ride-hailing workers, and provides for social security funds partly financed by aggregators. Trade unions have criticised the codes for weakening job security and the right to strike.

Other measures include the e-Shram portal, launched in 2021 to register unorganised workers, which has over 30 crore registrations; the Pradhan Mantri Shram Yogi Maan-dhan pension scheme for unorganised workers; the Atal Pension Yojana; the PM Jeevan Jyoti Bima and PM Suraksha Bima life and accident insurance schemes; and old-age, widow and disability pensions under the National Social Assistance Programme. Several states, starting with Rajasthan in 2023 and Karnataka in 2025, have passed laws on the welfare of platform-based gig workers.

The challenge is to extend real, portable protection to informal and gig workers without making formal job creation too costly.

Key takeaways

  • Over 80% of workers are informal; social security long covered only the formal sector.
  • The four labour codes, consolidating 29 laws, came into force on 21 November 2025, and recognise gig workers.
  • e-Shram has over 30 crore registrations; states such as Rajasthan and Karnataka have gig worker laws.

Why it matters for UPSC: Labour codes and gig worker welfare are major current topics in GS II and GS III.

Practice question: Do the new labour codes strike the right balance between flexibility for employers and protection for workers?
