The Himalayas

Note 092 of 300 · 2 min read · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

The Himalayas are the youngest and highest mountain system in the world, formed by the collision of the Indian and Eurasian plates. They stretch about 2,400 km in an arc from the Indus in the west to the Brahmaputra in the east, and are 150–400 km wide.

From north to south, the Himalayas have three parallel ranges. The Greater Himalayas or Himadri contain the highest peaks, with an average height of about 6,000 m: Mount Everest (8,849 m, on the Nepal-China border), Kangchenjunga (8,586 m, India's highest peak, in Sikkim on the Nepal border), Nanda Devi and others. The Lesser Himalayas or Himachal include the Pir Panjal and Dhauladhar ranges and hill stations such as Shimla, Mussoorie and Darjeeling. The Shiwaliks or Outer Himalayas are the lowest and youngest, made of unconsolidated sediments; the flat valleys between them and the Lesser Himalayas are called duns, such as Dehradun. North of the Greater Himalayas lie the Trans-Himalayan ranges: the Karakoram (with K2, 8,611 m, in territory administered by Pakistan and claimed by India), Ladakh and Zaskar.

The Himalayas are also divided from west to east into the Punjab (or Kashmir and Himachal), Kumaon, Nepal and Assam Himalayas. In the east, the Purvanchal hills, including the Patkai, Naga, Mizo and Garo-Khasi-Jaintia hills, curve along India's border with Myanmar.

The Himalayas shape India's climate by blocking cold Central Asian winds and forcing the monsoon to shed its rain. They feed perennial rivers through glaciers and snowmelt, hold rich forests and biodiversity, and form a strategic barrier. But they are geologically fragile: earthquakes, landslides, glacial lake outburst floods and the effects of road and dam building are growing concerns, as the Joshimath land subsidence of 2023 showed.

Key takeaways

  • The Himalayas have three parallel ranges: Himadri, Himachal and Shiwaliks.
  • Kangchenjunga (8,586 m) is India's highest peak; Everest is 8,849 m.
  • They shape climate and rivers but are fragile and disaster-prone.

Why it matters for UPSC: Himalayan ranges, passes, peaks and their significance are among the most frequently tested topics.

Practice question: Why is development in the Himalayas a particular challenge? Suggest a sustainable approach.
