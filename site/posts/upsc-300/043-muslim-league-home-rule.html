The Muslim League, separate electorates and Home Rule

Note 043 of 300 · 2 min read · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

The All-India Muslim League was founded in Dhaka in December 1906, led by the Aga Khan and Nawab Salimullah of Dhaka, among others. Earlier that year, a delegation of Muslim leaders (the Simla deputation) had asked Viceroy Minto for separate representation. The Indian Councils Act of 1909, known as the Morley-Minto reforms, enlarged the legislative councils and introduced separate electorates for Muslims, in which only Muslims could vote for Muslim candidates. Nationalists saw this as a deliberate attempt to divide communities, and separate electorates became a long-term source of communal politics.

Relations between the Congress and the League improved during the First World War. In the Lucknow Pact of 1916, the two agreed on a joint scheme of reforms, and the Congress accepted separate electorates. The same session reunited the moderates and extremists in the Congress. Tilak and Muhammad Ali Jinnah were key figures in this cooperation.

In 1916 two Home Rule Leagues were also founded: one by Tilak in April, working in Maharashtra, Karnataka and central India, and one by the Theosophist Annie Besant in September, working elsewhere. They demanded self-government within the British Empire, on the lines of Ireland's Home Rule movement, and spread nationalist ideas through newspapers, discussion groups and libraries, reaching new areas and younger people. Besant's internment in 1917 increased their popularity.

The British responded with the Montagu Declaration of August 1917, promising "the progressive realisation of responsible government in India". This led to the Montagu-Chelmsford reforms and the Government of India Act 1919, which introduced dyarchy in the provinces.

Key takeaways

  • The Muslim League was founded in 1906; the 1909 reforms introduced separate electorates.
  • The Lucknow Pact (1916) brought the Congress and League together.
  • Tilak's and Besant's Home Rule Leagues (1916) spread nationalism and prompted the 1917 Montagu Declaration.

Why it matters for UPSC: Separate electorates, the Lucknow Pact and the Home Rule movement are high-frequency Prelims topics.

Practice question: How did the introduction of separate electorates in 1909 shape communal politics in India?
