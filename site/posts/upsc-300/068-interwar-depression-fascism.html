The interwar years: the Great Depression and fascism

Note 068 of 300 · 2 min read · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

The 1920s began with recovery in many countries, but ended in disaster. In October 1929, the New York stock market crashed. Banks failed, credit collapsed and world trade shrank by about two-thirds over the following years. Unemployment in the United States reached about a quarter of the workforce. Countries raised tariffs to protect their industries, which deepened the slump. Prices of agricultural goods collapsed, hurting farmers in colonies such as India.

Governments responded in different ways. In the United States, President Franklin D. Roosevelt's New Deal used public works, social security and banking regulation. The economist John Maynard Keynes argued that governments should increase spending during depressions, ideas that shaped economic policy for decades.

Elsewhere, the crisis strengthened authoritarian movements. Fascism, which glorified the nation and the state, rejected democracy and liberalism, and used violence against opponents, first took power in Italy, where Benito Mussolini became prime minister in 1922. In Germany, the Nazi Party of Adolf Hitler exploited resentment over the Treaty of Versailles, economic misery and fear of communism. Hitler became chancellor in January 1933 and quickly established a dictatorship. The Nazis persecuted Jews through laws such as the Nuremberg Laws of 1935, rearmed Germany in violation of Versailles, and remilitarised the Rhineland in 1936. Japan's militarist government invaded Manchuria in 1931.

The League of Nations failed to stop aggression: it could not prevent Italy's conquest of Ethiopia in 1935–36 or Japan's expansion in China. Britain and France followed a policy of appeasement, culminating in the Munich Agreement of 1938, which allowed Germany to annex the Sudetenland from Czechoslovakia.

Key takeaways

  • The 1929 crash led to the Great Depression, mass unemployment and collapsing trade.
  • Fascism rose in Italy (1922) and Nazism in Germany (1933), exploiting economic crisis and resentment.
  • The League of Nations and appeasement failed to stop aggression.

Why it matters for UPSC: The Depression and the rise of fascism help explain the Second World War and modern debates on economic policy and democracy.

Practice question: How did the Great Depression contribute to the rise of fascism in Europe?
