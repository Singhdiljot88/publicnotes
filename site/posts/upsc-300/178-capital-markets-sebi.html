Capital markets and SEBI

Note 178 of 300 · 2 min read · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

Capital markets are where long-term funds are raised and traded. The primary market is where companies issue new shares or bonds to raise money, for example through an initial public offering (IPO). The secondary market is where existing securities are traded among investors, on stock exchanges such as the Bombay Stock Exchange (BSE, founded in 1875, Asia's oldest) and the National Stock Exchange (NSE, set up in 1992). The money market, by contrast, deals in short-term instruments such as treasury bills, commercial paper and certificates of deposit.

The Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI) regulates the securities market. It was set up in 1988 and given statutory powers by the SEBI Act, 1992, after a major securities scam. Its mandate is to protect investors, develop the market and regulate intermediaries such as brokers, mutual funds and portfolio managers. It can investigate and penalise insider trading, market manipulation and fraud.

India's markets have deepened considerably. Electronic trading and dematerialised shares replaced paper certificates in the 1990s. India moved to T+1 settlement, in which trades are settled one trading day after they take place, completing the transition in January 2023, one of the fastest cycles among major markets. Mutual fund investment, especially through systematic investment plans (SIPs), has grown rapidly, and the number of retail investors has multiplied. Foreign portfolio investors (FPIs) invest large sums, and their movements can cause volatility and affect the rupee.

Bond markets are less developed: the corporate bond market is shallow, and government securities dominate. Concerns include speculative trading in equity derivatives by retail investors, most of whom lose money according to SEBI's own studies, which led SEBI to tighten rules on futures and options from late 2024. Other concerns include governance in listed companies, the role of credit rating agencies, and protecting small investors.

Key takeaways

  • The primary market raises new capital; the secondary market trades existing securities.
  • SEBI (1988, statutory in 1992) regulates securities markets to protect investors.
  • India moved to T+1 settlement in 2023; SEBI tightened derivatives rules after finding most retail traders lose money.

Why it matters for UPSC: Capital market concepts and SEBI's role are regular Prelims and Mains topics.

Practice question: Why is a deep corporate bond market important for India's infrastructure financing?
