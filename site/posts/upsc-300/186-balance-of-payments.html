The balance of payments

Note 186 of 300 · 2 min read · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

The balance of payments (BoP) records all economic transactions between residents of a country and the rest of the world over a period. It has two main parts.

The current account records trade in goods (merchandise exports and imports, giving the trade balance), trade in services (such as software, business services, travel and transport), primary income (such as interest, dividends and wages earned across borders) and secondary income or transfers (mainly remittances sent home by workers abroad). India typically runs a large deficit in goods trade, because it imports crude oil, gold, electronics and other items, but a large surplus in services and remittances. India is the world's largest recipient of remittances: they reached a record of about 135 billion dollars in 2024-25, according to RBI data. As a result, India's current account deficit (CAD) has generally stayed modest, mostly between about 1% and 2% of GDP in recent years, and occasionally in surplus.

The capital account (in India's presentation, the capital and financial account) records flows of investment and borrowing: foreign direct investment, foreign portfolio investment, external commercial borrowings, banking capital including NRI deposits, and external assistance. A current account deficit must be financed by net capital inflows or by drawing down foreign exchange reserves. Errors and omissions balance the accounts.

If the overall balance is positive, reserves rise; if negative, they fall. India's foreign exchange reserves, managed by the RBI, provide a buffer against shocks; they stood at over 700 billion dollars for much of 2026, though they fell during periods of rupee pressure.

India's worst BoP crisis came in 1991, when reserves fell to a few weeks of imports. Since then, a CAD of around 2.5–3% of GDP has generally been seen as the comfortable limit.

Key takeaways

  • The current account covers goods, services, income and remittances; the capital account covers investment and borrowing.
  • India has a large goods deficit offset by services exports and record remittances of about 135 billion dollars in 2024-25.
  • Foreign exchange reserves, over 700 billion dollars for much of 2026, act as a buffer.

Why it matters for UPSC: BoP components and India's external position are frequently tested in Prelims and Mains.

Practice question: Why does India run a current account deficit, and how sustainable is it?
