Buddhism and Jainism: why new religions emerged

Note 005 of 300 · 2 min read · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

The sixth and fifth centuries BCE saw many new teachers challenge Vedic ritualism. Two of their movements, Buddhism and Jainism, became lasting religions. Historians link their rise to a changing society: growing cities and trade, a new merchant class, the costs of animal sacrifice to farmers, and dissatisfaction with the dominance of priests.

Siddhartha Gautama, the Buddha, was born at Lumbini in present-day Nepal. Traditional dates place his life around 563–483 BCE, though many scholars now prefer later dates. He attained enlightenment at Bodh Gaya, gave his first sermon at Sarnath and died at Kushinagar. He taught the Four Noble Truths, that suffering exists, has a cause in craving, can end, and that the path to its end is the Eightfold Path. He preached in Pali and other spoken languages rather than Sanskrit and founded a sangha open to people of all varnas, including women.

Vardhamana Mahavira, the 24th tirthankara of Jainism, taught a stricter path of five vows: non-violence (ahimsa), truth, non-stealing, non-possession and celibacy. The goal is liberation of the soul through right faith, right knowledge and right conduct, the three jewels. Jainism later split into the Digambara and Shvetambara sects.

Buddhist councils shaped the tradition. The first was held at Rajagriha after the Buddha's death, the third at Pataliputra under Ashoka, and the fourth in Kashmir under Kanishka, when the Mahayana school grew in importance.

Key takeaways

  • Social and economic change, not only spiritual seeking, explains the rise of new religions.
  • The Buddha taught the Four Noble Truths and the Eightfold Path; Mahavira taught five vows and the three jewels.
  • Both rejected the authority of Vedic ritual and taught in spoken languages.

Why it matters for UPSC: Places linked to the Buddha, the councils and the core teachings are recurring Prelims questions; their social context suits Mains.

Practice question: How did the material conditions of the sixth century BCE contribute to the rise of Buddhism and Jainism?
