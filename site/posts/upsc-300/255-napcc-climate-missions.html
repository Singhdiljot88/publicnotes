India's National Action Plan on Climate Change

Note 255 of 300 · 2 min read · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

India released its National Action Plan on Climate Change (NAPCC) in 2008. It set out eight national missions, each run by a ministry, to address both mitigation and adaptation.

The National Solar Mission, launched in 2010 as the Jawaharlal Nehru National Solar Mission, aimed to make solar power competitive; its target was raised to 100 GW by 2022, and solar has since become India's fastest-growing source of power. The National Mission for Enhanced Energy Efficiency created market-based tools such as the Perform, Achieve and Trade (PAT) scheme for energy-intensive industries. The National Mission on Sustainable Habitat promotes energy efficiency in buildings, urban transport and waste management. The National Water Mission aims to increase water use efficiency by 20%. The National Mission for Sustaining the Himalayan Ecosystem focuses on glaciers and mountain ecology. The National Mission for a Green India aims to increase forest and tree cover and improve forest quality. The National Mission for Sustainable Agriculture promotes climate-resilient farming. The National Mission on Strategic Knowledge for Climate Change builds research capacity. Additional missions, on human health and coastal areas, were later proposed.

States were asked to prepare State Action Plans on Climate Change, and most did so, though many lack funding and integration with state budgets.

Other measures include the Energy Conservation (Amendment) Act, 2022, which enabled a domestic carbon market; the National Green Hydrogen Mission (2023), which aims for production capacity of 5 million tonnes a year by 2030; the PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana (2024) for rooftop solar on one crore homes; and the National Adaptation Fund for Climate Change. India also founded the International Solar Alliance with France in 2015, headquartered in Gurugram, and the Coalition for Disaster Resilient Infrastructure in 2019.

Critics note that the missions have uneven progress, and that adaptation, especially for agriculture, health and cities, has received less money than mitigation.

Key takeaways

  • The NAPCC (2008) has eight national missions, including solar, energy efficiency, water and the Himalayas.
  • States have climate action plans, but many lack funding.
  • Newer initiatives include the Green Hydrogen Mission, rooftop solar and the International Solar Alliance.

Why it matters for UPSC: The NAPCC missions are a standard Prelims question, and their effectiveness is a common Mains theme.

Practice question: Has the NAPCC been effective in addressing climate change in India? Suggest improvements.
