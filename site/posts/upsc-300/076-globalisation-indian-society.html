Globalisation and Indian society

Note 076 of 300 · 1 min read · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

Globalisation refers to the growing integration of economies, cultures and societies through trade, investment, migration, technology and media. For India, it accelerated after the 1991 economic reforms and the spread of satellite television, mobile phones and the internet.

Its effects on society are mixed. On the positive side, globalisation created new jobs in information technology, business process outsourcing and services, expanded the middle class, increased access to consumer goods and information, and brought new opportunities for women in urban workplaces. Indian culture also spread abroad through cinema, food, yoga and the diaspora, which is the largest in the world at over 3 crore people.

On the negative side, critics argue that the benefits were unevenly distributed, widening inequality between cities and villages, skilled and unskilled workers, and regions. Traditional artisans and small producers faced competition from imports and large firms. Consumerism grew, and some worry that global media erode local languages and cultures. Informal and contract work increased, reducing job security.

Sociologists also describe "glocalisation", in which global products and ideas are adapted to local contexts, such as global fast-food chains offering vegetarian menus in India, and Indian traditions such as festivals and weddings incorporating global trends. Rather than replacing Indian culture, globalisation has often produced hybrid forms. Its effects on family structure, gender roles, caste and religion vary across regions and classes.

Key takeaways

  • Globalisation accelerated in India after 1991 and with new media and technology.
  • It created jobs and a larger middle class, but also inequality and job insecurity.
  • Glocalisation shows that culture adapts rather than simply disappears.

Why it matters for UPSC: The social effects of globalisation are a standard GS I question, often asked about women, culture or family.

Practice question: Has globalisation led to cultural homogenisation in India? Discuss with examples.
