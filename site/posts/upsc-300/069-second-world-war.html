The Second World War

Note 069 of 300 · 2 min read · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

The Second World War began on 1 September 1939, when Germany invaded Poland; Britain and France declared war two days later. Germany conquered much of Europe by 1940, including France, using fast-moving "blitzkrieg" tactics. Britain held out under Winston Churchill, winning the air Battle of Britain. In June 1941 Germany invaded the Soviet Union. On 7 December 1941, Japan attacked the US naval base at Pearl Harbor, bringing the United States into the war.

The tide turned in 1942–43. The Soviet victory at Stalingrad (1942–43) was a turning point in Europe, and the US victory at Midway (1942) in the Pacific. Allied forces landed in Normandy on 6 June 1944 (D-Day). Germany surrendered on 8 May 1945. The United States dropped atomic bombs on Hiroshima (6 August 1945) and Nagasaki (9 August), and Japan surrendered on 15 August, formally signing on 2 September 1945.

The war killed an estimated 70 to 85 million people, the majority of them civilians. The Nazis murdered about six million Jews in the Holocaust, along with millions of others. Over 2.5 million Indians served in the British Indian Army, the largest volunteer army in history, fighting in North Africa, Italy and South-East Asia; the battles of Imphal and Kohima in 1944 stopped the Japanese advance into India. The Bengal famine of 1943 killed around three million people.

The war's consequences shaped the modern world. The United Nations was founded in 1945. The United States and the Soviet Union emerged as superpowers. European empires were weakened, speeding decolonisation, including India's independence. The Nuremberg and Tokyo trials established the principle of individual responsibility for war crimes.

Key takeaways

  • The war began with Germany's invasion of Poland on 1 September 1939 and ended in 1945.
  • Stalingrad and Midway were turning points; atomic bombs were used on Hiroshima and Nagasaki.
  • The war led to the UN, the superpower rivalry and the end of European empires.

Why it matters for UPSC: The Second World War's course and consequences, and India's part in it, are frequent exam topics.

Practice question: How did the Second World War accelerate decolonisation in Asia and Africa?
