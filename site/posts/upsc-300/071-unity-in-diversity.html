Unity in diversity: the shape of Indian society

Note 071 of 300 · 2 min read · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

India is one of the most diverse societies in the world. The 2011 Census recorded more than 19,500 mother tongues, which were rationalised into 121 languages with 10,000 or more speakers each; 22 are listed in the Eighth Schedule of the Constitution. Hindi is the largest mother tongue, spoken by about 44% of people. India is home to followers of all major religions: in 2011, about 79.8% of the population was Hindu, 14.2% Muslim, 2.3% Christian, 1.7% Sikh, 0.7% Buddhist and 0.4% Jain. There are over 700 notified Scheduled Tribes. Regional cultures, cuisines, festivals and dress vary enormously.

Sociologists have long asked what holds this diversity together. Several forces are usually identified. Geography gives the subcontinent a natural unity bounded by mountains and seas. A shared civilisational history, pilgrimage networks linking distant regions, and traditions such as Bhakti and Sufism created cultural bonds. The freedom struggle built a common political identity. Today, the Constitution, a single citizenship, democratic institutions, a national market, the Indian Railways, cinema, cricket and the internet all act as unifying forces.

Diversity also creates tensions: disputes over language, religious conflict, caste discrimination, regional demands and inter-state disputes over water. The Indian approach has been to accommodate rather than suppress diversity, through linguistic states, minority rights under Articles 29 and 30, special provisions for some regions, and federalism.

The phrase "unity in diversity" is sometimes criticised as a slogan that hides inequality. A more careful view is that unity in India is not uniformity; it is an ongoing negotiation in which democratic institutions play the central role.

Key takeaways

  • India has 121 languages with over 10,000 speakers, 22 scheduled languages and all major religions.
  • Geography, history, the freedom struggle and the Constitution are key unifying forces.
  • India accommodates diversity through federalism, linguistic states and minority rights.

Why it matters for UPSC: Diversity and national integration are recurring GS I themes, often asked in Mains essay and society questions.

Practice question: "Unity in India is not uniformity." Discuss the role of constitutional institutions in sustaining unity amid diversity.
