The science of climate change

Note 251 of 300 · 2 min read · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

The Earth's climate is warming because human activities have increased the concentration of greenhouse gases in the atmosphere. Carbon dioxide, released mainly by burning coal, oil and gas and by deforestation, is the most important. Its concentration has risen from about 280 parts per million before the industrial revolution to over 420 ppm today. Methane, from agriculture, livestock, landfills and fossil fuel leaks, is far more potent over short periods. Nitrous oxide, from fertilisers, and fluorinated gases add to the effect.

The Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (IPCC), set up in 1988 by the World Meteorological Organization and the UN Environment Programme, assesses the science. Its Sixth Assessment Report (2021–23) concluded that it is "unequivocal" that human influence has warmed the atmosphere, ocean and land. Global average temperature in 2011–2020 was about 1.1°C above the 1850–1900 average. The World Meteorological Organization reported that 2024 was the warmest year on record and the first calendar year more than 1.5°C above pre-industrial levels, though the Paris Agreement's 1.5°C limit refers to long-term averages over decades, not single years.

The effects are already visible: more frequent and intense heatwaves, heavier rainfall events, rising sea levels (from melting glaciers and ice sheets and the expansion of warming water), retreating Himalayan glaciers, ocean acidification and coral bleaching, and shifts in growing seasons. Some changes risk crossing tipping points, such as the loss of ice sheets or the dieback of the Amazon, which could be irreversible.

India is highly vulnerable because of its long coastline, dependence on the monsoon and agriculture, large poor population and Himalayan glaciers that feed major rivers. India's own climate assessment, released by the Ministry of Earth Sciences in 2020, found that average temperature in India had risen by about 0.7°C between 1901 and 2018.

Key takeaways

  • Human emissions have raised CO2 from about 280 ppm to over 420 ppm.
  • The IPCC's AR6 called human influence on warming "unequivocal"; 2024 was the first calendar year above 1.5°C.
  • India is highly exposed through heat, monsoon change, sea-level rise and glacier retreat.

Why it matters for UPSC: Climate science basics, the IPCC and greenhouse gases are standard Prelims questions and the foundation for Mains answers.

Practice question: Why is India particularly vulnerable to climate change? Discuss with examples.
