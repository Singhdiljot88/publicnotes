The Union Budget: funds, process and the 2026-27 numbers

Note 181 of 300 · 2 min read · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

Article 112 of the Constitution requires the President to lay before Parliament an "annual financial statement" of estimated receipts and expenditure; the word "budget" does not appear in the Constitution. The Union Budget is usually presented by the Finance Minister on 1 February, a practice adopted in 2017 so that it can be passed before the financial year begins on 1 April. The Railway Budget was merged with the Union Budget the same year.

Government money is held in three accounts. The Consolidated Fund of India (Article 266) receives all revenues, loans raised and loan repayments; no money can be withdrawn from it without a law passed by Parliament. The Contingency Fund of India (Article 267) is an imprest at the disposal of the President for urgent, unforeseen spending, later recouped through parliamentary approval. The Public Account holds money the government receives as a banker or trustee, such as provident funds and small savings deposits; withdrawals do not need parliamentary approval.

Some expenditure is "charged" on the Consolidated Fund and is not voted on, such as the salaries of the President, judges of the Supreme Court and the CAG, and interest on debt. The rest is voted through demands for grants, examined by departmental standing committees. Parliament then passes the Appropriation Bill, authorising withdrawals, and the Finance Bill, giving effect to tax proposals. A vote on account allows spending for part of the year when the full budget cannot be passed in time, usually in election years.

The Budget for 2026-27, presented on 1 February 2026, estimated total expenditure of about Rs 53.5 lakh crore and capital expenditure of about Rs 12.2 lakh crore. It set the fiscal deficit target at 4.3% of GDP, down from a revised 4.4% in 2025-26, and projected the Union government's debt at 55.6% of GDP. The new Income-tax Act, 2025 took effect from April 2026.

Key takeaways

  • Article 112 requires an annual financial statement; the Budget is presented on 1 February.
  • The Consolidated Fund needs parliamentary approval for withdrawals; the Contingency Fund and Public Account work differently.
  • Budget 2026-27: spending about Rs 53.5 lakh crore, capex Rs 12.2 lakh crore, fiscal deficit 4.3% of GDP.

Why it matters for UPSC: The budget process and government funds are high-frequency Prelims questions, and the latest numbers are used in Mains.

Practice question: How effective is parliamentary scrutiny of the Union Budget? Suggest ways to strengthen it.
