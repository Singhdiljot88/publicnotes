GM crops: Bt cotton, GM mustard and the debate

Note 213 of 300 · 2 min read · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

Genetically modified (GM) crops contain genes from another organism, introduced to give them a new trait. The best-known example in India is Bt cotton, which carries a gene from the soil bacterium Bacillus thuringiensis that produces a protein toxic to bollworms. Approved in 2002, it is the only GM crop legally cultivated in India and now covers over 90% of the country's cotton area. It initially raised yields and cut pesticide use, helping India become one of the world's largest cotton producers. Later, pink bollworms developed resistance, and illegal herbicide-tolerant cotton has spread.

GM crops are regulated under rules made under the Environment (Protection) Act, 1986. The Genetic Engineering Appraisal Committee (GEAC), under the Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change, approves field trials and commercial release. Bt brinjal was approved by GEAC in 2009, but the government imposed an indefinite moratorium in 2010 after public consultations raised safety concerns.

GM mustard, DMH-11, was developed by Delhi University scientists using a genetic system to make hybrid seeds easier to produce, which could raise yields. GEAC approved its environmental release in 2022. The approval was challenged in the Supreme Court, and in July 2024 a two-judge bench gave a split verdict; it asked the government to frame a national policy on GM crops after consultation.

Supporters argue that GM crops can raise yields, reduce pesticide use and help India cut its large edible oil imports. Opponents cite risks to biodiversity and health, the spread of herbicide resistance, contamination of non-GM crops, farmers' dependence on seed companies, and the lack of independent long-term safety studies. India imports GM soybean oil and allows GM cotton seed oil for consumption, which supporters say shows the inconsistency of the current policy.

Key takeaways

  • Bt cotton (2002) is India's only approved GM crop and covers over 90% of cotton area.
  • GEAC regulates GM crops; Bt brinjal has been under moratorium since 2010.
  • GM mustard DMH-11 was approved in 2022, but the Supreme Court gave a split verdict in 2024.

Why it matters for UPSC: GM crops are a recurring GS III topic linking science, agriculture, environment and regulation.

Practice question: Should India allow commercial cultivation of GM food crops? Argue with evidence.
