Recent climate summits: from Dubai to Belém

Note 253 of 300 · 2 min read · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

COP28 in Dubai (2023) concluded the first global stocktake under the Paris Agreement. For the first time, countries agreed to "transition away from fossil fuels in energy systems, in a just, orderly and equitable manner", and to triple renewable energy capacity and double the rate of energy efficiency improvement by 2030. The Loss and Damage Fund, agreed in principle at COP27 in Sharm el-Sheikh in 2022 to help vulnerable countries cope with climate impacts they cannot adapt to, was made operational, with initial pledges of several hundred million dollars, small compared with estimated needs.

COP29 in Baku, Azerbaijan (2024), was called the "finance COP". It agreed a New Collective Quantified Goal (NCQG) on climate finance: developed countries are to take the lead in mobilising at least 300 billion US dollars a year for developing countries by 2035, within a wider aim of 1.3 trillion dollars a year from all sources. India strongly criticised the outcome as inadequate and objected to the way it was adopted. COP29 also finalised rules for carbon markets under Article 6.

COP30 in Belém, Brazil (2025), held on the edge of the Amazon, produced a package of decisions on adaptation, carbon markets and finance. It adopted indicators for the Global Goal on Adaptation, set a goal to scale up adaptation finance, and adopted a decision on the just transition work programme. Proposals for a roadmap on moving away from fossil fuels did not make it into the formal outcome. India worked through the BASIC group (Brazil, South Africa, India and China) and the Like-Minded Developing Countries, stressing equity, finance and opposition to unilateral trade measures linked to climate, such as the EU's Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism.

COP31 is being held in Antalya, Türkiye, in November 2026, under an unusual arrangement in which Australia leads the negotiations.

Key takeaways

  • COP28 (2023) agreed to transition away from fossil fuels and operationalised the Loss and Damage Fund.
  • COP29 (2024) set a climate finance goal of 300 billion dollars a year by 2035, criticised by India.
  • COP30 (2025) focused on adaptation and implementation; COP31 is in Antalya in November 2026.

Why it matters for UPSC: Recent COP outcomes are frequently asked in Prelims and help Mains answers on climate diplomacy.

Practice question: Why do developing countries consider climate finance commitments inadequate? Discuss with reference to recent COPs.
