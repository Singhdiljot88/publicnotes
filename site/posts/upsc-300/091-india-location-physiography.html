India's location and physiographic divisions

Note 091 of 300 · 2 min read · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

India lies entirely in the northern hemisphere, between about 8°4' N and 37°6' N latitude and 68°7' E and 97°25' E longitude. The Tropic of Cancer (23°30' N) passes through eight states: Gujarat, Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh, Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, West Bengal, Tripura and Mizoram. India's area is about 3.28 million square km, the seventh largest in the world, and it has a land border of about 15,200 km and a coastline of about 7,500 km including the islands. Indian Standard Time is based on 82°30' E, which passes near Mirzapur in Uttar Pradesh, five and a half hours ahead of Greenwich Mean Time.

India is commonly divided into six physiographic regions. The Himalayas and other northern mountains form a young fold mountain system. The Northern Plains, built by the Indus, Ganga and Brahmaputra and their tributaries, are among the most fertile and densely populated regions in the world. The Peninsular Plateau, made of ancient, stable rocks, includes the Deccan plateau, the Central Highlands and the Chotanagpur plateau, and is rich in minerals. The Indian Desert, the Thar, lies west of the Aravalli hills. The Coastal Plains run along the Arabian Sea (narrower western plains) and the Bay of Bengal (wider eastern plains with large deltas). The Islands include the Andaman and Nicobar Islands, which are the tops of submerged mountains, and Lakshadweep, which are coral islands.

India's location at the head of the Indian Ocean, between West Asia and South-East Asia, has made it a crossroads of trade and culture for thousands of years. Its southernmost point, Indira Point on Great Nicobar, was submerged in the 2004 tsunami.

Key takeaways

  • India lies between about 8°4' N and 37°6' N; the Tropic of Cancer crosses eight states.
  • Indian Standard Time is based on 82°30' E.
  • Six physiographic regions: Himalayas, Northern Plains, Peninsular Plateau, Thar, coastal plains and islands.

Why it matters for UPSC: Location facts and physiographic divisions are foundational for every Indian geography question.

Practice question: How does India's location influence its climate, trade and strategic importance?
