Chandrayaan, Mangalyaan and Aditya: India's science missions

Note 222 of 300 · 2 min read · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

India's planetary missions have achieved notable firsts at low cost. Chandrayaan-1, launched in 2008, carried an impactor and international instruments, including NASA's Moon Mineralogy Mapper, which helped confirm the presence of water molecules on the lunar surface. The Mars Orbiter Mission (Mangalyaan), launched in November 2013, entered Mars orbit in September 2014, making India the first Asian country to reach Mars and the first country to do so on its first attempt, at a cost of about Rs 450 crore. Chandrayaan-2 (2019) placed an orbiter around the Moon that is still working, but its Vikram lander crashed during the final descent.

Chandrayaan-3 succeeded where its predecessor failed. Its lander Vikram touched down near the Moon's south pole on 23 August 2023, making India the fourth country to soft-land on the Moon and the first to land near the south polar region. The landing site was named Shiv Shakti point, and 23 August is now observed as National Space Day. The Pragyan rover confirmed the presence of sulphur and other elements in the lunar soil. The south pole interests scientists because permanently shadowed craters may hold water ice, a potential resource for future missions.

Aditya-L1, India's first solar observatory, was launched in September 2023 and placed in a halo orbit around the first Sun-Earth Lagrange point (L1), about 1.5 million km from Earth, in January 2024. From there it observes the Sun continuously, studying the corona, solar wind and flares, which affect satellites and power grids on Earth. AstroSat (2015) was India's first dedicated space astronomy observatory, and XPoSat (2024) studies X-ray polarisation.

Approved future missions include Chandrayaan-4, a lunar sample-return mission; a joint lunar polar mission with Japan (LUPEX, also called Chandrayaan-5); and the Venus Orbiter Mission.

Key takeaways

  • Chandrayaan-1 helped confirm water on the Moon; Mangalyaan reached Mars on the first attempt in 2014.
  • Chandrayaan-3 landed near the lunar south pole on 23 August 2023, now National Space Day.
  • Aditya-L1 studies the Sun from the L1 point; Chandrayaan-4 will return lunar samples.

Why it matters for UPSC: India's science missions are among the most frequently asked science and technology questions.

Practice question: Why is the lunar south pole important for future space exploration?
