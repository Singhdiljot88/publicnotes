Animal husbandry, dairy and fisheries

Note 199 of 300 · 2 min read · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

Livestock and fisheries have become the fastest-growing parts of the farm economy, and they matter especially to landless and small households, for whom animals are a source of income and insurance. Their growth has been faster than that of crops for many years, and they now account for a large share of the value of agricultural output.

India is the world's largest milk producer. Its dairy success is built on the co-operative model of Anand in Gujarat, where Amul was founded in 1946. Operation Flood, launched by the National Dairy Development Board in 1970 under Verghese Kurien, linked village milk co-operatives to urban markets and is often called the White Revolution. Unlike the Green Revolution, it relied largely on smallholders with a few animals. Low productivity per animal, fodder shortages and animal diseases remain problems. The National Livestock Mission, the Rashtriya Gokul Mission for indigenous breeds and a vaccination drive against foot-and-mouth disease and brucellosis are the main programmes.

India is also among the world's largest fish producers, with inland aquaculture now producing more than marine capture. The Blue Revolution scheme of 2015-16 was followed in 2020 by the Pradhan Mantri Matsya Sampada Yojana, which invests in harvesting, cold chains, harbours and fishers' welfare. Shrimp is a major export. Overfishing, pollution of coastal waters and disputes with neighbouring countries over fishing grounds are concerns.

Poultry, goats and sheep give quick returns to poor households. A separate Ministry of Fisheries, Animal Husbandry and Dairying was created in 2019 to give these sectors more attention.

Key takeaways

  • India is the largest milk producer, built on the co-operative model of Operation Flood.
  • Inland aquaculture now produces more fish than marine capture; PMMSY started in 2020.
  • Livestock acts as income and insurance for landless and small households.

Why it matters for UPSC: Allied sectors are central to farm-income strategies and appear in GS3 under animal rearing and food processing.

Practice question: Why is the livestock and fisheries sector important for doubling farm incomes, and what holds it back?
