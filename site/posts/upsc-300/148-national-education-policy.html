The National Education Policy 2020

Note 148 of 300 · 2 min read · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

The National Education Policy (NEP) 2020, approved in July 2020, replaced the National Policy on Education of 1986 (modified in 1992). It was drafted by a committee chaired by K. Kasturirangan.

In school education, it replaces the 10+2 structure with a 5+3+3+4 design: a foundational stage (ages 3–8, including three years of preschool and classes 1–2), a preparatory stage (classes 3–5), a middle stage (classes 6–8) and a secondary stage (classes 9–12). It prioritises foundational literacy and numeracy by class 3, through the NIPUN Bharat mission. It recommends teaching in the mother tongue or regional language at least until class 5, and preferably until class 8, where possible. It proposes flexible subject choice with no rigid separation between arts, science and commerce, vocational education from class 6, and board exams that test core competencies; CBSE introduced a second, optional board exam for class 10 from 2026. A new National Curriculum Framework and new NCERT textbooks are being introduced.

In higher education, the NEP targets a gross enrolment ratio of 50% by 2035 (it was about 28% in 2021–22 according to the All India Survey on Higher Education). It introduces four-year multidisciplinary undergraduate degrees with multiple entry and exit points, an Academic Bank of Credits, a Common University Entrance Test, and the phasing out of the affiliation system. It proposes a single regulator, the Higher Education Commission of India, replacing the UGC, AICTE and NCTE, and allows top foreign universities to set up campuses in India. It reiterates the goal of public spending on education of 6% of GDP; current combined spending is around 4–4.6%.

The NEP's language policy, particularly the three-language formula, has been opposed by states such as Tamil Nadu, which has refused to sign agreements linked to the PM SHRI schools scheme.

Key takeaways

  • NEP 2020 introduces a 5+3+3+4 school structure and stresses mother-tongue teaching up to class 5.
  • It targets a higher education GER of 50% by 2035, with multiple entry and exit.
  • Funding (6% of GDP target) and language policy remain contested.

Why it matters for UPSC: NEP 2020 is one of the most frequently asked policy topics in GS II.
