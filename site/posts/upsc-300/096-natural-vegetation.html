Natural vegetation and forests of India

Note 096 of 300 · 2 min read · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

India's natural vegetation varies with rainfall, temperature and altitude. The main types are tropical evergreen forests, in areas with over 200 cm of rain such as the Western Ghats, the North-East and the Andaman and Nicobar Islands, with tall trees such as rosewood, ebony and mahogany; tropical deciduous forests, the most widespread type, which shed their leaves in the dry season and include teak and sal; tropical thorn forests in dry areas of Rajasthan, Gujarat and the Deccan, with acacias and cacti; montane forests in the Himalayas, changing from deciduous to coniferous trees such as pine, deodar and fir with altitude, and to alpine meadows above the tree line; and mangrove forests in tidal areas of deltas, such as the Sundarbans, where the sundari tree gives the forest its name.

According to the India State of Forest Report 2023, released by the Forest Survey of India in December 2024, forest cover is about 21.76% of the country's area, and forest and tree cover together about 25.17%. Madhya Pradesh has the largest forest cover by area, followed by Arunachal Pradesh and Chhattisgarh. Among states, Mizoram has the highest share of its area under forest. The National Forest Policy of 1988 set a goal of one-third of the country under forest or tree cover.

Forests provide timber, fuel, fodder and non-timber products, support millions of forest-dwelling people, protect watersheds, prevent soil erosion and store carbon. Under the Paris Agreement, India has committed to creating an additional carbon sink of 2.5 to 3 billion tonnes of CO2 equivalent through additional forest and tree cover by 2030.

Concerns include the quality of forests (much of the gain is in open forest and plantations rather than dense natural forest), loss of forest in the North-East, diversion for mining and infrastructure, and the rights of forest dwellers under the Forest Rights Act, 2006.

Key takeaways

  • Tropical deciduous forests are India's most widespread type; mangroves line the deltas.
  • ISFR 2023: forest cover about 21.76%, forest and tree cover about 25.17%.
  • India aims for an additional carbon sink of 2.5–3 billion tonnes CO2e by 2030.

Why it matters for UPSC: Forest types and the latest State of Forest Report figures are frequently tested.

Practice question: Is the rise in India's recorded forest cover a reliable sign of ecological health? Discuss.
