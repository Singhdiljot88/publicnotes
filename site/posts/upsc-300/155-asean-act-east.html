ASEAN and India's Act East Policy

Note 155 of 300 · 2 min read · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

The Association of Southeast Asian Nations (ASEAN) was founded in Bangkok in 1967. It now has eleven members: Brunei, Cambodia, Indonesia, Laos, Malaysia, Myanmar, the Philippines, Singapore, Thailand, Vietnam and, since October 2025, Timor-Leste. ASEAN works by consensus and non-interference and sits at the centre of a network of regional forums, including the ASEAN Regional Forum, the East Asia Summit and the ASEAN Defence Ministers' Meeting Plus, giving it influence beyond its economic weight.

India's engagement began with the Look East Policy in 1991, aimed mainly at economic ties with South-East Asia after the Cold War. In 2014 it was upgraded to the Act East Policy, adding security, connectivity and cultural dimensions and extending to the wider Indo-Pacific, including Japan, South Korea and Australia. India became an ASEAN sectoral partner in 1992, a dialogue partner in 1996 and a summit-level partner in 2002. The relationship was upgraded to a comprehensive strategic partnership in 2022.

The ASEAN-India Trade in Goods Agreement came into force in 2010. India's trade deficit with ASEAN has grown, and both sides agreed to review the agreement. India withdrew from the Regional Comprehensive Economic Partnership (RCEP) in 2019, citing concerns about cheap imports, especially from China, and inadequate protection for its farmers and industries.

Connectivity projects include the India-Myanmar-Thailand Trilateral Highway and the Kaladan Multi-Modal Transit Transport Project, both delayed. India has also deepened security ties with individual countries: it sold BrahMos missiles to the Philippines, delivered from 2024, and has defence partnerships with Vietnam, Indonesia and Singapore. The North-East is meant to be India's gateway to South-East Asia.

Key takeaways

  • ASEAN (1967) has eleven members after Timor-Leste joined in October 2025.
  • Look East (1991) became Act East (2014); the partnership became comprehensive and strategic in 2022.
  • India left RCEP in 2019; connectivity projects such as the Trilateral Highway are delayed.

Why it matters for UPSC: ASEAN and Act East are among the most frequent IR questions in both Prelims and Mains.

Practice question: Assess the achievements and shortcomings of India's Act East Policy.
