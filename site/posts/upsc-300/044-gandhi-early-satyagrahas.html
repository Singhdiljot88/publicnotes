Gandhi's return and the early satyagrahas

Note 044 of 300 · 2 min read · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

Mohandas Karamchand Gandhi returned to India in January 1915 after two decades in South Africa, where he had developed satyagraha, a method of non-violent resistance grounded in truth and the willingness to suffer rather than inflict harm. On the advice of his political mentor Gokhale, he spent a year travelling across India.

His first campaigns in India were local. In Champaran, Bihar, in 1917, he took up the cause of peasants forced by European planters to grow indigo on part of their land under the tinkathia system. His inquiry led to a government committee and the end of the system. In Ahmedabad in 1918, he supported textile mill workers in a wage dispute, using a hunger strike for the first time in India. In Kheda, Gujarat, in 1918, he led peasants in refusing to pay land revenue after crop failure; Vallabhbhai Patel emerged as a leader here.

The first all-India campaign came in 1919 against the Rowlatt Act, which allowed detention without trial. Gandhi called for a nationwide hartal. In Punjab, protests led to the arrest of local leaders. On 13 April 1919, in Amritsar's Jallianwala Bagh, troops under Brigadier-General Reginald Dyer fired without warning on a peaceful crowd that had gathered on the day of the Baisakhi festival. Official figures put the dead at 379; Indian estimates were much higher. Tagore renounced his knighthood in protest. The Hunter Committee inquiry was widely seen as a whitewash.

These events turned Gandhi from a cooperator into an opponent of British rule, and set the stage for the Non-Cooperation movement.

Key takeaways

  • Gandhi's first Indian satyagrahas were in Champaran (1917), Ahmedabad and Kheda (1918).
  • The Rowlatt satyagraha of 1919 was his first all-India campaign.
  • The Jallianwala Bagh massacre on 13 April 1919 transformed Indian opinion.

Why it matters for UPSC: Gandhi's early campaigns and the Rowlatt-Jallianwala sequence are among the most tested events.

Practice question: Why did Gandhi's early local satyagrahas prove important for his later leadership of the national movement?
