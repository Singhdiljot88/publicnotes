The Lokpal and Lokayuktas

Note 133 of 300 · 2 min read · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

The idea of an ombudsman to investigate corruption by public servants was recommended by the First Administrative Reforms Commission in 1966. Lokpal bills were introduced in Parliament several times from 1968 but lapsed. The anti-corruption movement led by Anna Hazare in 2011 finally pushed Parliament to pass the Lokpal and Lokayuktas Act, 2013, which came into force in January 2014.

The Lokpal consists of a chairperson and up to eight members, half of whom must be judicial members and at least half from SC, ST, OBC, minority communities and women. They are appointed by the President on the recommendation of a selection committee of the Prime Minister, the Speaker, the Leader of the Opposition (or leader of the largest opposition party), the Chief Justice of India or a judge nominated by them, and an eminent jurist. The Lokpal's jurisdiction covers the Prime Minister (with safeguards relating to international relations, security, public order, atomic energy and space), ministers, MPs and Group A to D officers of the Union government. It has an inquiry wing and a prosecution wing, and can order investigation by agencies such as the CBI.

The first Lokpal was appointed only in March 2019, with Justice Pinaki Chandra Ghose as chairperson, more than five years after the law came into force. Justice A. M. Khanwilkar became chairperson in 2024. Critics point to the delay, the small number of prosecutions, the requirement that complaints be filed in a prescribed format, and the lack of independent investigative capacity.

The Act also required states to set up Lokayuktas within a year. Maharashtra had established the first Lokayukta as early as 1971, and Karnataka's Lokayukta became known for high-profile investigations. Most states now have Lokayuktas, but their powers and independence vary widely.

Key takeaways

  • The Lokpal and Lokayuktas Act, 2013 came into force in 2014; the first Lokpal was appointed only in 2019.
  • The Lokpal covers the Prime Minister (with safeguards), ministers, MPs and central officials.
  • State Lokayuktas vary greatly in powers and effectiveness.

Why it matters for UPSC: The Lokpal's composition, jurisdiction and performance are frequent Prelims and Mains questions.

Practice question: Why has the Lokpal not lived up to the expectations of the 2011 anti-corruption movement?
