West Asia and the Gulf: geography and India's stakes

Note 107 of 300 · 2 min read · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

West Asia, often called the Middle East, stretches from Turkey and the eastern Mediterranean to Iran and the Arabian Peninsula. Much of it is desert or semi-desert, but it holds about half of the world's proven oil reserves and a large share of its natural gas, concentrated around the Persian Gulf in Saudi Arabia, Iran, Iraq, Kuwait, the United Arab Emirates and Qatar.

The region's geography explains its strategic importance. It lies at the junction of Europe, Asia and Africa, and controls key sea routes: the Strait of Hormuz, the Bab-el-Mandeb, the Red Sea and the Suez Canal. Water is scarce; rivers such as the Tigris, Euphrates and Jordan are shared and contested, and Gulf states rely heavily on desalination.

India's stakes in the region are large. The Gulf supplies most of India's imports of crude oil, LPG and fertilisers. About nine million Indians live and work in the Gulf Cooperation Council countries, and their remittances are a major source of foreign exchange; the UAE and Saudi Arabia are among India's top trading partners. India signed a Comprehensive Economic Partnership Agreement with the UAE in 2022 and is negotiating with the GCC. The India-Middle East-Europe Economic Corridor (IMEC), announced at the G20 summit in New Delhi in 2023, aims to link India to Europe via the Gulf, Jordan and Israel by sea, rail and pipelines. India also develops the Chabahar port in Iran to reach Afghanistan and Central Asia, bypassing Pakistan.

Conflicts in the region, from wars involving Israel and Iran to attacks on shipping, directly affect India through oil prices, the safety of its diaspora and trade routes. India has carried out several evacuations of its citizens from the region.

Key takeaways

  • West Asia holds about half of the world's proven oil reserves and controls key sea routes.
  • About nine million Indians live in the Gulf, and the region supplies most of India's energy imports.
  • IMEC and Chabahar are India's connectivity projects through the region.

Why it matters for UPSC: West Asia links geography, energy security, diaspora and foreign policy, and appears often in GS II and GS III.

Practice question: Why is stability in West Asia vital for India's economic and strategic interests?
