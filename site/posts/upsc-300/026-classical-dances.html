India's classical dances

Note 026 of 300 · 2 min read · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

Classical dance in India traces its principles to the Natyashastra, a treatise attributed to Bharata Muni and composed roughly two thousand years ago. It describes the elements of performance: nritta (pure dance), nritya (expressive dance that conveys meaning through gestures and expressions) and natya (drama). It also sets out the theory of rasa, the emotional flavour a performance evokes in the audience.

The Sangeet Natak Akademi recognises eight classical dance forms. Bharatanatyam from Tamil Nadu, once performed in temples, was revived in the twentieth century by Rukmini Devi Arundale and others. Kathak from north India developed in temples and Mughal and Rajput courts and is known for footwork and spins. Kathakali from Kerala is a dance-drama with elaborate make-up, performed by men in its traditional form. Kuchipudi comes from a village of that name in Andhra Pradesh. Odissi from Odisha is known for its tribhanga (three-bend) posture; its revival owes much to Kelucharan Mohapatra. Manipuri from Manipur has gentle, flowing movements and is linked to the Raas Leela. Mohiniyattam from Kerala is a graceful solo dance traditionally performed by women. Sattriya, from the Vaishnava monasteries (satras) of Assam founded by Sankardeva, was recognised as classical in 2000.

The Ministry of Culture also lists Chhau, the masked dance of eastern India, among classical forms. Chhau is inscribed on UNESCO's list of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity, as are Kutiyattam, the Sanskrit theatre of Kerala, and the Ramlila.

Key takeaways

  • The Natyashastra sets out the theory of dance, including nritta, nritya, natya and rasa.
  • The Sangeet Natak Akademi recognises eight classical dances; Sattriya was added in 2000.
  • Each dance is tied to a region, a religious tradition and often a modern revival.

Why it matters for UPSC: Dance-state matching and the features of each form are standard Prelims questions.

Practice question: How did the twentieth-century revival of classical dances relate to the nationalist movement?
