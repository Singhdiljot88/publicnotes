One Health and zoonotic diseases

Note 220 of 300 · 2 min read · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

Zoonotic diseases are infections that pass from animals to humans. Around 60% of known infectious diseases and about three-quarters of new and emerging ones are thought to be zoonotic. Examples include rabies, bird flu, Nipah virus, Kyasanur Forest Disease (monkey fever), anthrax, brucellosis, scrub typhus, Ebola, mpox and, most likely, COVID-19.

India faces a heavy zoonotic burden. Rabies, mostly spread by dog bites, kills thousands of Indians each year, a large share of the world's rabies deaths; the National Action Plan for Dog Mediated Rabies Elimination aims to end such deaths by 2030. Nipah virus, carried by fruit bats, has caused repeated outbreaks in Kerala since 2018, with high death rates, and earlier in West Bengal; there is no approved vaccine or specific treatment. Kyasanur Forest Disease, transmitted by ticks, affects forest areas of the Western Ghats.

The causes of rising zoonoses include deforestation and encroachment into wildlife habitats, which bring people and animals into closer contact; intensive livestock farming; trade in wildlife; urbanisation; and climate change, which shifts the range of disease-carrying insects such as mosquitoes and ticks.

The One Health approach recognises that the health of people, animals and the environment is linked, and that preventing disease requires these sectors to work together. Globally, the WHO, the Food and Agriculture Organization, the World Organisation for Animal Health and the UN Environment Programme form the "Quadripartite" for One Health. In India, the government has set up a National One Health Mission to coordinate surveillance across ministries, and the foundation of the National Institute for One Health in Nagpur was laid in 2023. The National Centre for Disease Control runs the Integrated Disease Surveillance Programme, and the Pandemic Treaty adopted by the World Health Assembly in May 2025 aims to improve global preparedness.

Key takeaways

  • Around 60% of infectious diseases and most emerging ones are zoonotic.
  • Rabies, Nipah and Kyasanur Forest Disease are major zoonotic threats in India.
  • The One Health approach links human, animal and environmental health; India has a One Health mission and a new national institute.

Why it matters for UPSC: Zoonoses and One Health are frequent GS II and GS III topics after COVID-19.

Practice question: How does environmental degradation increase the risk of zoonotic diseases? What should India do?
