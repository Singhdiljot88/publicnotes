India's wars: 1962, 1965 and 1971

Note 055 of 300 · 2 min read · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

India fought three major wars in its first quarter-century. The 1962 war with China grew out of disputed boundaries in the western sector (Aksai Chin) and eastern sector (the McMahon Line in what is now Arunachal Pradesh), tensions after the Dalai Lama fled to India in 1959, and India's "forward policy" of setting up posts in contested areas. In October-November 1962, Chinese forces advanced in both sectors before declaring a unilateral ceasefire and withdrawing in the east. The defeat led to the resignation of Defence Minister V. K. Krishna Menon and a major expansion of India's armed forces.

In 1965, Pakistan launched Operation Gibraltar, sending infiltrators into Kashmir, followed by an armoured attack in the Chhamb sector. India responded by crossing the international border towards Lahore and Sialkot. Major tank battles, including at Asal Uttar, followed before a UN-brokered ceasefire. Prime Minister Lal Bahadur Shastri and Pakistan's President Ayub Khan signed the Tashkent Declaration in January 1966, with Soviet mediation; Shastri died in Tashkent the next day. His slogan "Jai Jawan, Jai Kisan" dates from this period.

The 1971 war arose from the crisis in East Pakistan, where the Pakistani army's crackdown after the Awami League's election victory drove around ten million refugees into India. India signed a treaty of friendship with the Soviet Union in August 1971. After Pakistan's air strikes on Indian bases on 3 December 1971, India went to war. On 16 December, about 93,000 Pakistani personnel surrendered in Dhaka, and Bangladesh became independent. The Simla Agreement of 1972 between Indira Gandhi and Zulfikar Ali Bhutto committed both sides to resolve disputes bilaterally and converted the ceasefire line in Kashmir into the Line of Control.

Key takeaways

  • The 1962 war with China exposed defence weaknesses and changed India's security policy.
  • The 1965 war ended with the Tashkent Declaration (1966).
  • The 1971 war created Bangladesh; the Simla Agreement (1972) set up the Line of Control.

Why it matters for UPSC: These wars are core post-independence history and essential background for India-China and India-Pakistan questions.

Practice question: How did the 1971 war change the strategic situation in South Asia?
