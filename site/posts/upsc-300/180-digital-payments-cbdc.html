Digital payments, UPI and the digital rupee

Note 180 of 300 · 2 min read · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

India has become one of the world's leaders in digital payments. The key institution is the National Payments Corporation of India (NPCI), a not-for-profit company set up in 2008 by banks under the guidance of the RBI. It runs RuPay (a domestic card network launched in 2012), the Immediate Payment Service (IMPS), the Aadhaar Enabled Payment System, FASTag toll payments through the National Electronic Toll Collection system, and the Bharat Bill Payment System.

The Unified Payments Interface (UPI), launched in 2016, allows instant transfers between bank accounts using a mobile phone and a virtual address, without sharing account details. It is interoperable, so any UPI app can pay any bank account, and free for most person-to-person and merchant payments. UPI now processes well over ten billion transactions a month and accounts for the large majority of digital payment transactions in India. It has been linked with payment systems in countries such as Singapore, the UAE, France and Nepal, and is accepted in several others. UPI Lite allows small payments offline, and UPI 123PAY works on basic phones. Concerns include concentration, with two private apps handling most transactions, and digital fraud.

The RBI regulates payment systems under the Payment and Settlement Systems Act, 2007. RTGS (real-time gross settlement) handles large-value payments and NEFT handles retail transfers; both have been available round the clock since 2019–20.

A central bank digital currency (CBDC) is a digital form of a country's currency issued by its central bank. The RBI launched pilots of the digital rupee (e-rupee) in December 2022, for both wholesale use between banks and retail use by individuals and merchants. Unlike UPI, which moves money between bank accounts, the e-rupee is itself a liability of the RBI, like cash. Its uptake has been modest so far. The RBI has repeatedly warned against private cryptocurrencies, which are taxed in India but not recognised as legal tender.

Key takeaways

  • NPCI (2008) runs UPI, RuPay, IMPS and FASTag systems.
  • UPI (2016) processes well over ten billion transactions a month and is being linked abroad.
  • The RBI's digital rupee pilots began in December 2022; private cryptocurrencies are not legal tender.

Why it matters for UPSC: Digital payments, UPI and the e-rupee are high-frequency current topics in Prelims and Mains.

Practice question: What risks accompany India's rapid shift to digital payments? How should they be managed?
