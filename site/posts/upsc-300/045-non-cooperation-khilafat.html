The Non-Cooperation and Khilafat movements

Note 045 of 300 · 2 min read · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

After the First World War, Indian Muslims were angered by the harsh treatment of the defeated Ottoman Sultan, whom many regarded as the Khalifa, the spiritual head of Islam. The Khilafat movement, led by the Ali brothers (Muhammad Ali and Shaukat Ali), Maulana Azad and others, demanded the protection of the Khalifa's position. Gandhi saw an opportunity to unite Hindus and Muslims against British rule.

The Congress launched the Non-Cooperation movement in 1920, approved at a special session in Calcutta in September and confirmed at the Nagpur session in December. Its programme included surrendering titles and honours, boycotting government schools, colleges, law courts and legislative councils, boycotting foreign cloth and promoting khadi and the charkha. Gandhi promised swaraj within a year. The Nagpur session also reorganised the Congress on a linguistic basis and opened it to mass membership.

The response was enormous. Thousands of students left government institutions for national schools such as Jamia Millia Islamia and Kashi Vidyapith. Lawyers including Motilal Nehru and C. R. Das gave up their practices. Foreign cloth was burnt in bonfires. Peasants, workers and tribal communities joined, sometimes going beyond Gandhi's programme.

In February 1922, at Chauri Chaura in the United Provinces, a crowd set fire to a police station, killing 22 policemen. Gandhi called off the movement, believing the people were not ready for non-violence. Many leaders disagreed. Gandhi was arrested and sentenced to six years in prison. The Khilafat issue ended when Turkey abolished the caliphate in 1924. Within the Congress, C. R. Das and Motilal Nehru formed the Swaraj Party in 1923 to contest council elections.

Key takeaways

  • Non-Cooperation (1920–22) combined with the Khilafat movement to unite Hindus and Muslims.
  • It used boycott of institutions, titles and foreign cloth, and promoted khadi.
  • Gandhi withdrew it after Chauri Chaura in February 1922.

Why it matters for UPSC: The programme, spread and withdrawal of Non-Cooperation are core topics for Prelims and Mains.

Practice question: Was Gandhi right to withdraw the Non-Cooperation movement after Chauri Chaura? Present both sides.
