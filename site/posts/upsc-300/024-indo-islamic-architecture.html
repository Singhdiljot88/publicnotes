Indo-Islamic architecture: from the Qutub Minar to the Taj Mahal

Note 024 of 300 · 2 min read · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

The Delhi Sultanate brought new building techniques to India. The most important was the true arch and dome, built with voussoirs and held together with lime mortar, replacing the older Indian method of corbelling. Decoration avoided human figures and used calligraphy, geometric patterns and floral designs called arabesques.

Early monuments include the Quwwat-ul-Islam mosque and the Qutub Minar in Delhi, begun by Qutbuddin Aibak and completed by Iltutmish, with later additions. The Alai Darwaza, built by Alauddin Khalji in 1311, is considered the first building in India with a true dome and arches. The Tughlaqs favoured massive sloping walls, as at Tughlaqabad. Regional styles flourished in Bengal, Gujarat, Jaunpur and the Deccan, where the Gol Gumbaz at Bijapur has one of the largest domes in the world and a famous whispering gallery.

Mughal architecture combined Persian, Central Asian and Indian elements. Humayun's tomb in Delhi, completed in the 1570s, introduced the charbagh, a garden divided into four parts, and the double dome. Akbar's Fatehpur Sikri, built in red sandstone, includes the Buland Darwaza and the Panch Mahal, and borrowed freely from Rajput and Gujarati forms. Under Shah Jahan, white marble and pietra dura inlay (called parchin kari) reached their height in the Taj Mahal at Agra, built as a tomb for Mumtaz Mahal.

Indo-Islamic architecture shows how Indian craftsmen and foreign designs merged to produce something new. Humayun's tomb, the Qutub complex, Fatehpur Sikri, the Red Fort, Agra Fort and the Taj Mahal are all UNESCO World Heritage Sites.

Key takeaways

  • The true arch and dome arrived with the Sultanate; the Alai Darwaza (1311) is an early example.
  • Mughal buildings introduced the charbagh and double dome and later used marble and pietra dura.
  • The style blended Persian and Central Asian design with Indian craftsmanship.

Why it matters for UPSC: Monument-to-ruler matching and architectural features are recurring Prelims questions.

Practice question: "Indo-Islamic architecture was a synthesis rather than an import." Discuss with examples.
