Environmental Impact Assessment

Note 237 of 300 · 2 min read · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

Environmental Impact Assessment (EIA) is the process of studying the likely environmental and social effects of a project before it is approved, so that harm can be avoided, reduced or offset. In India, EIA became mandatory for certain projects through a notification under the Environment (Protection) Act in 1994. The current framework is the EIA Notification of 2006, which has been amended many times.

The 2006 notification divides projects into Category A, appraised at the central level by an Expert Appraisal Committee of the Environment Ministry, and Category B, appraised by state-level authorities. Category B is further divided into B1, which needs a full EIA report, and B2, which does not. The process has four stages: screening (deciding whether a project needs EIA), scoping (setting the terms of reference for the study), public consultation (a public hearing near the site and written comments from affected people) and appraisal (expert review leading to a decision on clearance with conditions).

Critics point to several weaknesses. EIA reports are prepared by consultants paid by project developers, which creates a conflict of interest, and some reports have been found to copy material from other projects. Public hearings are sometimes held with little notice or in languages local people do not understand. Monitoring of clearance conditions after approval is weak. The draft EIA Notification of 2020 drew wide protest because it proposed to allow post-facto clearance for projects that had started without approval and to exempt more projects from public consultation.

Post-facto clearance went to the Supreme Court. In the Vanashakti case, a two-judge bench struck down such clearances in May 2025. In November 2025, a three-judge bench recalled that judgment by a 2:1 majority, reopening the question.

Key takeaways

  • The EIA Notification, 2006 sets Category A (central) and B (state) projects and a four-stage process: screening, scoping, public consultation, appraisal.
  • Key weaknesses: developer-funded reports, weak public hearings and poor post-clearance monitoring.
  • The Supreme Court struck down post-facto clearance in May 2025, but a larger bench recalled that ruling in November 2025.

Why it matters for UPSC: EIA sits at the heart of the development versus environment debate and is a regular mains question.

Practice question: Critically examine the role of public consultation in India's Environmental Impact Assessment process.
