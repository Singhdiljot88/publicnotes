The Government of India Act 1935 and the 1937 elections

Note 048 of 300 · 2 min read · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

The Government of India Act 1935 was the longest act the British Parliament had passed. It drew on the Simon Commission, the Round Table Conferences and a White Paper of 1933. Much of its structure later found its way into the Constitution of India.

The act proposed an All-India Federation of British provinces and princely states, with power divided into three lists: federal, provincial and concurrent. The federation never came into being because not enough princes joined. It abolished dyarchy in the provinces and introduced provincial autonomy: elected Indian ministers responsible to provincial legislatures would run all provincial subjects, though governors kept special powers. Dyarchy was introduced at the centre instead, but this part was never implemented. The act also set up a Federal Court (established in 1937) and a Reserve Bank of India (which had been created separately by the RBI Act of 1934), extended separate electorates, and enlarged the electorate to roughly a tenth of the population. Burma was separated from India.

The Congress criticised the act as a "charter of slavery" but contested the 1937 provincial elections. It won majorities in five provinces and was the largest party in others, eventually forming ministries in eight of eleven provinces. The Muslim League performed poorly, even in Muslim-majority provinces. The Congress ministries introduced reforms in areas such as tenancy, prohibition and civil liberties.

The ministries resigned in 1939 when the Viceroy declared India a party to the Second World War without consulting Indian leaders. The Muslim League celebrated their resignation as "Deliverance Day".

Key takeaways

  • The 1935 Act introduced provincial autonomy and proposed an All-India Federation that never formed.
  • Its three lists, Federal Court and governor's powers influenced the Constitution of India.
  • The Congress formed ministries after the 1937 elections and resigned in 1939.

Why it matters for UPSC: The 1935 Act is a key source of the Indian Constitution and a frequent Prelims and Mains topic.

Practice question: Which features of the Government of India Act 1935 were carried into the Constitution of India, and why?
