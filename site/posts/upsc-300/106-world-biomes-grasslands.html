The world's major biomes and grasslands

Note 106 of 300 · 2 min read · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

A biome is a large region with a characteristic climate, vegetation and animal life. The main terrestrial biomes follow broad patterns of temperature and rainfall.

Tropical rainforests lie near the equator, in the Amazon basin, the Congo basin and South-East Asia. They have high temperatures and rainfall all year, and the greatest biodiversity of any biome. Tropical savannas, such as the East African grasslands and the Llanos and Campos of South America, have tall grasses with scattered trees and distinct wet and dry seasons; they support large herds of grazing animals. Hot deserts, such as the Sahara, Kalahari, Arabian and Thar, lie mainly along the subtropical high-pressure belts and on western coasts beside cold currents.

Temperate grasslands lie in the interiors of continents in the mid-latitudes, with hot summers, cold winters and moderate rainfall. They have different names on each continent: the Prairies of North America, the Pampas of Argentina, the Steppes of Eurasia, the Veldt of South Africa, the Downs of Australia and the Canterbury plains of New Zealand. Their fertile soils have made many of them the world's great wheat and cattle regions. Mediterranean regions, with hot dry summers and mild wet winters, grow citrus fruits, olives and grapes.

Temperate deciduous forests cover parts of Europe, eastern North America and East Asia. The taiga, or boreal coniferous forest, stretches across northern Canada, Scandinavia and Siberia, supplying softwood timber. The tundra, near the Arctic, has permanently frozen subsoil (permafrost), short summers and low-growing mosses and lichens. Thawing permafrost is releasing methane, a powerful greenhouse gas.

Key takeaways

  • Biomes reflect patterns of temperature and rainfall.
  • Temperate grasslands have different names on each continent: Prairies, Pampas, Steppes, Veldt and Downs.
  • The taiga is the largest forest biome; thawing tundra permafrost releases methane.

Why it matters for UPSC: Matching grasslands, biomes and regions is a common Prelims question type.

Practice question: Why have temperate grasslands become the world's major granaries? What environmental costs has this had?
