The First World War

Note 066 of 300 · 2 min read · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

The First World War (1914–18) grew from rivalries among the great powers. Europe was divided into two alliances: the Triple Alliance of Germany, Austria-Hungary and Italy, and the Triple Entente of Britain, France and Russia. Imperial competition, a naval arms race between Britain and Germany, militarism and nationalism, especially in the Balkans, raised tensions. The spark came on 28 June 1914, when Archduke Franz Ferdinand of Austria-Hungary was assassinated in Sarajevo by a Bosnian Serb nationalist. Within weeks, the alliance system drew all the powers into war.

On the western front, the war became a stalemate of trench warfare, with battles such as the Somme and Verdun killing hundreds of thousands for little territorial gain. New weapons included machine guns, poison gas, tanks, submarines and aircraft. The Ottoman Empire joined Germany; Italy switched sides to join the Entente. Russia withdrew in 1918 after the Bolshevik revolution. The United States entered the war in 1917, partly because of German submarine warfare, tipping the balance. Germany signed an armistice on 11 November 1918.

About 1.3 million Indian soldiers served, in France, Mesopotamia, East Africa, Gallipoli and Palestine; more than 74,000 died. India Gate in Delhi commemorates them. Indians expected political concessions in return, and disappointment fuelled the national movement.

The Treaty of Versailles (1919) blamed Germany for the war, imposed heavy reparations, reduced its army and took away its colonies. The Austro-Hungarian, Ottoman and Russian empires collapsed. The League of Nations was created to keep the peace, but the United States never joined. Many historians see the harsh peace as one cause of the Second World War.

Key takeaways

  • Alliances, imperialism, militarism and Balkan nationalism led to war in 1914.
  • Around 1.3 million Indian soldiers served, and more than 74,000 died.
  • The Treaty of Versailles punished Germany and created the League of Nations.

Why it matters for UPSC: The causes and consequences of the First World War, and India's role in it, are recurring exam topics.

Practice question: Was the Treaty of Versailles a cause of the Second World War? Discuss.
