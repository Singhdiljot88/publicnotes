Caste in modern India: continuity and change

Note 072 of 300 · 2 min read · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

Caste combines two related ideas. Varna is the four-fold scriptural classification of Brahmin, Kshatriya, Vaishya and Shudra. Jati refers to the thousands of endogamous groups, often linked to traditional occupations, that people actually belong to. Untouchability placed some groups outside the varna order altogether and subjected them to extreme discrimination.

The Constitution abolished untouchability (Article 17), prohibited discrimination on grounds of caste (Article 15) and allowed reservations for Scheduled Castes, Scheduled Tribes and, later, Other Backward Classes in education, public jobs and legislatures. Laws such as the Protection of Civil Rights Act, 1955 and the SC/ST (Prevention of Atrocities) Act, 1989 provide legal protection. According to the 2011 Census, Scheduled Castes made up 16.6% and Scheduled Tribes 8.6% of the population.

Sociologists describe several processes of change. M. N. Srinivas used the term Sanskritisation for lower castes adopting the practices of higher castes to rise in status, and "dominant caste" for landowning groups with local power. Urbanisation, education and new occupations have weakened rules on food, contact and occupation, especially in cities. At the same time, caste has become a basis for political mobilisation, and inter-caste marriage remains uncommon: surveys typically find only around 5% of marriages are inter-caste. Atrocities against Dalits continue to be reported in large numbers each year.

The decision to count caste in the next Census, announced by the Union government in April 2025, has revived debate. Supporters say data is needed for fair policy; critics worry it will entrench caste identities.

Key takeaways

  • Caste combines varna (four orders) and jati (thousands of endogamous groups).
  • The Constitution abolished untouchability and allows reservations; SCs were 16.6% and STs 8.6% in 2011.
  • Caste has weakened in ritual life but persists in marriage and has grown in political importance.

Why it matters for UPSC: Caste is central to GS I society and GS II social justice questions, and the caste census is a live issue.

Practice question: Has caste in India declined or merely changed its form? Discuss with examples.
