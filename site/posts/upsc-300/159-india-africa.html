India and Africa

Note 159 of 300 · 2 min read · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

India's ties with Africa are old: trade across the Indian Ocean dates back centuries, and Gandhi's political career began in South Africa. During the freedom struggle and after, India supported African liberation movements and opposed apartheid. A large Indian diaspora, nearly three million, lives in African countries such as South Africa, Mauritius, Kenya and Tanzania.

Modern engagement has expanded. India-Africa trade was around 100 billion US dollars in recent years, making India one of Africa's largest trading partners. India imports crude oil, gold, coal and minerals, and exports refined petroleum, pharmaceuticals, vehicles and machinery. Indian generic medicines are vital to African public health. India has extended lines of credit worth over 12 billion dollars for infrastructure projects, and Indian companies have invested heavily in telecoms, energy and agriculture.

Institutionally, India held three India-Africa Forum Summits, in 2008, 2011 and 2015, the last attended by all 54 African countries. A fourth summit has been repeatedly delayed. India's development partnership emphasises capacity building, through scholarships, training under the Indian Technical and Economic Cooperation (ITEC) programme, the Pan-African e-Network for tele-education and tele-medicine, and its successor e-VidyaBharti and e-ArogyaBharti. India has opened many new embassies in Africa since 2018.

During its G20 presidency in 2023, India championed the admission of the African Union as a permanent member of the G20, which was agreed at the New Delhi summit. India also presents itself as a voice of the Global South, hosting the Voice of the Global South summits.

India's engagement is often compared with China's much larger lending and infrastructure presence. India's approach is described as demand-driven and focused on skills and local ownership, but it has been criticised for slow project delivery.

Key takeaways

  • India-Africa trade is around 100 billion dollars, with India supplying generic medicines and receiving energy and minerals.
  • Three India-Africa Forum Summits were held (2008, 2011, 2015); a fourth is pending.
  • India pushed for the African Union's permanent G20 membership in 2023.

Why it matters for UPSC: India-Africa relations are increasingly asked in GS II, often compared with China's role.

Practice question: How can India strengthen its partnership with Africa in the face of China's growing presence?
