The structure of Indian banking

Note 175 of 300 · 2 min read · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

India's banking system includes several types of institutions. Scheduled commercial banks, listed in the Second Schedule of the RBI Act, include public sector banks, private sector banks, foreign banks, regional rural banks (RRBs), small finance banks and payments banks. Cooperative banks, urban and rural, form a separate tier.

Public sector banks, majority-owned by the government, still hold a large share of deposits and loans, though private banks have grown rapidly. A series of mergers, culminating in 2020, reduced the number of public sector banks to twelve. State Bank of India, which grew out of the Imperial Bank of India and was nationalised in 1955, is the largest. The government has announced plans to privatise some public sector banks and has put its stake in IDBI Bank, held jointly with LIC, up for strategic sale, but such sales have been slow.

Regional rural banks were set up from 1975 to provide credit to small farmers, labourers and artisans. They are jointly owned by the Union government, the sponsor bank and the state government, and have been amalgamated to bring their number down to one per state under the "one state, one RRB" policy.

Differentiated banks were introduced in 2014–15. Small finance banks, many converted from microfinance institutions, focus on small businesses and unserved areas, with at least 75% of lending to priority sectors. Payments banks, such as India Post Payments Bank, can accept deposits up to a limit (now Rs 2 lakh per customer) and provide payments and remittances, but cannot lend.

Cooperative banks long suffered from dual control by the RBI and state registrars of cooperative societies, which contributed to failures such as the Punjab and Maharashtra Cooperative Bank in 2019. A 2020 amendment to the Banking Regulation Act brought them more fully under RBI supervision. Deposit insurance through the DICGC covers each depositor up to Rs 5 lakh per bank, raised from Rs 1 lakh in 2020.

Key takeaways

  • Scheduled commercial banks include public, private, foreign, regional rural, small finance and payments banks.
  • Mergers reduced public sector banks to twelve by 2020.
  • Payments banks cannot lend; deposit insurance covers up to Rs 5 lakh per depositor per bank.

Why it matters for UPSC: Bank types, their features and recent reforms are standard Prelims topics.

Practice question: Should the government privatise public sector banks? Discuss the arguments for and against.
