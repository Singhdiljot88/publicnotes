GST: structure, council and the 2025 reform

Note 184 of 300 · 2 min read · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

The Goods and Services Tax, introduced on 1 July 2017, replaced a tangle of central and state indirect taxes, including excise duty, service tax, VAT, central sales tax, entry tax and octroi, with a single destination-based tax. It was made possible by the Constitution (101st Amendment) Act, 2016, which gave both Parliament and state legislatures concurrent power to tax goods and services (Article 246A) and created the GST Council (Article 279A).

GST is a dual tax. On transactions within a state, the Union levies Central GST and the state levies State GST at equal rates. On inter-state supplies and imports, the Union levies Integrated GST, which is then shared with the destination state. Because tax paid on inputs can be set off against tax on output through input tax credit, GST taxes only the value added at each stage and removes the "tax on tax" cascading of the old system. The GST Network (GSTN) runs the IT backbone, including e-invoicing and e-way bills for moving goods. Alcohol for human consumption is outside GST, and petroleum products have been kept out so far, though they can be brought in on the Council's recommendation.

States were guaranteed compensation for five years for revenue losses, assuming 14% annual growth, funded by a compensation cess; the guarantee ended in June 2022, and the cess was continued to repay loans taken during the pandemic.

In September 2025 the GST Council approved the biggest overhaul since launch. From 22 September 2025, the earlier four main slabs of 5%, 12%, 18% and 28% were reduced to two main rates of 5% and 18%, with a 40% rate for a small set of luxury and "sin" goods, and many everyday goods moved to lower rates or exempted. Gross GST collections in 2025-26 were about Rs 22.27 lakh crore, up 8.3% on the previous year. Remaining issues include an appellate tribunal that took years to begin functioning, compliance burdens on small businesses and states' loss of fiscal autonomy.

Key takeaways

  • GST (2017) replaced many indirect taxes; the 101st Amendment created Article 246A and the GST Council.
  • CGST and SGST apply within a state; IGST applies to inter-state supplies; alcohol and petroleum are outside GST.
  • From 22 September 2025, GST moved to two main slabs of 5% and 18%, plus 40% for select goods; FY26 collections were about Rs 22.27 lakh crore.

Why it matters for UPSC: GST structure and the 2025 reform are among the most likely GS III and Prelims questions.

Practice question: Evaluate the impact of the 2025 GST rate rationalisation on consumers, businesses and states.
