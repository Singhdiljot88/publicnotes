Insolation and the Earth's heat budget

Note 086 of 300 · 2 min read · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

The Earth receives energy from the Sun as short-wave radiation, called insolation (incoming solar radiation). The amount received at any place depends on the angle of the Sun's rays, the length of the day, the transparency of the atmosphere and the distance from the Sun. Because the Earth's axis is tilted by about 23.5 degrees, the angle of the Sun and day length change through the year, producing seasons. The tropics receive more insolation than the poles, which creates the temperature differences that drive winds and ocean currents.

Not all incoming radiation is absorbed. Of every 100 units reaching the top of the atmosphere, about 30 are reflected back to space by clouds, the atmosphere and the surface. This reflectivity is called albedo. Fresh snow and ice have a high albedo; forests and oceans have a low albedo. When ice melts because of warming, the darker surface beneath absorbs more heat, causing more warming: a positive feedback important in climate change, especially in the Arctic.

The remaining energy is absorbed by the atmosphere and the surface. The Earth then radiates energy back as long-wave (infrared) radiation. Greenhouse gases absorb much of this and re-radiate it, warming the lower atmosphere. Over the long term, incoming and outgoing energy balance; this is the heat budget. Rising greenhouse gas concentrations upset this balance, causing global warming.

The surface is heated directly, and the air is heated mainly from below by conduction, convection and radiation from the ground. Temperature also varies with land and sea: land heats and cools faster than water, which explains land and sea breezes and the monsoon.

Key takeaways

  • Insolation varies with the Sun's angle, day length and atmospheric conditions.
  • About 30% of incoming radiation is reflected (albedo); melting ice reduces albedo and adds warming.
  • The heat budget balances incoming and outgoing energy; greenhouse gases shift the balance.

Why it matters for UPSC: Insolation, albedo and the heat budget are basic to climate and climate-change questions.

Practice question: Explain the role of albedo in the Earth's heat budget and in climate change.
