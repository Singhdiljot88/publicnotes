Measuring India's GDP: the new 2022-23 series

Note 162 of 300 · 2 min read · Fact-checked 2 November 2026

India's national accounts are compiled by the National Statistics Office (NSO) under the Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation (MoSPI). Base years are revised periodically, because an economy's structure, prices and data sources change. The series with base year 2011-12, introduced in 2015, drew criticism: some economists argued it overstated growth, partly because it relied on a corporate database (the MCA-21 filings) and on single deflation, which can distort real growth when input and output prices move differently.

On 27 February 2026, MoSPI released a new series with 2022-23 as the base year. The year was chosen as the most recent "normal" year after the pandemic disruptions of 2020 and 2021. The new series estimated real GDP growth for 2025-26 at 7.6%, and growth of 7.8% in the October-December 2025 quarter. Nominal GDP levels for recent years were revised down by roughly 3 to 4% compared with the old series.

The methodological changes are significant. The new series uses more administrative and high-frequency data, such as GST returns, vehicle registrations from the e-Vahan portal and the Public Financial Management System. It applies double deflation in manufacturing and agriculture, deflating inputs and outputs separately. It aligns the accounts with supply and use tables to reduce the gap between production-side and expenditure-side estimates, the "discrepancies" that critics had highlighted. It draws on the Annual Survey of Unincorporated Sector Enterprises (ASUSE) and the Periodic Labour Force Survey to measure the informal sector better, and includes activities such as hired domestic work and the gig and platform economy.

The CPI was rebased to 2024 and the Index of Industrial Production updated in the same period, so India's main economic statistics now share more recent foundations.

Key takeaways

  • MoSPI released a new GDP series with base year 2022-23 on 27 February 2026.
  • It estimated real growth of 7.6% in 2025-26, but revised nominal GDP levels down by about 3–4%.
  • Double deflation, GST and other administrative data, and better informal-sector measurement are key changes.

Why it matters for UPSC: The new GDP series is a high-priority current topic and a classic question on the reliability of official statistics.

Practice question: Why do statistical agencies revise base years? How does the 2022-23 GDP series address earlier criticisms?
