Indian Democracy in 100 Notes · 🌏 Theme 9: Foreign policy & democracy

Trade, tariffs, and economic diplomacy in 2025-26

Note 086 of 100 · 3 min read · Fact-checked 1 November 2026 · Not legal advice

Trade became the sharpest test of India's foreign policy in 2025–26, because tariffs are not only about prices. They affect jobs, farmers, exporters and the political choices a government can defend at home. Three stories ran in parallel: difficult negotiations with the United States, new agreements with Europe, and a continuing debate about dependence on China.

The United States story began with a shock. In August 2025, Indian goods faced a combined United States tariff burden approaching 50%, made up of a 25% reciprocal tariff plus a further 25% linked to India's purchases of Russian oil. On 6 February 2026, the two governments announced a framework for an interim agreement: Washington said it would cut the reciprocal rate to 18% and remove the Russian-oil penalty from 7 February 2026, while India said it would cut or eliminate tariffs on a range of United States industrial and agricultural goods and intended to buy about $500 billion of United States energy, aircraft and other goods over five years. That framework then stalled. On 20 February 2026, the United States Supreme Court ruled that the emergency-powers law used for the sweeping reciprocal tariffs did not authorise them, and later in 2026 a further Section 301 measure was reported, with India receiving a 10% rate in that forced-labour investigation. By October 2026, India's finance minister described talks as having reached a plateau, and its trade minister said a first tranche was in final drafting — a reminder that a framework is not a signed deal.

Europe moved faster. The United Kingdom–India Comprehensive Economic and Trade Agreement was signed in July 2025, after negotiations launched in 2022. India and the European Union announced the conclusion of their free trade negotiations on 27 January 2026, after intermittent talks since 2007, relaunched in 2022. Supporters present these deals as diversification away from dependence on any single market; critics in India worry about the effect on small farmers, dairy and manufacturing, and about whether labour and environmental conditions attached to trade will limit policy space.

The lesson for students is that economic diplomacy is domestic politics by other means. A tariff cut abroad can mean a livelihood question at home, and that is why even a government that wants open trade negotiates protections for sensitive sectors.

Key takeaways

  • United States tariffs in 2025 reached about 50% in combined terms, then a February 2026 framework proposed 18% — but the deal remained unfinished in late 2026.
  • The United Kingdom deal (July 2025) and the European Union deal (concluded January 2026) show diversification towards Europe.
  • Trade deals are judged in India not only by export gains, but by effects on farmers, workers and policy space.

Why it matters for civil studies: Trade questions test your ability to link an external tariff to a domestic constituency and to distinguish a framework, a signed agreement and an implemented deal.

Discussion question: When a trade partner links tariffs to a separate issue, such as oil purchases, should India treat trade as a purely economic negotiation?
