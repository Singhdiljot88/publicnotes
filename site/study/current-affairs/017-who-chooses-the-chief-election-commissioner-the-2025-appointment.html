Who chooses the Chief Election Commissioner? The 2025 appointment

Elections · Reference edition 1 November 2026 · 2 min read

Study context: GS II — Salient features of the Representation of People's Act; appointment to various Constitutional posts, powers, functions and responsibilities of various Constitutional Bodies.

Background explainer from the November 2026 study collection. Events discussed may predate the collection; this is not a monthly news update.

Article 324 of the Constitution vests the superintendence, direction, and control of elections in the Election Commission of India, headed by the Chief Election Commissioner and such other Election Commissioners as the President may fix. For decades, the appointment process was not spelled out in detail in the Constitution, and in practice the government of the day appointed Commissioners. That arrangement was challenged in the Supreme Court, which in March 2023 directed that appointments be made by a committee comprising the Prime Minister, the Leader of the Opposition, and the Chief Justice of India, until Parliament legislated.

Parliament did legislate, in December 2023, with the Chief Election Commissioner and Other Election Commissioners (Appointment, Conditions of Service and Term of Office) Act, 2023. The Act replaced the Chief Justice on the selection committee with a Union Cabinet Minister nominated by the Prime Minister, so that the committee consists of the Prime Minister as chair, the Leader of the Opposition in the Lok Sabha, and a Union Minister. A search committee led by the Law Minister first shortlists candidates, and the President formally appoints the person selected.

The first Chief Election Commissioner appointed under the new Act took office in February 2025. Gyanesh Kumar, a retired Indian Administrative Service officer of the 1988 Kerala cadre who had served as an Election Commissioner since March 2024, took charge as the 26th Chief Election Commissioner on 19 February 2025, succeeding Rajiv Kumar, who demitted office on 18 February 2025. The selection meeting on 17 February 2025 was attended by Prime Minister Narendra Modi, Home Minister Amit Shah as the nominated Minister, and Leader of the Opposition Rahul Gandhi, who recorded a dissent note urging that the appointment wait until the Supreme Court decided challenges to the 2023 Act.

The change matters because the Election Commission's independence is judged not only by what it does during an election, but by how its members are chosen. Supporters of the 2023 Act argue that Parliament is entitled to design the appointment process, and that the presence of the Leader of the Opposition provides a check. Critics argue that replacing the Chief Justice with a Minister gives the government two of the three votes on the committee, and that this weakens the independence the Supreme Court had sought to protect in 2023.

Challenges to the 2023 Act were already pending when the appointment was made, and the appointment process itself remained a subject of political debate. The case is therefore unfinished, and readers should distinguish between the law as enacted and the law as it may finally be upheld or modified by the Court.

Key takeaways

  • Gyanesh Kumar took charge as the 26th Chief Election Commissioner on 19 February 2025, the first appointed under the 2023 Act.
  • The 2023 Act's selection committee comprises the Prime Minister, the Leader of the Opposition, and a Union Minister nominated by the Prime Minister.
  • The Supreme Court's March 2023 direction had included the Chief Justice of India on the committee, until Parliament legislated.

Why it matters for civil studies

How an election watchdog is appointed shapes trust in it, even before a single vote is cast.

Think it through

Should the Chief Justice, a Minister, or neither sit on the committee that chooses election commissioners?
