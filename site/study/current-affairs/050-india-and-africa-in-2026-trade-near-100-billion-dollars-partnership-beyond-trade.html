India and Africa in 2026: trade near 100 billion dollars, partnership beyond trade

World affairs · Reference edition 1 November 2026 · 2 min read

Study context: GS II — India and its neighbourhood — relations; bilateral, regional and global groupings and agreements; important international institutions, agencies and fora.

Background explainer from the November 2026 study collection. Events discussed may predate the collection; this is not a monthly news update.

India's partnership with Africa in 2026 is best measured in two ways: the trade that already exists, and the cooperation that trade alone does not capture. Bilateral trade between India and Africa approached 100 billion dollars in 2024–25, spanning energy, infrastructure, agriculture and technology, and the African Development Bank and India intensified cooperation ahead of the fourth India–Africa Forum Summit planned for 2026, across digital infrastructure, industrial development and finance. India, a member of the Bank since 1983, has contributed to all of its general capital increases, and Indian companies won 173 contracts worth about 760 million dollars — 5.3 per cent of Bank procurement — between 2020 and 2025.

Digital public infrastructure has become the partnership's signature offer. India's experience building large-scale interoperable payment and identity systems is being used to support Africa's financial inclusion agenda, including a 2-million-dollar contribution to the African Digital Financial Inclusion Facility in 2023 and a 6-million-dollar India Trust Fund phase signed in 2024. Healthcare and industrial development form another pillar: through the African Pharmaceutical Technology Foundation, the Bank is promoting local manufacturing of medicines and vaccines to address an African pharmaceutical investment gap estimated at 111 billion dollars by 2030.

The human and agricultural face of the partnership was visible in October 2026, when the India–Africa Food Security, Agri Trade and Investment Summit met on 7 October in Greater Noida, bringing together representatives of more than 30 African countries, diplomats, scientists, industry leaders and farmers' organisations under the theme Partnering for Prosperity, Nourishing Future Generations. Discussions covered seeds and agricultural research, biotechnology, rice, grains and pulses, soil health, irrigation, fertilisers, agri-technology, climate-smart agriculture, food processing, trade corridors and investment — and the summit announced a follow-up Asia–Africa Seed Trade and Investment Summit for 2027.

The partnership's logic, as African officials at these meetings have framed it, is a shift from transactions to longer-term partnerships: connecting markets and institutions, collaborating on technology and knowledge, and building food-secure and prosperous agricultural systems. For India, Africa is also the proving ground of its Global South diplomacy — the region where its development cooperation, its digital public infrastructure exports and its voice for reform of global institutions are tested together. The question for the next phase is whether summit commitments convert into financing, factories and food systems, or remain a calendar of meetings around a trade figure that is already large.

Key takeaways

  • India–Africa trade approached 100 billion dollars in 2024–25, and Indian firms won 173 African Development Bank contracts worth about 760 million dollars in 2020–2025.
  • Digital public infrastructure is a signature offer, with Indian support for financial inclusion and a 6-million-dollar India Trust Fund phase in 2024.
  • The October 2026 India–Africa food security summit in Greater Noida drew over 30 African countries and announced a seed-trade summit for 2027.

Why it matters for civil studies

South–South cooperation is judged differently from trade: by technology shared, capacity built and food systems strengthened, not just by export totals.

Think it through

Should India measure its Africa partnership by trade volume, by development outcomes, or by diplomatic support for global reform — and can it maximise all three?
