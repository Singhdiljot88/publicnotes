UPI at ten: 25 billion payments a month

Society · Reference edition 1 November 2026 · 2 min read

Study context: GS I — Salient features of Indian Society, Diversity of India; role of women, population, poverty and developmental issues, urbanisation.

Background explainer from the November 2026 study collection. Events discussed may predate the collection; this is not a monthly news update.

In October 2026, India's Unified Payments Interface processed 25.47 billion transactions worth 31.53 lakh crore rupees, according to National Payments Corporation of India data, both monthly records. Average daily transaction value crossed 1 lakh crore rupees for the first time, at about 1,01,707 crore rupees a day, with about 822 million payments a day. Volume was up 23 per cent year-on-year against October 2025, when 20.70 billion transactions were recorded, and festive spending, including a peak of about 873 million transactions on 9 October ahead of Navratri, helped drive the record. UPI completed a decade of public operations in 2026, having launched in April 2016 with 21 banks; by 2026 more than 750 banks were connected, serving over 550 million registered users and more than 65 million merchants.

The year's progression shows steady growth rather than a single jump. In the financial year 2025-26, UPI processed 24,162 crore transactions worth 314 lakh crore rupees. Monthly volume crossed 23 billion for the first time in May 2026, reached 23.66 billion in July worth 29.88 lakh crore rupees, and 24.51 billion in August worth 29.82 lakh crore rupees, before the October record. UPI now processes nearly half of global real-time payment volume, according to analysis cited of the International Monetary Fund, and on its busiest days it processes more transactions than Visa and Mastercard combined worldwide, as reported by industry analysts.

The unresolved question is who pays for it. NPCI had planned to introduce a 0.4 per cent merchant discount rate for person-to-merchant UPI transactions above 2,000 rupees from 15 October 2026, ending six years of free merchant transactions, but the framework was deferred until after Diwali. The government has paid roughly 2,000 crore rupees a year in subsidies to banks and fintech firms to sustain the zero-fee model, while NPCI has said the real cost of running the system is closer to 20,000 crore rupees a year. Parliament's Standing Committee on Finance flagged the model's sustainability in its 32nd report.

The social meaning of UPI is easy to miss behind the numbers. A street vendor, a bus conductor, and a large retailer now settle on the same rails, instantly and interoperably, without needing to share bank details. That is why UPI is studied as digital public infrastructure rather than as a payments product: it is a public utility built by a public corporation, and its pricing, governance, and fraud controls are therefore public questions.

Key takeaways

  • UPI set monthly records in October 2026: 25.47 billion transactions worth 31.53 lakh crore rupees, with daily value crossing 1 lakh crore rupees for the first time.
  • From 21 banks in 2016, UPI grew to over 750 banks, 550 million users, and 65 million merchants by 2026, handling nearly half of global real-time payment volume.
  • A planned 0.4 per cent merchant fee on larger transactions was deferred past Diwali, leaving the question of who funds the free system unresolved.

Why it matters for civil studies

Digital public infrastructure shows how the state can build a platform others innovate on, and how pricing decisions for a free public utility become fiscal decisions.

Think it through

Should merchants pay a small fee for UPI, should the government keep subsidising it, or should the cost fall elsewhere?
