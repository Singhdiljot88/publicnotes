WPL 2026: RCB chase 204 to win a second title

Sports · Reference edition 1 November 2026 · 2 min read

Study context: GS II — Important aspects of governance, transparency and accountability (sports governance as a governance case study) — no dedicated UPSC syllabus topic for sport itself.

Background explainer from the November 2026 study collection. Events discussed may predate the collection; this is not a monthly news update.

The fourth edition of the Women's Premier League ran from 9 January to 5 February 2026, featuring five teams, Mumbai Indians, Royal Challengers Bengaluru, Delhi Capitals, UP Warriorz, and Gujarat Giants, playing 22 matches across Navi Mumbai and Vadodara. The final, on 5 February at the Baroda Cricket Association Stadium in Vadodara, produced the highest successful chase in WPL history. Delhi Capitals, batting first, scored 203 for 4, with captain Jemimah Rodrigues contributing 57 and Laura Wolvaardt 44. Royal Challengers Bengaluru replied with 204 for 4 in 19.4 overs to win by six wickets and claim their second title, after their first in 2024.

The chase was built on one partnership. Captain Smriti Mandhana scored 87 off 41 balls and Georgia Voll made 79, and their 165-run second-wicket stand turned the match in Bengaluru's favour. Mandhana was named player of the match, and she finished the season as Orange Cap winner with 377 runs. Gujarat Giants' Sophie Devine was named player of the tournament, and she also claimed the Purple Cap with 17 wickets. Delhi Capitals, for their part, suffered a fourth successive final defeat, having reached the final in every season of the WPL so far without winning one.

The result matters beyond the trophy because of what it says about the women's game in India. A 204-run chase in a final, played before a large audience and decided by two batters scoring at more than two runs a ball, is the kind of match that changes what young players believe is possible. The WPL's four seasons have produced four champions' campaigns, with Mumbai Indians winning in 2023 and 2025 and Bengaluru in 2024 and 2026, which suggests a competitive league rather than a one-team procession.

The structural question remains. Five teams, 22 matches, and a short January-to-February window make the WPL much smaller than the IPL, and its expansion, player pay, and domestic pathway will decide whether the 2026 final was a peak or a platform. Delhi's repeated final defeats are also a study in sporting psychology: reaching four finals proves a system works, and losing all four proves that the last step is the hardest.

Key takeaways

  • RCB beat Delhi Capitals by six wickets in the WPL 2026 final on 5 February in Vadodara, chasing a record 204 after Delhi's 203 for 4.
  • Smriti Mandhana (87 off 41) and Georgia Voll (79) shared a 165-run partnership; Mandhana won the Orange Cap with 377 runs.
  • Delhi Capitals lost a fourth consecutive WPL final, while RCB drew level with Mumbai Indians on two titles each.

Why it matters for civil studies

Women's leagues test whether sports policy delivers equal investment, media coverage, and pathways, not just equal celebration.

Think it through

What should the WPL expand first: more teams, a longer season, or higher pay for domestic players?
