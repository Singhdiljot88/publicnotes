Haryana 2024: a third term on a narrow vote margin

Elections · Reference edition 1 November 2026 · 2 min read

Study context: GS II — Salient features of the Representation of People's Act; appointment to various Constitutional posts, powers, functions and responsibilities of various Constitutional Bodies.

Background explainer from the November 2026 study collection. Events discussed may predate the collection; this is not a monthly news update.

Haryana voted in a single phase on 5 October 2024 to elect all 90 members of its Legislative Assembly, and counting took place on 8 October 2024. Exit polls had widely predicted a Congress victory, and the early trends on counting day appeared to support that expectation. By the end of counting, however, the result had reversed, and the ruling Bharatiya Janata Party had retained power.

The BJP won 48 seats, crossing the majority mark of 46, while the Congress won 37 seats. The Indian National Lok Dal won two seats and independents won three. The BJP's victory gave it a third consecutive term in Haryana, a first for any party in the state's history. Chief Minister Nayab Singh Saini, who had taken office earlier in 2024, continued as Chief Minister.

What made the result striking was how close the vote was. The BJP secured 39.94 per cent of the vote and the Congress 39.09 per cent, a gap of less than one percentage point, yet that translated into an 11-seat gap in the Assembly. That difference is a normal feature of first-past-the-post elections, in which a party can win many seats narrowly and lose others narrowly, but it also explains why the result felt unexpected to observers who had focused on vote share or on exit polls.

The Congress, which had complained during counting about a slowdown in the publication of trends on the Election Commission's website, did not succeed in overturning the result, and the Commission rejected the suggestion that the counting process itself was flawed. The episode is a useful reminder that a complaint about the speed of reporting is not the same as evidence about the validity of votes counted, and that the two should be assessed separately.

Haryana 2024 also matters for what it did not do. It did not produce a hung Assembly, it did not require post-poll bargaining for a majority, and it did not break the pattern of the BJP's state-level organisation converting a narrow vote lead into a workable majority. For students of elections, it is a clean example of how first-past-the-post can amplify a small vote advantage.

Key takeaways

  • The BJP won 48 of 90 seats in Haryana in 2024, and the Congress won 37, with counting on 8 October 2024.
  • The vote shares were close, 39.94 per cent for the BJP and 39.09 per cent for the Congress, yet the seat gap was 11.
  • The BJP's win gave it a third consecutive term in Haryana, the first time any party had done so.

Why it matters for civil studies

First-past-the-post can turn a vote lead of under one point into a clear Assembly majority.

Think it through

Should a state election that is this close in votes produce a clearer check on the winning party?
