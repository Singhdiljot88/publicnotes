Current affairs explainers · Note 047

The United Nations opens a global dialogue on AI governance

World affairs · Reference edition 1 November 2026 · 2 min read

Study context: GS II — India and its neighbourhood — relations; bilateral, regional and global groupings and agreements; important international institutions, agencies and fora.

Background explainer from the November 2026 study collection. Events discussed may predate the collection; this is not a monthly news update.

On 6–7 July 2026, the United Nations convened the first annual Global Dialogue on Artificial Intelligence Governance in Geneva, the first universal forum operating under a General Assembly mandate in which governments and other stakeholders can deliberate collectively on AI governance. Established by consensus through General Assembly Resolution 79/325 of August 2025, the dialogue brought together more than 4,200 participants from nearly 170 states, alongside international organisations, technology companies, civil society, academia and the technical community. It opened Geneva Digital Week and overlapped with the International Telecommunication Union's AI for Good Global Summit, held in Geneva from 7 to 10 July.

Secretary-General António Guterres opened the session with a warning: artificial intelligence is expanding faster than the legal rules designed to govern it. He renewed his call to prohibit lethal autonomous weapons systems that can make life-or-death decisions without meaningful human control, and proposed an AI Child Safety Pledge requiring developers to demonstrate that systems intended for children meet basic protections before release. The dialogue's sessions covered safety, equitable access, technical standards, capacity-building, interoperability and human rights, and the preliminary report of the Independent International Scientific Panel on AI was presented.

The central message from Geneva was that everyone agreed AI needs rules, and nobody agreed whose rules. The dialogue produced no treaty and no rulebook; a second session was scheduled for New York in May 2027. At the AI for Good Summit, the World Intellectual Property Organization's Assistant Director General Ken-Ichiro Natsume explained that member states had not yet reached agreement to begin treaty negotiations on AI and intellectual property, and discussions centred instead on three foundations: high-quality data, technological innovation and effective regulation. Private licensing agreements between AI developers and rights holders, speakers observed, were proliferating faster than legislation.

A parallel track showed a different model. On 29 September 2026, President Donald Trump and major American technology companies — Google, Anthropic, Meta, OpenAI, xAI and Nvidia — signed a voluntary agreement in Washington on the safe and responsible development of advanced AI models, setting out four levels of oversight. Kazakhstan's President Kassym-Jomart Tokayev then proposed developing that Washington Model into an international treaty under UN auspices, with proposals to be presented at the G20 summit in Miami in December 2026. The contrast captures the state of AI governance in 2026: a universal dialogue that compares approaches, a voluntary corporate model, and a treaty proposal — but no binding global rules yet.

Key takeaways

  • The UN's first Global Dialogue on AI Governance met in Geneva on 6–7 July 2026, with over 4,200 participants from nearly 170 states, under Resolution 79/325.
  • It produced no treaty: WIPO confirmed no consensus to negotiate one, and a second session was set for New York in May 2027.
  • A voluntary Washington agreement of September 2026 was proposed by Kazakhstan as the basis for a future UN AI treaty.

Why it matters for civil studies

Global governance often begins as dialogue: comparing national approaches can build the common ground that later treaties require — or stall indefinitely.

Think it through

Should AI be governed by a binding treaty, voluntary corporate commitments, or national laws first — and who loses while the world decides?
