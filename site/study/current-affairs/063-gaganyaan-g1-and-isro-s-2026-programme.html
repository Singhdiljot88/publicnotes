Gaganyaan G1 and ISRO's 2026 programme

Science · Reference edition 1 November 2026 · 2 min read

Study context: GS III — Science and Technology — developments and their applications and effects in everyday life; awareness in the fields of IT, Space, Computers, robotics, nano-technology, bio-technology.

Background explainer from the November 2026 study collection. Events discussed may predate the collection; this is not a monthly news update.

India's human spaceflight programme spent 2026 preparing for a flight with no humans aboard. The first uncrewed Gaganyaan mission, G1, was targeted for launch within calendar year 2026, according to the ISRO chairman's statement of September 2026, and it will carry Vyommitra, a half-humanoid robot that reports conditions inside the crew module. The logic is deliberate: before risking astronauts, ISRO wants a full orbital test of the crew module, its life-support systems, and its recovery. Two further uncrewed flights, G2 and G3, are expected in 2027, and the first crewed flight, H1, has no firm date yet because it depends on the results of those tests.

The groundwork visible in 2026 shows why the schedule has moved before. In October 2026 ISRO completed crew module–service module separation tests at the Vikram Sarabhai Space Centre in Thiruvananthapuram, validating the system that must separate the capsule cleanly before re-entry. In July 2026 the agency reported flotation tests for the crew module uprighting system, umbilical separation testing, and a main parachute air-drop test. The ISRO chairman said in September 2026 that more than 8,000 tests had been completed for the test flight. G1 was first planned for December 2020, and a December 2025 target stated in October 2025 was also missed, so the 2026 target should be read as a goal, not a guarantee.

Gaganyaan sits inside a larger approved programme. The Union Cabinet approved the programme's expansion in September 2024, alongside Chandrayaan-4, a lunar sample-return mission expected around 2028 at about 2,104 crore rupees, and Shukrayaan, a Venus orbiter with a March 2028 window at about 1,236 crore rupees. The first module of the planned Bharatiya Antariksh Station is targeted for 2028, with the full station aimed at 2035. The four astronaut-designates are Indian Air Force officers, including Group Captains Prasanth Balakrishnan Nair, Ajit Krishnan and Angad Pratap, and Wing Commander Shubhanshu Shukla.

The deeper point is institutional. A crewed programme forces a space agency to prove reliability in public, test by test, because failure costs lives rather than hardware. That is why the uncrewed sequence, the robot passenger, and the slow release of test results matter more than any single launch date.

Key takeaways

  • G1, the first uncrewed Gaganyaan flight carrying the robot Vyommitra, was targeted for 2026, with two more uncrewed flights expected in 2027 before any crewed flight.
  • October 2026 separation tests and earlier parachute and flotation tests validated key crew-module systems ahead of G1.
  • The same approved programme includes Chandrayaan-4 around 2028, a Venus orbiter in March 2028, and a first space-station module by 2028.

Why it matters for civil studies

Large technology programmes test how governments set targets, report delays honestly, and balance prestige against safety.

Think it through

When a flagship mission misses repeated targets, should the agency revise the date, the scope, or the way it communicates?
