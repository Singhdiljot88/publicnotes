IPL 2026: RCB defend the crown

Sports · Reference edition 1 November 2026 · 2 min read

Study context: GS II — Important aspects of governance, transparency and accountability (sports governance as a governance case study) — no dedicated UPSC syllabus topic for sport itself.

Background explainer from the November 2026 study collection. Events discussed may predate the collection; this is not a monthly news update.

The 2026 Indian Premier League, the 19th edition of the tournament, ran from 28 March to 31 May 2026, with ten teams playing 74 matches across 13 venues. On 31 May, at the Narendra Modi Stadium in Ahmedabad, defending champions Royal Challengers Bengaluru defeated Gujarat Titans by five wickets in the final to secure their second consecutive title. Gujarat, batting first after Bengaluru won the toss and chose to field, scored 155 for 8, and Bengaluru chased the target down in 18 overs, reaching 161 for 5, with Virat Kohli unbeaten on 75 off 42 balls and named player of the match.

The result placed Bengaluru in rare company. They became only the third franchise, after Chennai Super Kings and Mumbai Indians, to win back-to-back IPL titles, and the first franchise to win the IPL and the Women's Premier League in the same year. Their route to the final was direct: a 92-run win over Gujarat Titans in Qualifier 1 sent them straight through, while Gujarat beat Rajasthan Royals by seven wickets in Qualifier 2 to set up a rematch. In the final, a three-wicket haul from Rasikh Salam restricted Gujarat, and a fast start from Venkatesh Iyer, 32 off 16, put Bengaluru in control of the chase, with Kohli pacing the remainder.

The season's individual honours told a different story from the team result. The awards were dominated by Rajasthan Royals sensation Vaibhav Sooryavanshi, who was named most valuable player and won the Orange Cap with 776 runs, while Kagiso Rabada of Gujarat Titans won the Purple Cap with 29 wickets. That a player from a team that did not reach the final could dominate the awards is a reminder that T20 cricket rewards individual brilliance within a team structure, and that the best player and the best team are different questions.

Bengaluru's back-to-back titles also closed a narrative arc. The franchise had waited 18 seasons for a first title, winning it in 2025 against Punjab Kings, and then defended it at the first attempt. For the league, the result confirmed the IPL's competitive balance: no franchise has built a dynasty, and the tournament's economics, auction, and player turnover keep resetting the field each year.

Key takeaways

  • Royal Challengers Bengaluru beat Gujarat Titans by five wickets in the IPL 2026 final on 31 May in Ahmedabad, chasing 156 with 12 balls to spare.
  • RCB became the third franchise to defend the IPL title, and the first to win the IPL and WPL in the same year.
  • Vaibhav Sooryavanshi (776 runs, Orange Cap and MVP) and Kagiso Rabada (29 wickets, Purple Cap) won the season's individual awards.

Why it matters for civil studies

The IPL is India's largest sports economy, shaping broadcasting, advertising, urban revenues, and youth aspiration, so its governance and economics are policy subjects.

Think it through

Does a franchise league's auction system create fairer competition than traditional club structures, or does money still decide?
