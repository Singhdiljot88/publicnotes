Current affairs explainers · Note 028

UPI crosses 20 billion transactions in a month

Economy · Reference edition 1 November 2026 · 2 min read

Study context: GS III — Indian Economy and issues relating to planning, mobilisation of resources, growth, development and employment; Government Budgeting.

Background explainer from the November 2026 study collection. Events discussed may predate the collection; this is not a monthly news update.

The Unified Payments Interface, or UPI, is a real-time payment system developed by the National Payments Corporation of India, which allows instant transfer of money between bank accounts using a mobile phone. Launched in 2016, UPI allows a user to pay a merchant, a friend, or a service provider by scanning a code or entering an identifier, without needing to share bank account details, and it operates around the clock, including on holidays.

In August 2025, UPI crossed 20 billion transactions in a single month for the first time. The platform recorded 20.01 billion transactions in August, up from 19.47 billion in July, with a total value of 24.85 lakh crore rupees, slightly lower than July's 25.08 lakh crore rupees. The average daily volume was 645 million transactions, with an average daily value of about 80,177 crore rupees, and on some days daily volume crossed 700 million. The milestone was announced by the Department of Financial Services and the National Payments Corporation of India.

Year-on-year growth was strong. Transaction volume was about 33 to 34 per cent higher than in August 2024, when about 14.9 billion transactions were recorded, while value was about 21 to 24 per cent higher, depending on the precise comparison used in different reports. The platform had 684 banks live on it in July and August 2025, giving it a reach that spans large public and private banks as well as smaller banks and payment providers.

The milestone matters for three reasons. First, it shows the scale of India's digital payment infrastructure, which now handles more transactions in a month than many countries' payment systems handle in a year. Second, it shows how a public infrastructure, built by a non-profit company owned by banks and the Reserve Bank, can support a competitive market of apps and providers on top of it. Third, it raises questions about fraud, failed transactions, and digital exclusion, since a system used by hundreds of millions of people must also work for people with limited connectivity, literacy, or access to a smartphone.

UPI's growth has also attracted international interest, with several countries exploring similar instant payment systems or linking to UPI for cross-border payments. That interest, however, should not obscure the domestic challenge: maintaining reliability, security, and low cost at a scale of 20 billion transactions a month, while ensuring that the benefits reach small merchants and rural users as well as urban smartphone users.

Key takeaways

  • UPI recorded 20.01 billion transactions in August 2025, for the first time crossing 20 billion in a month, with a value of 24.85 lakh crore rupees.
  • UPI was launched by the National Payments Corporation of India in 2016 and had 684 banks live on it in August 2025.
  • Average daily volume in August 2025 was 645 million transactions, with an average daily value of about 80,177 crore rupees.

Why it matters for civil studies

A public payment infrastructure can support a competitive market, but scale also creates new risks around fraud and exclusion.

Think it through

Should instant payments be free for users, or should someone pay for the infrastructure that makes them possible?
