The TB elimination drive: a public-health campaign at scale

Science · Reference edition 1 November 2026 · 2 min read

Study context: GS III — Science and Technology — developments and their applications and effects in everyday life; awareness in the fields of IT, Space, Computers, robotics, nano-technology, bio-technology.

Background explainer from the November 2026 study collection. Events discussed may predate the collection; this is not a monthly news update.

Tuberculosis is curable, yet it still kills, which is why India's elimination drive is as much a test of administration as of medicine. On World TB Day 2026, 24 March, Union Health Minister Jagat Prakash Nadda announced at Greater Noida that India had recorded a 21 per cent decline in TB incidence and a 25 per cent decline in mortality over the past decade, against a 12 per cent global decline in incidence over 2015 to 2024, with treatment coverage reported at 92 per cent against 78 per cent globally. The government has set its elimination goal ahead of the United Nations Sustainable Development Goal target of 2030. These are government-reported figures, and they describe estimated rates, not a count of every case.

The campaign's machinery is visible in its numbers. A 100-day intensified TB Mukt Bharat campaign, launched on World TB Day 2026, covered 1.58 lakh villages and urban wards with locally tailored micro-plans, building on an intensified campaign begun in December 2024 that used portable X-rays, AI-enabled diagnostics, and molecular testing. A government backgrounder of 13 October 2026 reported cumulative screenings of more than 28.1 crore people under the intensified campaign, more than 20 lakh people given TB preventive treatment, and 5.7 lakh new Ni-kshay Mitras joining the community-support initiative. Government funding for TB elimination rose from 640 crore rupees in 2015-16 to 6,356 crore rupees in 2025-26, according to the minister.

The newest instrument is young people. In October 2026 the Health Ministry launched youth-led JOSH, Joint Squad for Health, teams in 55 districts across 11 states and union territories, each team bringing together five MY Bharat volunteers and five National Cadet Corps cadets to raise awareness, encourage early diagnosis, support patients in completing treatment, and address stigma. The first phase covers 55 districts, with expansion planned across the country. The design recognises a medical fact: TB treatment only works if patients finish it, and finishing it often depends on food, encouragement, and someone noticing when a patient disappears.

The cautions are part of the record too. Screening totals measure reach, not cures; treatment coverage measures how much of the estimated burden is reached by services; and certification of a panchayat as TB-free reflects the programme's framework, not a permanent absence of risk. The drive's real test will be whether incidence keeps falling after the campaigns end.

Key takeaways

  • India reported a 21 per cent fall in TB incidence and 25 per cent fall in mortality over the past decade, with 92 per cent treatment coverage, ahead of the global pace.
  • The 100-day TB Mukt Bharat campaign covered 1.58 lakh villages and wards, with 28.1 crore cumulative screenings reported by October 2026.
  • Community support, from 7.16 lakh Ni-kshay Mitras to new youth-led JOSH teams, is treated as part of treatment, not an add-on.

Why it matters for civil studies

Disease elimination shows how a state combines budgets, technology, volunteers, and local plans, and how to read official health statistics without mistaking activity for outcome.

Think it through

When a campaign reports screenings and certifications, what evidence would convince you that transmission itself is actually falling?
