The India–New Zealand free trade agreement: nine months from launch to signature

Trade · Reference edition 1 November 2026 · 2 min read

Study context: GS III — Indian Economy — growth, development and employment; effects of liberalisation on the economy (trade).

Background explainer from the November 2026 study collection. Events discussed may predate the collection; this is not a monthly news update.

On 20 October 2026, the India–New Zealand Free Trade Agreement entered into force, completing one of the fastest trade negotiations India has ever concluded. Negotiations were launched on 16 March 2025 by Commerce and Industry Minister Piyush Goyal and New Zealand Trade and Investment Minister Todd McClay, substantively concluded on 22 December 2025 after five formal rounds, signed in New Delhi on 27 April 2026, and ratified by both countries by 21 September 2026. The New Zealand Parliament passed the implementing legislation on 16 September 2026 by 93 votes to 29.

The market-access bargain is deliberately lopsided. New Zealand removed duties on every tariff line for Indian exports from day one, eliminating tariffs of up to 10 per cent on textiles, clothing, leather, footwear, ceramics, carpets, automobiles and auto components. India, in return, will cut or remove duties on about 70 per cent of its tariff lines, covering 95 per cent of bilateral trade by value; about 57 per cent of New Zealand's exports to India enter duty-free immediately, including sheepmeat, wool, coal and most forestry products, with a second round of tariff cuts following on 1 January 2027. India kept protections for dairy and several agricultural products, the politically sensitive core of the negotiation.

Beyond goods, the agreement carries a New Zealand commitment to facilitate 20 billion dollars of investment into India over fifteen years, access for Indian service providers across about 118 sectors and sub-sectors, and new temporary-entry routes for Indian workers and students. The two prime ministers have set an aspirational target of doubling bilateral trade in goods and services to 7 billion New Zealand dollars by 2030. The starting point is modest: in 2025–26 India exported 566.51 million dollars of goods to New Zealand and imported 589.1 million dollars, and total bilateral trade is around 1.3 billion dollars.

Speed was possible because the stakes were small and the politics were aligned: New Zealand wanted agricultural and forestry access, India wanted a demonstration that it could close a modern agreement quickly and give its exporters, especially small firms, a new market. The harder test begins now, as the Commerce Ministry itself has acknowledged: converting zero-duty access into orders requires meeting New Zealand standards, finding distributors, and competing on price and delivery with suppliers that already enjoy preferential access there.

Key takeaways

  • Negotiations launched in March 2025 were concluded in December 2025, signed on 27 April 2026 and entered into force on 20 October 2026.
  • New Zealand removed duties on 100 per cent of Indian tariff lines from day one; India cut duties on about 70 per cent of lines covering 95 per cent of trade value, protecting dairy.
  • New Zealand also committed to facilitate 20 billion dollars of investment into India over fifteen years, with a goal of doubling bilateral trade by 2030.

Why it matters for civil studies

Small deals can be strategic: they test negotiating machinery, create precedents on services and investment, and give exporters a low-risk new market.

Think it through

Is a fast, small deal with a 1.3-billion-dollar partner worth more as a template for bigger negotiations, or as trade in its own right?
