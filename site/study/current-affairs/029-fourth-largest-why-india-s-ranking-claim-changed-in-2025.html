Fourth largest? Why India's ranking claim changed in 2025

Economy · Reference edition 1 November 2026 · 2 min read

Study context: GS III — Indian Economy and issues relating to planning, mobilisation of resources, growth, development and employment; Government Budgeting.

Background explainer from the November 2026 study collection. Events discussed may predate the collection; this is not a monthly news update.

In May 2025, NITI Aayog Chief Executive Officer B. V. R. Subrahmanyam told a press briefing, after the 10th Governing Council meeting, that India had become the world's fourth-largest economy. His words were widely reported: we are the fourth largest economy as I speak. We are a 4 trillion dollar economy as I speak, and this is not my data. This is IMF data. India today is larger than Japan. Until 2024, India had been the world's fifth-largest economy, behind the United States, China, Germany, and Japan.

The claim rested on a projection, not on final data. The International Monetary Fund's World Economic Outlook of April 2025 projected India's nominal gross domestic product at 4,187 billion dollars for 2025, just ahead of Japan's 4,186 billion dollars, a margin of about one billion dollars on economies of more than four trillion dollars each. A margin that small can be reversed by a small change in growth, prices, or exchange rates, and it was not a measured, final ranking.

By October 2025, the IMF's updated outlook had reversed the order. Japan was projected at 4,280 billion dollars and India at 4,125 billion dollars, putting Japan back in fourth place and India in fifth. The change reflected, among other factors, a weaker rupee, which reduces India's dollar-denominated GDP, and stronger-than-expected performance in Japan, including yen appreciation. Contemporary analysis noted that India's nominal growth in the April to June quarter had been lower than projected, while Japan's real growth and currency had performed better.

The episode is a useful lesson in how to read rankings. A ranking based on nominal GDP in current United States dollars is sensitive to exchange rates, so a country can rise or fall in the ranking without any change in what its people actually produce. A ranking based on purchasing power parity, which adjusts for price differences, gives a different order, and a ranking based on GDP per person, which divides output by population, gives a very different picture again, with India far lower than fourth.

The government's year-end economic review, reported in December 2025, again described India as having overtaken Japan, with GDP valued at 4.18 trillion dollars, and said the ranking would be confirmed in 2026 once final annual data were released. As of 1 November 2026, readers should therefore treat fourth largest as a contested claim based on projections and on the choice of measure, not as a settled fact, and should check which vintage of IMF data and which measure, nominal or purchasing power parity, a particular claim uses.

Key takeaways

  • In May 2025 the NITI Aayog CEO said India had become the fourth-largest economy, citing IMF data, with India at 4,187 billion dollars and Japan at 4,186 billion in the April 2025 outlook.
  • The IMF's October 2025 outlook reversed the order, putting Japan at 4,280 billion dollars and India at 4,125 billion dollars.
  • Nominal GDP rankings in dollars are sensitive to exchange rates, so a small currency move can reverse a ranking based on a one-billion-dollar margin.

Why it matters for civil studies

A ranking that depends on a one-billion-dollar margin on a four-trillion-dollar economy is a projection, not a settled fact.

Think it through

Should India celebrate a size ranking, or focus on income per person and living standards?
