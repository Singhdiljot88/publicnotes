The end of Left-Wing Extremism: how India declared itself Naxal-free

Security · Reference edition 1 November 2026 · 2 min read

Study context: GS III — Security challenges and their management in border areas; role of external state and non-state actors; various security forces and agencies and their mandate.

Background explainer from the November 2026 study collection. Events discussed may predate the collection; this is not a monthly news update.

On 31 March 2026, the Union government's self-imposed deadline for eliminating Left-Wing Extremism arrived, and the Ministry of Home Affairs declared the country effectively free of the insurgency. Prime Minister Narendra Modi, speaking at the Rashtriya Ekta Diwas gathering in Ekta Nagar, Gujarat, on 31 October 2026, said that 126 districts were affected by Left-Wing Extremism in 2015 and that by March 2026 no district remained on the affected list. The arc is striking: the Home Affairs list of affected districts fell from 126 before April 2018 to 90 in April 2018, 70 in July 2021, 38 in April 2024 and eight in December 2025, before reaching zero in March–April 2026. Thirty-seven districts remain classified as legacy and thrust districts, receiving security and development support while the gains settle, and one is a district of concern.

The insurgency began in Naxalbari, West Bengal, in 1967 and sought to overthrow the Indian state through protracted people's war. Its armed core, the Communist Party of India (Maoist), was formed on 21 September 2004 from the merger of the People's War Group and the Maoist Communist Centre, and is banned as a terrorist organisation. At its peak it affected more than 180 districts across the forested spine of central and eastern India — the Red Corridor — with Bastar in southern Chhattisgarh as its last stronghold. The government's response combined security operations under the SAMADHAN doctrine and a clear-hold-build strategy with development saturation through the Aspirational Districts Programme and placement-linked skill training.

The final phase was marked by decapitation and surrender. Over the past three years, nearly 4,800 insurgents surrendered, more than 2,200 were arrested and over 700 were neutralised, according to a summary of the campaign published in April 2026; in 2025 alone, the South Asia Terrorism Portal counted 333 Maoists killed, 398 arrested and 1,351 surrendered. The surrender on 15 October 2025 of Mallojula Venugopal, a Politburo and Central Committee member and the movement's spokesman, in Gadchiroli, Maharashtra, was described as a decisive ideological and operational rupture. Violent incidents halved, and the Maoist Politburo was reported down to a single active member.

Declarations deserve scrutiny as well as recognition. Analysts note that residual violence and splinter activity may persist, that the causes the insurgency fed on — underdevelopment, tribal rights, forest disputes, recruitment networks and security gaps — are not erased by a change in classification, and that the official claim is best read as the dismantling of the organised insurgency rather than the end of every grievance in the former conflict zone. The government's own framing acknowledges this: legacy districts keep receiving support precisely so that the gains, in roads, mobile connectivity, schools and healthcare, are not lost.

Key takeaways

  • The Home Affairs affected-district list fell from 126 in 2015 to zero by March 2026, the government's deadline for eliminating Left-Wing Extremism.
  • The campaign combined SAMADHAN security operations and clear-hold-build strategy with development saturation in affected districts.
  • Nearly 4,800 insurgents surrendered in three years, but 37 legacy districts still receive support and residual splinter violence may persist.

Why it matters for civil studies

Counter-insurgency has two halves — security operations and development — and the second half decides whether the first half's gains last.

Think it through

When a government declares an insurgency over, what should citizens watch to judge whether the declaration is real: incidents, surrenders, or development in former strongholds?
