Measuring work: employment, unemployment and the informal workforce

Economy · Reference edition 1 November 2026 · 2 min read

Study context: GS III — Indian Economy and issues relating to planning, mobilisation of resources, growth, development and employment; inclusive growth and issues arising from it.

Employment statistics answer a deceptively simple question: who counts as working? In India, the main official source is the Periodic Labour Force Survey, or PLFS, conducted by the National Statistical Office. It reports indicators such as the labour force participation rate, the worker population ratio and the unemployment rate, broken down by rural and urban areas, sex and age. How these are defined decides what the headline numbers can and cannot show.

A person is counted as employed if they worked for at least some time in the reference period, including unpaid work in a family enterprise, which is common in agriculture and household businesses. Unemployment, in the usual-status sense, refers to people who are available for work and seeking it but without work. Because many people cannot afford to remain without any work, open unemployment can look low even where underemployment is widespread: people may work fewer hours than they want, or in jobs far below their skills, and still count as employed.

India's workforce is dominated by informal employment, meaning work without formal contracts, paid leave or social security coverage in many cases. This includes self-employment, casual wage work, domestic work and much of agriculture and construction. Informal workers are harder to survey, their incomes fluctuate, and they are often the first to lose work in a downturn and the last to regain it. Any employment debate that looks only at formal payroll data will therefore miss most of the workforce.

Measurement choices also shape gender analysis. Women's paid work is often under-recorded when it overlaps with unpaid household work, and participation rates differ sharply between survey definitions. A careful reader compares trends from the same survey and definition over time, asks whether a change reflects more jobs or a change in method, and looks at the quality of work, including wages, hours, security and safety, alongside the headline rate.

Key takeaways

  • The PLFS is the main official source for labour force participation, worker population and unemployment rates in India.
  • Open unemployment can understate distress because underemployment and informal work are widespread.
  • Employment quality, including wages, hours and security, matters as much as the headline unemployment rate.

Why it matters for civil studies

Jobs policy cannot be judged from one headline rate; the definitions behind the numbers decide what counts as work.

Think it through

Why might unemployment appear low in a region where most people still describe themselves as struggling for decent work?
