Allied sectors: dairy, livestock and fisheries beyond crops

Agriculture · Reference edition 1 November 2026 · 2 min read

Study context: GS III — Major crops, cropping patterns, irrigation, storage, transport and marketing of agricultural produce; direct and indirect farm subsidies and minimum support prices; Public Distribution System; land reforms in India; food processing and related industries.

Allied sectors, including animal husbandry, dairying, fisheries and poultry, now provide a large and growing share of agricultural value and a steadier income than seasonal crops. Milk is produced daily, eggs and poultry give frequent cash flow, and fish farming can use ponds and tanks unsuitable for crops. For landless and small households, livestock and fisheries often matter more than crop income, because they require less land, though they require feed, veterinary care, water and market access.

Dairying is India's best-known allied success. Operation Flood, implemented in phases from 1970, built village cooperative societies that collected milk, district unions that processed it, and state federations that marketed it, with the Amul cooperative in Gujarat as the model. The National Dairy Development Board supported this structure, and India became the world's largest milk producer. The cooperative model gave farmers a daily buyer and a share in processing margins, though its success varied by state, and private dairies now compete with cooperatives, raising questions about procurement prices and farmer bargaining power.

Fisheries include marine capture, inland capture and aquaculture, with the Blue Revolution and later the Pradhan Mantri Matsya Sampada Yojana, launched in 2020, supporting infrastructure, hatcheries, cold chains and fisher welfare. Fisheries provide protein and employment, especially in coastal and riverine communities, but face overfishing, pollution, conflicts over fishing zones and the vulnerability of small fishers to weather and market shocks. Livestock faces its own risks: fodder scarcity, disease outbreaks such as foot-and-mouth disease and lumpy skin disease, and the cost of veterinary services, which are often weaker in remote areas.

Policy for allied sectors must balance production, welfare and sustainability. Vaccination, breed improvement, fodder development, insurance for livestock, and cold chains for milk and fish all raise productivity, but they require public investment and extension services that reach small producers. Climate change adds pressure through heat stress on animals, water scarcity and changing fish stocks. For civil studies, allied sectors show why agricultural policy cannot focus on crops alone: diversification into livestock and fisheries is often the most practical route to steadier rural incomes and better nutrition.

Key takeaways

  • Allied sectors give steadier, often daily income than seasonal crops, and matter most for landless and small households.
  • Operation Flood, from 1970, built dairy cooperatives from village societies to state federations, with Amul as the model.
  • Pradhan Mantri Matsya Sampada Yojana, launched in 2020, supports fisheries infrastructure, cold chains and fisher welfare.

Why it matters for civil studies

Diversification beyond crops is often the most reliable way to stabilise rural incomes and nutrition.

Think it through

Should public support favour crop productivity, or allied sectors that give daily income, when budgets cannot fund both equally?
