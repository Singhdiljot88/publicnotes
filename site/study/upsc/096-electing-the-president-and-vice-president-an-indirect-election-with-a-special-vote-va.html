UPSC foundations · Note 096

Electing the President and Vice-President: an indirect election with a special vote value

Constitution and political system · Reference edition 1 November 2026 · 2 min read

Study context: GS II — Indian Constitution — historical underpinnings, evolution, features, amendments, significant provisions and basic structure.

The President is not directly elected by voters. Under Article 54, the President is elected by an electoral college consisting of the elected members of both Houses of Parliament and the elected members of the legislative assemblies of states and of the union territories of Delhi and Puducherry. Nominated members do not vote in the presidential election. The election follows the system of proportional representation by means of the single transferable vote, and voting is by secret ballot.

The value of votes is weighted to balance the Union and the states. The value of a member of a legislative assembly's vote is broadly based on the population of the state divided by the number of elected assembly members, while the value of a member of Parliament's vote is based on the total value of assembly votes divided by the total number of elected members of Parliament. The design ensures that neither Parliament alone nor the states alone can elect the President, and that larger states carry greater weight without entirely overwhelming smaller ones.

The Vice-President is elected differently. Under Article 66, the Vice-President is elected by an electoral college consisting of the members of both Houses of Parliament, including nominated members, by proportional representation through the single transferable vote. State assemblies do not vote. The Vice-President is the ex officio Chairman of the Rajya Sabha, and acts as President when the President's office is vacant or the President is unable to discharge functions.

These indirect elections reflect the President's role as head of state in a parliamentary system. The President is not a rival centre of popular mandate to the Prime Minister, but a constitutional head whose election draws authority from both Parliament and the states. The single transferable vote also allows voters to mark preferences, so that a candidate needs broad support across the college rather than a simple first-preference plurality alone.

Key takeaways

  • The President is elected by an electoral college of elected MPs and elected MLAs, including Delhi and Puducherry, under Article 54.
  • Voting is by proportional representation through the single transferable vote, with weighted vote values balancing Parliament and the states.
  • The Vice-President is elected by MPs alone under Article 66 and serves as ex officio Chairman of the Rajya Sabha.

Why it matters for civil studies

Indirect presidential elections are a design choice: they give the head of state legitimacy from Parliament and the states without creating a rival popular mandate.

Think it through

Should the President be elected by a wider college, by Parliament alone, or directly by voters — and what would each choice do to the parliamentary system?
