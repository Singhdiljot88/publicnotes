India and West Asia: energy, workers and a changing Gulf

International relations and India · Reference edition 1 November 2026 · 2 min read

Study context: GS II — India and its neighbourhood — relations; bilateral, regional and global groupings and agreements involving India and/or affecting India's interests; effect of policies and politics of developed and developing countries on India's interests, Indian Diaspora.

West Asia, often called the Middle East, is central to India's energy security, trade and diaspora. The region supplies a large share of India's crude oil and liquefied natural gas imports, and Gulf countries host millions of Indian workers, whose remittances support households across India, particularly in Kerala and other coastal and southern states. India's relationships in the region therefore combine foreign policy with the welfare of citizens abroad, including evacuation operations when conflict erupts.

India's approach has been to maintain relations with all major actors in the region, including Israel, Palestine, Iran, Saudi Arabia and the United Arab Emirates, rather than aligning exclusively with one side. India recognised Israel in 1950 and established full diplomatic relations in 1992, while continuing to support a two-state solution for Israel and Palestine. Relations with Saudi Arabia and the United Arab Emirates have deepened through strategic partnership agreements, defence cooperation, investment and energy deals, including long-term supply arrangements.

The Abraham Accords of 2020, under which the United Arab Emirates and Bahrain normalised relations with Israel, changed the region's diplomacy and created new possibilities for cooperation, including through the I2U2 grouping of India, Israel, the United Arab Emirates and the United States, which focuses on food security, water, energy, transport and space. India has also sought connectivity through the India-Middle East-Europe Economic Corridor, announced in 2023, linking India with the Gulf and Europe by sea and rail, although its implementation depends on regional stability.

West Asia tests India's ability to balance competing interests: energy imports, worker welfare, defence ties, and positions on conflicts in which partners disagree. A policy that works for one relationship may strain another, and the test of the approach is whether India can protect its citizens abroad, secure energy supplies at affordable prices, and maintain credibility with all sides at once.

Key takeaways

  • West Asia supplies a large share of India's oil and gas imports and hosts millions of Indian workers whose remittances support households in India.
  • India recognised Israel in 1950, established full diplomatic relations in 1992, and continues to support a two-state solution for Israel and Palestine.
  • The I2U2 grouping of India, Israel, the UAE and the US, and the India-Middle East-Europe Corridor announced in 2023, reflect new regional cooperation.

Why it matters for civil studies

West Asia combines foreign policy with citizen welfare: energy, remittances and evacuations make it a domestic issue as well as a diplomatic one.

Think it through

Can India maintain relations with all sides in West Asia indefinitely, or will a future conflict force it to choose?
