India Notes

Post-Independence India — 1947 to 2000

Building a republic, surviving wars and Emergency, and opening the economy.

The timeline

Date / periodEvent / person / milestone — why it matters
26 January 1950Constitution comes into force — India becomes a republic; President Rajendra Prasad, Prime Minister Jawaharlal Nehru.
A written constitution with fundamental rights, federalism and parliamentary democracy frames everything after.
1951–1952First general election — universal adult franchise, Election Commission under Sukumar Sen.
The world's largest democracy votes on a scale never attempted before.
1956States Reorganisation Act — states reorganised largely on linguistic lines.
Language becomes a principle of state formation, with lasting political consequences.
1962India–China war.
A defeat that reshapes defence, foreign policy and Nehru's legacy.
1964–1966Nehru dies (1964); Lal Bahadur Shastri (1964–66), slogan 'Jai Jawan, Jai Kisan'; Tashkent (1966).
Succession, food security and war with Pakistan test the young republic.
1965, 1971Wars with Pakistan; 1971 war leads to the creation of Bangladesh.
1971 is both a military victory and a humanitarian crisis — refugees, not just armies, drive it.
1975–1977Emergency (25 June 1975–21 March 1977) — press censorship, arrests, forced sterilisation drives; Janata victory in 1977.
The strongest test of democratic institutions — and their recovery by election.
1984Operation Blue Star, assassination of Indira Gandhi, anti-Sikh violence; Bhopal gas tragedy (December 1984).
A year of trauma in Punjab, Delhi and Bhopal that still shapes debates on security and accountability.
1991Economic liberalisation — Manmohan Singh as Finance Minister, delicensing, opening to trade and investment.
The end of the Licence Raj and the start of high-growth, service-led change — with new inequalities.
199273rd and 74th Amendments — panchayats and municipalities given constitutional status.
Local government becomes a third tier, with reserved seats for women and Scheduled Castes/Tribes.
1998Pokhran-II nuclear tests (May 1998).
India declares itself a nuclear-weapons state, with sanctions and a new strategic debate.
1999Kargil War.
A limited war in Kashmir that tests nuclear-age restraint and civil–military relations.

Key takeaways

  • The Constitution survived war, Emergency and linguistic reorganisation.
  • Economic change in 1991 was a policy choice with winners and losers, not an automatic boom.
  • Local government, not just Delhi, is where most citizens meet the state.

Why it matters: It is the direct background to today's politics, economy and borders.

Discussion / revision question: Did the Emergency strengthen Indian democracy in the long run by showing its limits — or weaken it?
