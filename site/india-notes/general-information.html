India Notes

Bharat: General Information — Chapter 1

India at a glance: its shape, its states, its records, and its national symbols — with the book's errors corrected.

1. India at a glance — the shape of the country

India covers about 3.28 million square kilometres, the world's seventh-largest country by land area. It stretches roughly 3,214 km from north to south and 2,933 km from east to west, so one country contains Himalayan cold, Thar desert heat, and tropical coasts. Its clock is Indian Standard Time, following the meridian of 82°30′ East longitude, passing near Mirzapur in Uttar Pradesh. The geographical centre is usually given as Karondi village in Katni district, Madhya Pradesh.

The southernmost point of the Indian Union is Indira Point in the Andaman and Nicobar Islands (Great Nicobar). On the mainland, the southern tip is Kanyakumari. The western and eastern coasts open onto the Arabian Sea and the Bay of Bengal.

3. Records of land, water and building

  • Longest sea beach in India: Marina Beach, Chennai — about 13 km along the Bay of Bengal (the promenade itself is about 6 km).
  • Highest waterfall named in the book: Kunchikal Falls, Karnataka, about 455 m. Waterfall heights are measured and ranked in different ways, so treat 'highest' claims with care.
  • Highest battlefield in the world: Siachen, Ladakh.
  • Longest river bridge: the Bhupen Hazarika Setu (Dhola–Sadiya Bridge) over the Lohit, a Brahmaputra tributary, in Assam.
  • Longest road tunnel: the Atal Tunnel, about 9 km, in Himachal Pradesh.
  • Largest residential building: Rashtrapati Bhavan, New Delhi — the President's official residence and estate.

5. Corrections to the book — read these before you quote it

  • Spelling: the book prints 'Bharat Ratan' in places; the honour is Bharat Ratna.
  • Highest peak: the book names Godwin-Austen (K-2). K-2 lies in territory claimed by India but administered by Pakistan. The highest peak wholly within India is Kangchenjunga (8,586 m, on the Sikkim–Nepal border). State both facts, and say which one a question is asking for.
  • Population: the book's 142.7 crore is a UN-based estimate from around the book's edition. Later UN estimates put India's population higher (around 146 crore in 2025), and India's last completed Census remains 2011 (121 crore). Do not quote 142.7 crore as today's figure without a date.
  • Forest cover: the book's 24.62% is forest plus tree cover. Forest cover alone is about 21.8% in recent India State of Forest Reports — check which measure a question wants.

Key takeaways

  • Area 3.28 million sq km; north–south 3,214 km; east–west 2,933 km; IST = 82°30′ E.
  • 28 states, 8 Union Territories; Rajasthan largest by area, Goa smallest; Uttar Pradesh largest by population, Sikkim smallest.
  • K-2 is the highest peak in India-claimed territory; Kangchenjunga is the highest peak wholly in India — that distinction is a classic quiz trap.

Why it matters: It is the one-page foundation every other topic in this set builds on — get the shape, size and symbols right, and the rest is easier to place.

Discussion / revision question: Why does India have a single time zone even though it spans almost 3,000 km east to west? If a question asks for India's highest peak, when would you answer K-2 and when Kangchenjunga?
