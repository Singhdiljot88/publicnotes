India Notes

Sports — Chapter 30

India at the Games and beyond: Olympic firsts, Asian and Commonwealth Games, sport by sport, and the stadiums — updated past the book.

1. The big picture — India at the Games

India's Olympic story began in 1900, when Norman Pritchard won two silver medals in athletics — the book spells his name 'Prichard', but Pritchard is the usual spelling. For decades hockey carried India's medal tally: eight Olympic golds between 1928 and 1980, plus later medals. Individual medals arrived more slowly — K. D. Jadhav's wrestling bronze in 1952 was the first individual Olympic medal for independent India — and then, from the 2000s onward, shooting, boxing, badminton, wrestling, weightlifting and athletics all joined the list.

Up to the Tokyo Games (held in 2021), the book totals India's Olympic medals at 35: 10 gold, 9 silver, 16 bronze. That total is now out of date — see the update box below. Tokyo itself was, at the time, India's best single Games with 7 medals, headlined by Neeraj Chopra's javelin gold (87.58 m), Mirabai Chanu's weightlifting silver, and bronze medals for P. V. Sindhu, Lovlina Borgohain, Bajrang Punia and the men's hockey team, plus Ravi Kumar Dahiya's wrestling silver.

Update since the book (2024–2025) — learn this alongside the book

  • Paris Olympics 2024: India won 6 medals — 1 silver (Neeraj Chopra, javelin) and 5 bronze. India's all-time Olympic total therefore rose from 35 to 41 medals (10 gold, 10 silver, 21 bronze). Tokyo (7 medals) remains India's best single Games.
  • Men's hockey: after the Tokyo bronze, India won bronze again at Paris 2024. The men's hockey Olympic tally is now 8 gold, 1 silver and 4 bronze (1968, 1972, 2020, 2024) — 13 medals in all, not the 12 in the book.
  • Chess: Gukesh Dommaraju, listed in the book for his young grandmaster norms, became World Chess Champion in 2024 — the youngest ever undisputed world champion.
  • Cricket (not in the book's sports chapter in this form): India won the ICC T20 World Cup in 2024 and the ICC Champions Trophy in 2025.
  • P. V. Sindhu remains the only Indian woman with two individual Olympic medals (silver Rio 2016, bronze Tokyo 2020). No confirmed-licence photograph of Sindhu is embedded here — that section is text-only.

3. Asian Games and Commonwealth Games

  • Asian Games: India hosted the first Asian Games, New Delhi 1951, and hosted again in 1982. First Indian golds in 1951 included Levi Pinto (100 m and 200 m) and Sachin Nag (100 m freestyle swimming). Kamaljit Sandhu won India's first women's Asian Games gold (400 m, Bangkok 1970).
  • Commonwealth Games: India first took part in 1934 and hosted in Delhi in 2010 — its most successful edition, with 101 medals including 38 gold. In Birmingham 2022 India won 61 medals (22 gold, 16 silver, 23 bronze). Shooting has historically been India's richest Commonwealth sport, and Achanta Sharath Kamal's table-tennis golds span 2006 to 2022, including a gold at age 40.

4. Sport by sport — the names that keep coming up in quizzes

Badminton

Prakash Padukone: first Indian All England champion (1980), first Indian badminton World Cup winner (1981), first Indian world No. 1, and a record 9 national titles. P. V. Sindhu: 5 World Championship medals (1 gold, 2 silver, 2 bronze). Satwiksairaj Rankireddy and Chirag Shetty: first Indian pair to be world No. 1 and first Indian pair to win Asian Games gold. Saina Nehwal: youngest Indian to win an international title at the time (Asian Satellite, New Delhi 2005, aged 15). Aparna Popat won 10 successive national titles.

Cricket

First Test win: Madras (Chennai), 24 March 1952, vs England. First Test wicket and first five-wicket haul for India: Mohammad Nissar, Lord's 1932. First Test century: Lala Amarnath, 1933–34. First Test hat-trick: Harbhajan Singh. Records that still anchor revision: Sachin Tendulkar — 15,921 Test runs and 51 Test centuries; Anil Kumble — 619 Test wickets; Virat Kohli — captained India in 68 Tests and won 40 as captain, both Indian records in the book. Team records in the book: lowest Test innings total 36 (Adelaide, 2020, vs Australia); highest 759/7 declared (Chennai, 2016, vs England); first tied Test 1986 vs Australia.

Hockey, football, tennis and the rest

Hockey: Major Dhyan Chand is 'the Hockey Wizard'; his autobiography is 'Goal'; National Sports Day (29 August) is his birthday. Vandana Katariya scored a hat-trick at Tokyo 2020. Football: India reached the Olympic semi-final in 1956 (best Olympic showing); Neville D'Souza scored a hat-trick vs Australia that year. India won Asian Games football gold in 1951 and 1962. The national championship is the Santosh Trophy, most often won by West Bengal (32 times in the book). Lawn tennis: first Indian Wimbledon quarter-finalist Ghaus Mohammad (1939); Ramanathan Krishnan reached two Wimbledon semi-finals (1960, 1961) and won 8 national titles; Leander Paes and Mahesh Bhupathi won the 1999 Wimbledon men's doubles — India's first Grand Slam title in the modern era; Leander Paes holds the Davis Cup record for India (126 matches). Sania Mirza reached a career-high singles ranking in the top 30 (2007) and won 6 Grand Slam titles in doubles. Chess: first grandmaster Viswanathan Anand (1987); first woman grandmaster: S. Vijayalakshmi; first international master from India: Manuel Aaron (1961). Boxing: M. C. Mary Kom — 6 world championship titles. Billiards and snooker: Wilson Jones, first Indian world billiards champion (1958, Kolkata); Pankaj Advani, 27-time world champion across billiards and snooker in the book's count. Shooting: Anjali Bhagwat won the 2002 Champions of Champions title; Abhinav Bindra was the first Indian shooter to reach world No. 1. Golf: Royal Calcutta Golf Club (1829) is India's first golf club; the Yak Golf Course in Sikkim (about 13,025 ft) is among the world's highest courses; Jeev Milkha Singh was the first Indian in a US Open (2002). Squash and table tennis: Saurav Ghoshal and Joshna Chinappa dominate the national squash records in the book; Sharath Kamal won the national table-tennis title 10 times, Manika Batra won 4 medals at the 2018 Commonwealth Games. Miscellaneous: kho-kho means 'go and catch' (Marathi), with 9 players a side; Dola Banerjee was the first Indian woman to win gold at an international archery event (2005).

Key takeaways

  • Olympics: first medallist Pritchard (1900); first independent-India individual medal Jadhav (1952); first individual gold Bindra (2008); first athletics gold Neeraj Chopra (Tokyo 2020) — and the book total of 35 medals (to Tokyo) is now 41 after Paris 2024, so always date an Olympic total.
  • Hockey: 8 golds (1928–1980) plus 1 silver and 4 bronze = 13 medals; Dhyan Chand = 'Hockey Wizard', Sports Day 29 August.
  • Asian Games hosted by India: 1951 and 1982, both in Delhi; Commonwealth Games hosted: Delhi 2010 (101 medals); stadiums: Eden Gardens (Kolkata, 1864) is the oldest, Narendra Modi Stadium (Ahmedabad) is the largest by capacity.

Why it matters: Sport is the easiest way into Indian history — one medal table carries independence, Partition, gender, and commercialisation with it.

Discussion / revision question: Why did India's Olympic medal count stay so low for so long, and what changed after 2000? Use hockey, shooting and badminton as examples. A quiz asks: 'Who was India's first Olympic medallist?' Why is the answer Pritchard (1900), not Jadhav (1952)?
