India Today — 2000 to 2026

Digital growth, new welfare rights, major reforms, and the politics of the present.

The timeline

Date / periodEvent / person / milestone — why it matters
2000sIT and services growth — Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune; UPI later transforms payments (launched 2016).
India becomes a global services and digital-payments leader, changing jobs and everyday transactions.
2005MGNREGA (Mahatma Gandhi National Rural Employment Guarantee Act) — right to 100 days of work.
A rights-based welfare model: work as a legal entitlement, not charity.
2008Mumbai attacks (26 November 2008).
A turning point for counter-terrorism, coastal security and India–Pakistan relations.
2011India wins the Cricket World Cup at home; Anna Hazare anti-corruption movement.
Sport and protest both become national television moments.
2014Narendra Modi becomes Prime Minister; BJP wins a single-party Lok Sabha majority.
A shift from coalition politics to single-party majorities at the Centre.
2016–2017Demonetisation (8 November 2016); GST follows (1 July 2017).
Two sweeping economic reforms in eight months — debated on growth, informality and federal tax-sharing.
2019Article 370 changes in Jammu and Kashmir; Citizenship (Amendment) Act passed.
Constitutional status and citizenship become the sharpest political divides of the decade.
2020–2021COVID-19 pandemic; farm laws passed (2020) and repealed (2021) after farmer protests.
Public health, migrant labour and farm politics collide in one crisis period.
2023Chandrayaan-3 lands near the Moon's south pole (23 August 2023); G20 presidency and summit in Delhi.
Space and diplomacy milestones — science achievement presented as national capability.
2024Lok Sabha election — BJP-led NDA returns, without a single-party BJP majority.
Coalition politics returns to Delhi after a decade of majorities.
2025–2026Continuing debates: voter-roll revision (SIR), data protection rules, trade deals with the UK, EU and US, and state elections.
The present is still being written — today's entries should be checked against current news before quoting them.

Key takeaways

  • The 2000s are not just 'recent' — they include wars, pandemics and constitutional changes.
  • Technology, welfare and identity politics now interact in every election.
  • Any 'today' entry ages fast — re-check it before reusing it.

Why it matters: It connects the long past to the headlines a reader sees now.

Discussion / revision question: Which 2000s change — digital payments, MGNREGA, or GST — most changed daily life for an ordinary family?
