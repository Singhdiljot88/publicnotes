Glossary

45 terms that come up again and again in Indian polity, each in one or two plain sentences, with links to the notes that explain them properly.

A B C D E F G J L M N O P Q R S T U Z

A

Article 19
Guarantees freedoms including speech and expression, peaceful assembly and association, subject to reasonable restrictions on listed grounds. Read: Note 036, Note 051, Note 061
Article 21
Protects life and personal liberty. Courts have read privacy, a clean environment and the right to water into it. Read: Note 037, Note 066
Article 324
Vests superintendence, direction and control of elections in the Election Commission. Read: Note 007, Note 011, Note 013
Article 324(5)
Protects the Chief Election Commissioner from removal except in the manner of a Supreme Court judge: proved misbehaviour or incapacity and a special majority in both Houses. Read: Note 018, Note 017
Article 356
Allows President's Rule in a state when its government cannot be carried on in line with the Constitution, often after a Governor's report. Read: Note 006
Article 368
Sets out how Parliament amends the Constitution, by special majority and, for some federal changes, state ratification. Read: Note 004
Article 74
Says the President acts on the advice of the Council of Ministers headed by the Prime Minister; advice is binding after one request to reconsider. Read: Note 005, Note 090

B

Basic structure doctrine
The rule from Kesavananda Bharati (1973) that Parliament cannot use its amending power to destroy the Constitution's essential features. Read: Note 004, Note 033, Note 002
BRICS
A grouping that began with Brazil, Russia, India and China, added South Africa in 2010 and expanded from 2024; India chairs it in 2026. Read: Note 085

C

CBDR-RC
Common But Differentiated Responsibilities and Respective Capabilities: the climate principle that all countries share responsibility, but richer, high-emitting countries should do more. Read: Note 089
Chief of Defence Staff
The post created in 2019 to head the Department of Military Affairs and advise the Defence Minister. It does not command the Service Chiefs. Read: Note 090
Coalition government
A government formed by two or more parties when no single party has a majority of 272 Lok Sabha seats. Read: Note 042
Collegium
The judge-led system for appointing and transferring higher court judges, built up through Supreme Court judgments. Read: Note 032, Note 031
Comptroller and Auditor General (CAG)
The constitutional auditor of public accounts, which reports to Parliament and state legislatures. Read: Note 008

D

Delimitation
Redrawing constituency boundaries so each representative serves a roughly equal population; seat allocation between states has been frozen on 1971 figures. Read: Note 028
Directive Principles
Part IV goals that guide the state towards welfare and social justice. Courts cannot enforce them directly. Read: Note 003, Note 071

E

ECINET
The Election Commission's single digital portal that merged over 40 earlier apps for registration, roll search, e-EPIC and grievances. Read: Note 016
Electoral bonds
Instruments that allowed anonymous political donations from 2018 until the Supreme Court struck the scheme down in 2024. Read: Note 046

F

FCRA
The Foreign Contribution (Regulation) Act, which governs how organisations accept and use foreign donations. Read: Note 068
Finance Commission
A body set up every five years under Article 280 to recommend how tax revenue is shared between the Centre and states. Read: Note 026, Note 077
Form 6, 7 and 8
Voter registration forms: Form 6 to enrol, Form 7 to object to or delete an entry, and Form 8 to correct or shift an entry. Read: Note 012, Note 015, Note 019, Note 096
Fundamental Rights
The rights in Part III of the Constitution that people can enforce in court. Read: Note 001, Note 003

G

Gram Sabha
The assembly of all adult voters in a panchayat area, under Article 243(b). Read: Note 099, Note 067, Note 009
GST Council
The body under Article 279A, chaired by the Union Finance Minister with state finance ministers, that recommends GST rates and rules. Read: Note 026, Note 077

J

Judicial review
The power of courts to test laws and government action against the Constitution. Read: Note 033

L

Leader of the Opposition
A statutory post (1977) for the leader of the largest opposition party, conventionally recognised when it holds 10% of seats. Read: Note 024
Lok Adalat
A people's court that settles disputes by agreement, part of the legal aid system led by NALSA. Read: Note 039

M

MGNREGA
The 2005 rural jobs law that let households demand up to 100 days of paid work a year; replaced from July 2026 by VB–G RAM G. Read: Note 072
Minimum Support Price (MSP)
A price floor announced by the government for major crops, on the advice of the Commission for Agricultural Costs and Prices. Read: Note 079, Note 062
Model Code of Conduct
Election Commission guidelines for parties and governments during election periods. Read: Note 007, Note 030
Money Bill
A bill dealing only with taxes or spending; the Lok Sabha has the final say on it. Read: Note 021, Note 023

N

NFSA
The National Food Security Act, 2013, which makes subsidised grain a legal entitlement for up to 75% of rural and 50% of urban people. Read: Note 073

O

OCI
Overseas Citizenship of India: lifelong visa-free travel and most economic rights for people of Indian origin, but no vote or constitutional office. Read: Note 087
One Nation, One Election
The proposal to hold Lok Sabha and state assembly elections together, as India did until 1967. Read: Note 030, Note 050

P

PESA
The Panchayats (Extension to Scheduled Areas) Act, 1996, which gives gram sabhas in Scheduled Areas a role in decisions affecting them. Read: Note 067
Public Interest Litigation (PIL)
A case filed in the public interest, often on behalf of people who cannot reach court themselves. Read: Note 034
Purna Swaraj
Complete independence: the goal adopted by the Congress at Lahore in December 1929, marked by a public pledge on 26 January 1930. Read: Lahore, 1929

Q

Quad
The Quadrilateral Security Dialogue of Australia, India, Japan and the United States, focused on the Indo-Pacific. Read: Note 085
Question Hour
The first hour of a parliamentary sitting, when members question ministers. Read: Note 022

R

Right to Information (RTI)
The 2005 law that lets citizens request information from public authorities. Read: Note 070, Note 099

S

SIR (Special Intensive Revision)
A door-to-door re-verification of electoral rolls ending in a draft roll, claims and objections, and a final roll. Read: Note 013, Note 014, Note 015
Strategic autonomy
India's aim to decide foreign policy by its own interests without joining a binding alliance; now often called multi-alignment. Read: Note 081, Note 085

T

TRP
Television Rating Points: estimates of viewing from a sample of metered homes that drive TV advertising. Read: Note 052

U

Undertrial
A person held in custody who has been accused but not convicted. Read: Note 035

Z

Zero Hour
The time after Question Hour when members raise urgent matters without prior notice. Read: Note 022
